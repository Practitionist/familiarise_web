# ADR 34 — Booking mode, the pay-link window and per-consultant request caps

## Context

Until #1703 the request-for-approval path had no switch of its own. A consultee reached it only when the slot they picked was already contended (the grid marked it `isAllocated`), and the documentation described a per-consultant "requires approval" preference that the code never had. The pay-link a consultant's approval minted stayed open for 48 hours with no reminder, and nothing bounded how many open requests a single consultant could be holding: the only caps were per consultee (three open requests, ten submissions an hour).

The 2026-09-18 requests train asked three product questions, and the owner locked the answers on 2026-09-18 after a research pass over what comparable marketplaces do. This ADR records decisions D1, D2, D3, D4 and D6 of that train and the reasoning behind them. D1, D2 and D4 are schema decisions, shipped in #1724; D3 and D6 need no schema and shipped in #1702, #1720 and #1721, but the _why_ belongs here rather than scattered across five PR bodies. D5 (the PR-train sequencing) is a delivery decision, not a design one, and stays in the plan file rather than an ADR.

## Decisions

1. **D1 — a real per-consultant booking mode.** `ConsultantProfile.bookingMode` is `INSTANT` or `REQUEST`, default `INSTANT`. `INSTANT` is exactly the behaviour the platform had: a free slot goes straight to checkout, a contended slot becomes a request. `REQUEST` sends every slot through `POST /api/scheduling/request-for-approval`, and the expert page says so next to the button ("the expert confirms before you pay") and next to the price ("Requests reviewed first"). Subscriptions ignore the mode; they are always paid first and scheduled later. The column lives on the profile, not on a plan, because it describes how the consultant works and because the expert page decides its CTA from the one profile payload it already loads.
2. **D2 — pay after approval stays; the pay-link window is 24 hours with one reminder at 12.** No authorisation hold is taken at request time. In India a hold is a slow refund in disguise: UPI debits at authorisation, Razorpay's capture ceiling is 3 days, and the refund of an uncaptured amount is a 5–7 working-day wait the consultee did not sign up for. So money moves only after the consultant says yes, and the buyer's exposure is a link, not a debit. The window shrinks from 48 to 24 hours because italki's 48 h and Topmate's 72 h are provider-response windows, not payment windows; a buyer who asked for a time and got a yes should not need two days to pay for it, and a consultant should not have their calendar held that long by a link nobody clicks. One reminder goes out with 12 hours left; it is the existing pay-link email with a different heading, staged as a `FailedEmail` row whose type and entity reference are the once-guard (ADR 27), and it is never sent after a capture or after the link has lapsed. The 48 h PENDING expiry and the 24 h pay-link expiry both tell the consultee, through the existing appointment-cancelled bell and email with the platform as the actor.
3. **D4 — a pause toggle and an optional cap, not a platform default.** `acceptingRequests` is the snooze: while it is false the request route answers `409 CONSULTANT_PAUSED` before it takes any lock, the Requests tab shows a banner, and the expert page names the recourse. `maxOpenRequests` is nullable and null by default, because the research found no marketplace that documents a numeric queue cap; it is a tool a flooded consultant opts into, refused as `409 CONSULTANT_AT_CAPACITY` from a count of open PENDING consultations and subscriptions taken inside the consultant-keyed slot lock. The per-consultee caps are unchanged and sit in front of it. A response-rate metric (answers within 24 h over the last 30 days, read from `BookingStatusHistory`) is shown to the consultant on their own Home; it drives no ranking yet.
4. **The 30-day unallocated-subscription refund stays, and the consultant is nudged before it fires.** The research found no comparable platform that puts a scheduling deadline on the provider, which makes the refund a deliberate trust differentiator rather than a gap to close. What was missing was the consultant hearing about it in time: the stale-request sweep now nudges them at day 3, 7 and 14 after payment while a paid subscription still has no session times, through the new-booking-request bell family (a payload discriminator, not a new workflow, because the plan caps an environment at 20) and its email twin, each stage keyed once through the notification outbox.
5. **D3 — a consultant-initiated decline or cancel stays a 100 % refund; there is no consultant-discretion partial.** This matches the industry row above: Airbnb, Eventbrite, Clarity and Topmate all make the buyer whole when the provider is the one who backs out, and none of them let the provider choose a smaller number. Giving a consultant a partial-refund dial on their own cancellation would let the person who broke the booking also set its price, which is the wrong party to trust with that number; the buyer-side ladder (`CancellationPolicyTier`, ADR 28) already carries the graduated cases, and the privileged `POST /api/admin/refunds` route already exists for the genuine exception. Adding a second, consultant-facing partial path would duplicate that machinery for a case the research gives no precedent for.
6. **D6 — the heat map and the confirm dialog get read-worthy states, not new hues.** The palette is locked, as `lib/scheduling/interval-status-tokens.ts` itself records, so `outsidePeriod` and `past` are hatched (two angles, `outsidePeriod` at 45°, `past` at 135°, so "outside the booking's period" and "already gone" cannot be mistaken for each other) rather than given a fifth colour, and `rescheduling` gets a dashed border so it stops reading as the solid-amber `partiallyBooked` state. The confirm dialog that opens off the grid follows the same "explain, don't decorate" rule: a sentence summary and a per-slot status list instead of emoji, viewer-zone dates throughout, an in-dialog success state with a direct link to the appointment, and — on a conflict — a "Pick another time" deep link that carries the requested slot forward rather than dead-ending the flow. Keyboard operation of the grid (a roving-tabindex ARIA grid with arrow/Home/End/PageUp/PageDown) was deliberately not built alongside this: it is the largest single item in the grid-UX work and blocks nothing else, so it is filed as #1715 and left for its own PR.

## Consequences

The schema change is three additive columns and one enum, shipped in the same PR as the code that reads them per the freeze rule, with defaults that cover every existing row and therefore no backfill. The request route gains two typed refusals a client can branch on. The pay-link window is now one constant (`lib/payments/constants.ts`) that the mint, the emails, the consultee and admin pending-payment views and the docs all read, so the number can never drift between surfaces again. The abandoned-payments run and the stale-request sweep each gain one pass; both follow the state-as-outbox pattern of ADR 27 and add no table. The Novu template for `new-booking-request` gains a branch and needs one `npm run novu:sync` to render the nudge sentence; until then the nudge renders as the plain request sentence, which is readable but wrong.

What this ADR does not do: it does not add a consultant-discretion partial refund, it does not take a hold at request time, it does not use the response rate for ranking or visibility, and it does not build keyboard operation of the grid. Each of those would be its own decision, and the last one already has a number.
