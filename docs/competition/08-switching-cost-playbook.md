# Switching Cost Playbook — Making It Impossible to Leave

The best retention strategy isn't a feature — it's making leaving painful. Not through lock-in tricks, but by accumulating so much value on-platform that switching means starting over. Every week a consultant stays on Familiarise, they deposit more reputation, more data, more revenue infrastructure, and more relationships into the platform. Our job is to make those deposits compound.

This document maps every switching cost lever we have, ranks them by impact, and lays out a priority order for engineering investment.

---

## Category 1: Reputation Capital (Highest Switching Cost)

Reputation is the single most powerful retention mechanism in any marketplace. It is non-portable by design.

### Reviews and Ratings
- **Why it matters:** 50 five-star reviews on Familiarise = starting from zero on Topmate. This is the single biggest switching cost in any marketplace. Amazon sellers don't leave Amazon because of reviews. Airbnb hosts don't leave Airbnb because of reviews. The pattern is universal.
- **Mechanics:** Each completed session prompts a review. Reviews are attached to the consultant's Familiarise profile, visible to all prospective consultees. They cannot be exported, screenshotted for credibility on another platform, or transferred.
- **Compounding effect:** More reviews → higher search ranking on-platform → more bookings → more reviews. The flywheel accelerates retention over time.
- **Competitive note:** Topmate has reviews but their volume per consultant is low because sessions happen on Zoom with no natural post-session prompt. Our integrated video means we control the review trigger.

### Verified Badges
- **"Verified Expert"** — Identity and credential verification completed
- **"Top Rated"** — Sustained high ratings over 20+ sessions
- **"Founding Member"** — Early adopter badge (time-locked, impossible to earn after launch window)
- **Why it matters:** These trust signals take months to earn. They visually differentiate established consultants from newcomers. Leaving means losing visible credibility markers that directly affect conversion rates.

### Client Testimonials
- Attached to profile, visible to new seekers browsing the consultant's page
- Richer than star ratings — specific quotes about outcomes and experience
- Accumulated over time, each one adding social proof that compounds

### Profile SEO Ranking
- Over time, Google starts ranking a consultant's Familiarise profile for searches of their name and expertise area
- A consultant with 6+ months on-platform may find their Familiarise profile appearing on page 1 of Google for "[Name] [expertise] consultant"
- Leaving the platform means losing organic search traffic that was driving free discovery
- This is a silent switching cost — most consultants won't realize it until they leave and their inbound drops

## Category 2: Data Accumulation (Medium-High Switching Cost)

Data is the quiet lock-in. It doesn't feel like lock-in until you try to leave and realize you're flying blind.

### Earnings Analytics
- Monthly and quarterly revenue trends
- Average session value by service type
- Client demographics and geographic breakdown
- Peak booking hours and seasonal patterns
- Conversion rates: profile views → bookings, trials → paid sessions
- **Impact:** 6 months of analytics data = irreplaceable business intelligence. A consultant optimizing their pricing based on 6 months of demand data cannot replicate that insight on day 1 of a new platform.

### Session Recordings Library
- All past session recordings organized, searchable, and accessible through the dashboard
- Consultants reference past sessions for follow-ups, consultees revisit for notes
- **Impact:** Leave = lose the entire archive. Stream.io recordings are platform-bound.

### Client Conversation History
- Years of chat messages with clients: context, shared documents, follow-up notes, informal agreements
- Non-exportable from Stream.io chat infrastructure
- **Impact:** Starting fresh on a new platform means losing all conversational context with existing clients. "What did we discuss last time?" becomes unanswerable.

### Calendar and Scheduling Patterns
- The platform learns availability patterns, auto-suggests optimal slot configurations
- Recurring schedules, blackout dates, timezone preferences — all configured over time
- **Impact:** New platform = reconfigure everything from scratch. Hours of setup work that took months to fine-tune.

## Category 3: Revenue Lock-In (High Switching Cost)

Money is the most tangible switching cost. When leaving means losing revenue, people don't leave.

### Active Subscriptions
- If 20 clients have active monthly subscriptions, switching platforms means asking all 20 to cancel and resubscribe on a new platform
- Subscriber fatigue is real — most won't bother. Expect 40-60% subscriber loss on any platform migration.
- **Impact:** A consultant earning ₹50K/month from subscriptions risks losing ₹20-30K/month in the migration. That math keeps people on-platform.

### Referral Credits
- Earned credits from referring other consultants or consultees
- Only spendable on-platform (reduced commission rates, premium features, promotional boosts)
- **Impact:** Leaving = leaving money on the table. Even ₹2,000 in unused credits creates psychological friction.

### Pending Payouts
- Any pending earnings in the payout cycle create friction against switching mid-cycle
- Combined with a standard payout schedule (weekly/biweekly), there is always some amount "in transit"
- **Impact:** Not a long-term lock-in, but creates micro-friction at any given moment

### Multi-Week Class Enrollments
- Classes span multiple weeks with enrolled students expecting continuity
- Cannot switch platforms mid-class without disrupting enrolled consultees
- **Impact:** Creates a rolling window where switching is practically impossible — and if a consultant always has an active class, that window never opens

## Category 4: Operational Investment (Medium Switching Cost)

Every hour spent configuring the platform is an hour they'd have to spend again elsewhere.

### Configured Services
- 4 service types (consultations, subscriptions, webinars, classes) each with:
  - Custom pricing tiers
  - Detailed descriptions and cover images
  - Slot configurations and scheduling rules
  - Trial session settings
  - Approval workflows
- **Impact:** A consultant with 8-10 configured services has invested 3-5 hours in setup. Recreating this on a new platform is tedious enough to prevent casual switching.

### Team Collaborators
- Revenue split configurations with co-hosts
- Coordinator permissions for assistants
- Shared event management for webinars and classes
- **Impact:** All collaborative relationships need to be rebuilt. If a consultant co-hosts with 3 other experts, all 4 need to switch simultaneously — coordination cost makes this unlikely.

### Discount Codes and Promotions
- Active discount campaigns with tracking and analytics
- Shareable links with embedded promo codes distributed across social media
- **Impact:** Switching means all distributed promo links break. Social media posts, email signatures, and shared links all point to the old platform.

### Waitlist Subscribers
- People signed up for notifications about upcoming webinars, classes, or availability
- Not portable — these are platform-registered users, not an email list the consultant owns
- **Impact:** Leaving means losing a warm audience that was ready to buy

---

## Category 5: Network & Social (Growing Over Time)

Network effects are the slowest to build but the hardest to replicate.

### Follower/Subscriber Base
- Consultees who follow specific consultants receive notifications about new services, schedule changes, and promotions
- A consultant with 500 followers has a built-in distribution channel for every new offering
- **Impact:** This audience is platform-specific. Leaving means losing the notification channel entirely.

### Collaborator Network
- Revenue-sharing relationships with other experts on the platform
- Co-hosted events, cross-referrals, shared audiences
- **Impact:** These relationships are formalized on-platform. Moving means either convincing collaborators to move too or losing those revenue streams.

### Referral Network
- Active referral codes and the users they've brought in
- Ongoing referral commissions from previously referred consultants' earnings
- **Impact:** Referral income is passive and platform-bound. Leaving means abandoning a revenue stream that requires zero ongoing effort.

## The Switching Cost Timeline

| Time on Platform | Switching Cost | What They Lose |
|-----------------|----------------|----------------|
| 1 week | Very low | Nothing meaningful — basic profile setup only |
| 1 month | Low | Some scheduling config, a few reviews, initial chat history |
| 3 months | Medium | Analytics data, 10-20 reviews, chat history, recording library starts |
| 6 months | High | 30+ reviews, earnings trends, recording library, active subscriptions, SEO traction |
| 1 year | Very high | 50+ reviews, full analytics history, established SEO ranking, active subscriptions, referral credits, collaborator network |
| 2+ years | Near impossible | Complete business infrastructure — hundreds of reviews, years of analytics, deep client relationships, passive referral income, Google ranking their profile |

**The critical window is 0-3 months.** If a consultant survives the first 3 months, switching costs compound rapidly. Our onboarding and early engagement strategy must focus on accelerating value accumulation in this window — push them to get their first 10 reviews, their first subscription client, their first collaborator, as fast as possible.

## Strategic Notes

**Do not make switching costs feel hostile.** The goal is accumulated value, not punitive lock-in. Every switching cost should be something the consultant actively wants — better analytics, more reviews, richer data. The lock-in is a side effect of genuine value, not a trap.

**Portable data is fine for low-value items.** Let consultants export basic profile info, service descriptions, even earnings reports. These are easy to recreate anyway. The non-portable items (reviews, chat history, recordings, follower base, SEO ranking) are the real anchors, and they're non-portable by nature, not by policy.

**The compounding effect is the strategy.** No single switching cost is insurmountable at any given moment. But 50 reviews + 6 months of analytics + 15 active subscribers + 3 collaborators + Google ranking + ₹5,000 in referral credits + 200 followers = a consultant who will never leave. The combination is the lock-in.
