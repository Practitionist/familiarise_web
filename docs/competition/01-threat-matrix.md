# Threat Matrix — Every Competitor Ranked

**Last updated:** March 2026
**Author:** Familiarise Strategy Team
**Classification:** Internal — Do Not Distribute

---

## How to Read This Document

Every competitor is assigned a threat tier based on three factors:

1. **Market overlap** — Are they targeting the same users (Indian experts + seekers)?
2. **Momentum** — Are they growing, stagnating, or declining?
3. **Switching cost** — How hard is it for a creator on their platform to move to us (or vice versa)?

Tiers: **CRITICAL** (existential if ignored), **HIGH** (direct competitor, active threat), **MEDIUM** (indirect or emerging threat), **LOW** (watching, not acting yet).

## CRITICAL THREAT

### 1. Topmate (topmate.io)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Creators | ~300,000 |
| Users | ~1,000,000+ |
| Funding | $1.13M (seed) |
| Revenue | ~$68K/yr (estimated) |
| Commission | 10% (effective 16-18% in India) |
| Founded | 2021 (India) |
| Mobile Apps | Yes (iOS + Android) |
| Trust Score | 51.2/100 (Scamadviser) |

**Overview:**
Topmate is the default platform for Indian creators offering 1-on-1 consultations. They pioneered the "link-in-bio for paid calls" model in India and have significant brand recognition in the tech/startup creator community. They are our single most direct competitor.

**Business Model:**
10% commission on all transactions. Payment processing via Stripe (no Indian gateway). Creators set up a profile page, share a link, and get booked. Calls happen over Zoom (external links, not integrated). Revenue is heavily concentrated in a small percentage of top creators — the median creator earns virtually nothing due to extreme power-law distribution.

**Why They're a Threat:**
- First-mover advantage in India. "Topmate link" is becoming a generic term like "Calendly link."
- 300K creators is a massive supply-side network. Even if most are inactive, the brand gravity pulls new creators to them by default.
- Mobile apps (we don't have one).
- WhatsApp integration for notifications (we don't have this).
- AI-powered matching (we don't have this).
- They're the incumbent. We have to actively convince creators to switch.

**Their Achilles Heel:**
This is where it gets interesting. Topmate has serious, structural problems:

- **Trust crisis.** 51.2/100 trust score on Scamadviser. Reddit and Twitter are full of complaints about:
  - Account closures with funds seized (no appeal process)
  - Payout delays and failures
  - Unresponsive support
  - Creators locked out of their own earnings
- **No RBI PA license confirmed.** Operating payment aggregation in India without confirmed compliance is a regulatory time bomb. If RBI cracks down, their Indian operations are at risk.
- **Stripe-only payments.** Indian creators lose 3% Stripe fee + 3-5% forex conversion on every transaction. Effective take rate is 16-18%, not the advertised 10%. This is a massive hidden cost that most creators don't realize until they see their payouts.
- **Zoom dependency.** Sessions happen on Zoom. No integrated video, no session recordings owned by the platform, no chat continuity. The experience is fragmented.
- **Power-law economics.** A tiny fraction of creators earn real money. The rest get a few bookings and churn. Topmate doesn't solve this — they just provide a link.
- **No multi-service support.** No webinars, no classes, no subscriptions, no document review. Creators who want to offer more than 1-on-1 calls need additional tools.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
1. **Lead with the money argument.** "You're paying 16-18% on Topmate. Pay 10% on Familiarise. That's ₹600-800 saved per ₹10,000 in bookings." Make this the headline of every migration campaign.
2. **Topmate Migration Program.** Show your Topmate dashboard, get 5% commission for 3 months. Make switching frictionless.
3. **Trust positioning.** Every time a Topmate payout complaint surfaces on social media, we should be in the replies (tastefully) offering a reliable alternative. Build a reputation for "the platform that actually pays you."
4. **Integrated experience.** Stream.io video + chat is objectively better than a Zoom link. Demo this relentlessly.
5. **Multi-service upsell.** Once a creator is on Familiarise for consultations, show them webinars and classes. Now they're consolidated and switching cost is high.

### 2. SuperProfile (superprofile.bio)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Creators | ~38,000 |
| Users | ~4,000,000 |
| Funding | Undisclosed (backed by notable angels) |
| Revenue | Not publicly disclosed |
| Pricing | ₹99/month flat subscription (no commission) |
| Founded | 2022 (India) |
| Mobile Apps | No |

**Overview:**
SuperProfile is the newest serious threat and the one growing fastest. They've taken a radically different approach: flat ₹99/month subscription instead of commission. This is a direct attack on the commission model that Topmate (and we) use. They position as a "link-in-bio store" for creators — consultations, digital products, courses, community, all in one link.

**Business Model:**
₹99/month subscription. Zero commission. Creators keep 100% of their earnings minus payment gateway fees. They support UPI, which means Indian transactions are nearly free for creators. Revenue comes from volume of subscriptions, not transaction volume.

**Why They're a Threat:**
- **The pricing is devastating.** ₹99/month with zero commission is impossible to compete with on price alone. A creator doing ₹50K/month in bookings pays ₹99 on SuperProfile vs ₹5,000 on Familiarise (at 10%). The math is brutal.
- **4M users already.** They're growing fast, possibly faster than Topmate in India.
- **UPI support.** They've solved the payment problem that Topmate hasn't.
- **Digital products.** They support selling PDFs, templates, courses — not just calls. This broadens their creator base beyond consultants.
- **India-first design.** Built for the Indian market from day one.

**Their Achilles Heel:**
- **₹99/month is not sustainable at scale.** At 38K creators, that's ₹37.6L/month (~$41K). That barely covers a small team's salaries. They'll either need to raise prices, add commission, or raise significant funding. The current model is a growth hack, not a business model.
- **Feature depth is shallow.** They're wide (many product types) but not deep. No integrated video calls. No scheduling sophistication. No session recordings. No collaboration features. It's a storefront, not a platform.
- **No marketplace discovery.** SuperProfile is purely a link-in-bio tool. There's no marketplace where seekers browse and find experts. Creators must bring their own traffic. We can offer both.
- **Support and reliability are unproven.** At scale, support costs money. At ₹99/month, can they afford to provide real support? Unlikely.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
1. **Don't compete on price with SuperProfile.** We will lose that fight. Compete on value: integrated video, scheduling, marketplace discovery, analytics, reviews, collaboration.
2. **Position as "professional grade."** SuperProfile is for hobbyists selling ₹99 PDFs. Familiarise is for serious consultants running a practice. Different market segments.
3. **Emphasize marketplace.** SuperProfile creators must drive their own traffic. Familiarise will (eventually) have a marketplace where seekers discover experts. This is a fundamentally different value proposition.
4. **Watch their pricing evolution.** When SuperProfile inevitably raises prices or adds commission, be ready to capture creators who feel bait-and-switched.

## HIGH THREAT

### 3. GrowthSchool (growthschool.io)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Creators/Instructors | ~100+ curated |
| Users | ~1,000,000+ (claimed) |
| Funding | $5M (Sequoia India lead) |
| Revenue | ~$8.11M/yr (estimated) |
| Model | Cohort-based courses, platform-controlled pricing |
| Founded | 2021 (India) |

**Overview:**
GrowthSchool is a cohort-based learning platform backed by Sequoia. They bring in high-profile instructors (ex-Google, ex-Meta, startup founders) to run live courses. They've generated significant revenue but have equally significant reputation problems.

**Business Model:**
Platform-controlled. GrowthSchool sets prices, handles marketing, and pays instructors a fee. Instructors don't set their own rates. The platform keeps the lion's share. Typical course prices range from ₹5,000 to ₹15,000.

**Why They're a Threat:**
- **$5M Sequoia funding.** They have resources we don't.
- **$8M+ revenue.** They've proven Indians will pay for premium online education.
- **Brand recognition.** Heavy social media marketing has made them well-known in the startup/tech community.
- **Overlapping audience.** Their users are exactly our target demographic: ambitious Indian professionals willing to pay for expert access.
- **If they pivot to a marketplace model,** they could become a direct competitor overnight.

**Their Achilles Heel:**
- **Severe reputation damage.** Reddit threads, Twitter complaints, and social media posts accuse GrowthSchool of:
  - Bait-and-switch pricing (advertised ₹499, actual ₹5,000+)
  - Aggressive, borderline predatory sales calls
  - Fake urgency ("only 3 seats left" when there are 300)
  - Refund resistance
  - Course quality inconsistency
  - "Scam" label actively circulating
- **Instructor lock-in.** Instructors have no autonomy. They can't set prices, can't own the relationship with students, can't build their own brand. Good instructors will eventually want more control.
- **Not a marketplace.** GrowthSchool is a curated platform. They select instructors. We're building an open marketplace. Different model.
- **Cohort-only.** No 1-on-1 consultations, no ongoing subscriptions, no on-demand access.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
1. **Position against their model.** "On Familiarise, you own your brand, set your prices, and keep your clients. On GrowthSchool, you're an employee."
2. **Target their disillusioned instructors.** Instructors who've worked with GrowthSchool and felt underpaid or undervalued are prime migration targets.
3. **Target their disillusioned students.** People who've been burned by GrowthSchool's sales tactics are looking for trustworthy alternatives. Be that alternative.
4. **Don't copy their model.** We are not a cohort platform. We are a marketplace. Stay in our lane but absorb their displaced users.

### 4. Preplaced (preplaced.in)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mentors | ~600 |
| Users | Not publicly disclosed |
| Funding | Not publicly disclosed |
| Revenue | Not publicly disclosed |
| Commission | 20% |
| Founded | India |
| Niche | Tech career mentorship |

**Overview:**
Preplaced is a focused tech career mentorship platform. Mentors help with interview prep, resume reviews, career transitions, and job placement. They're small but deeply embedded in the Indian tech job-seeking community.

**Business Model:**
20% commission (double ours). Mentors are curated — not anyone can join. They offer free trial sessions as a conversion mechanism. Some mentors offer job placement guarantees.

**Why They're a Threat:**
- **Direct competitor in tech mentorship.** Our initial niche (tech/engineering first) overlaps almost perfectly with Preplaced.
- **Free trials.** This is a powerful conversion tool. Seekers try before they buy. We have trial sessions too, but they've been doing it longer.
- **Job placement angle.** "Get mentored AND get placed" is a stronger value proposition than "get mentored" alone.
- **Curated quality.** Because they vet mentors, the average quality is higher than an open marketplace.

**Their Achilles Heel:**
- **20% commission is steep.** Mentors lose a fifth of their earnings. At our 10%, we're immediately more attractive to supply-side.
- **Tiny scale.** 600 mentors is nothing. We can surpass this quickly.
- **Narrow niche.** Tech career mentorship only. No business consulting, no creative coaching, no webinars, no classes. We're broader.
- **No integrated video.** They use external tools for calls.
- **No self-serve.** You can't just sign up and start mentoring. The curation process is a bottleneck to growth.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
1. **Undercut on commission.** 10% vs 20%. Simple. Every Preplaced mentor should know this.
2. **Offer the same niche, plus more.** Start with tech mentorship (same as Preplaced) but offer webinars, classes, subscriptions — things Preplaced can't.
3. **Free trials.** We already have this. Make sure it's prominently featured.
4. **Open marketplace.** Let anyone with verified expertise join. Don't bottleneck growth with curation. Use reviews and ratings for quality signals instead.

## MEDIUM THREAT

### 5. Metvy (metvy.com)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Users | Not publicly disclosed |
| Funding | $188K (₹1.5Cr approx.) |
| Revenue | ₹1.22Cr/yr (~$134K) |
| Model | Networking-first, subscription-based |
| Founded | India |
| Languages | 8 (multilingual) |
| Notable | DPIIT recognized, government-backed |

**Overview:**
Metvy is a networking and mentorship platform with a unique angle: government backing (DPIIT recognition) and hyperlocal, multilingual focus. They support 8 Indian languages, which gives them access to non-English-speaking markets that every other competitor ignores.

**Business Model:**
Subscription-based with networking features. Users pay for premium access to mentors and networking tools. Not purely a consultation marketplace — more of a professional networking platform with mentorship bolted on.

**Why They're a Threat:**
- **Multilingual support (8 languages).** India has 22 official languages. The non-English market is enormous and completely unserved by Topmate, SuperProfile, or us.
- **Government backing.** DPIIT recognition opens doors to government contracts, startup programs, and institutional credibility.
- **Hyperlocal focus.** They understand tier-2 and tier-3 India in a way Silicon Valley-inspired platforms don't.
- **Revenue generating.** ₹1.22Cr is real revenue. They're not just burning investor money.

**Their Achilles Heel:**
- **Small funding.** $188K is barely enough to run a team for a year. They're resource-constrained.
- **Networking ≠ Consultation.** Their core product (networking) is different from ours (consultation marketplace). The overlap is limited.
- **No integrated video.** No session infrastructure.
- **Feature sprawl.** Trying to be a networking platform AND a mentorship platform AND multilingual AND hyperlocal dilutes focus.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
1. **Monitor, don't react.** Metvy is not a direct competitor today. They're in the networking space, we're in the consultation space.
2. **Note the multilingual opportunity.** When we're ready to expand beyond English, Metvy's approach is a useful reference. Multi-language support should be on our long-term roadmap.
3. **If they pivot to consultations,** revisit threat level.

### 6. ProPeers (propeers.in)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Users | Early stage |
| Funding | Unfunded |
| Revenue | Pre-revenue (estimated) |
| Model | Peer-to-peer student community |
| Founded | India |
| Focus | Mobile-first, student community |

**Overview:**
ProPeers is an early-stage, unfunded platform focused on peer-to-peer mentorship among students. Think of it as "students helping students" — college seniors mentoring juniors on placements, exam prep, and career decisions.

**Business Model:**
Still evolving. Community-first approach with monetization TBD. Mobile-first design targeting the student demographic.

**Why They're a Threat:**
- **Student market is massive.** India has 40M+ college students. If ProPeers captures even a fraction, they'll have a huge user base that could eventually want professional consultation.
- **Mobile-first.** We don't have mobile apps. Students live on their phones.
- **Community model.** Community creates stickiness that transactional marketplaces can't match.

**Their Achilles Heel:**
- **Unfunded.** No resources to scale.
- **Students have no money.** The willingness to pay in the student segment is extremely low. Monetization will be difficult.
- **Peer mentorship is low-value.** A college senior's advice is worth less than a 10-year industry veteran's. The market for peer advice is limited.
- **Very early stage.** They might not survive the next year.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
1. **Ignore for now.** They're not competing for the same creators or the same budget.
2. **Watch for pivot.** If they pivot to connecting students with professionals (instead of peers), they become a feeder into our market.

## LOW THREAT (WATCHING)

### 7. Cal.com

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Model | Open-source scheduling |
| Relevance | Scheduling infrastructure, not marketplace |
| Threat Condition | Only if they add marketplace/payment features |

**Overview:**
Cal.com is an open-source scheduling tool. Consultants use it for booking paid calls. It's not a marketplace — there's no discovery, no reviews, no platform. But it's relevant because it handles the scheduling layer that we also handle.

**Why We're Watching:**
If Cal.com adds marketplace features (expert discovery, reviews, integrated payments, commission model), they could become a serious competitor overnight. They have developer mindshare and open-source community loyalty.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
Our scheduling is good enough. Our differentiation is the marketplace + integrated video + multi-service model, not scheduling alone. Cal.com adding a marketplace would require a complete business model pivot, which is unlikely but possible.

### 8. Calendly

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Model | SaaS scheduling tool |
| Relevance | Scheduling only, no marketplace |
| Threat Condition | Same as Cal.com |

**Overview:**
Calendly is the dominant scheduling tool globally. Some consultants use Calendly + Stripe + Zoom to cobble together a consultation setup. This is our true competitor in the "DIY stack" sense — not another platform, but the combination of tools that a consultant assembles themselves.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
We replace 3-4 tools with one platform. The pitch: "Stop duct-taping Calendly + Zoom + Stripe + a spreadsheet. Use Familiarise."

### 9. WebinarJam

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Model | Webinar-specific SaaS tool |
| Relevance | Overlaps with our webinar feature only |
| Threat Condition | Only relevant for webinar-specific use case |

**Overview:**
WebinarJam (and similar tools like Demio, Livestorm) are purpose-built webinar platforms. They're not marketplaces. They're tools. But consultants who run webinars might use these instead of our webinar feature.

**Our Counter-Strategy:**
Our webinar feature is one of four service types. We're not competing with WebinarJam on webinar features alone — we're offering webinars as part of a complete consultation practice. A consultant who runs webinars AND 1-on-1 calls AND subscriptions AND classes wants one platform, not four tools.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Topmate is the dragon to slay.** Everything we do in the first 6 months should be framed as "why we're better than Topmate for Indian creators."
2. **SuperProfile is the dark horse.** Their pricing model is aggressive and unsustainable, but it could capture market share before it collapses. Watch closely.
3. **GrowthSchool's reputation damage is our opportunity.** Their disillusioned users and instructors are prime targets.
4. **Preplaced validates our initial niche.** Tech mentorship works. We just need to do it with lower commission and more features.
5. **No competitor has integrated video + multi-service + marketplace.** This combination is our strategic gap to fill.
