# ADR: Postgres is the only owner of notification preferences; Novu is a plain in-app transport

- **Status:** accepted by the owner on 2026-11-06.
- **Related:** PR #2623 (the notification subsystem re-land), `docs/notifications/README.md`, `docs/enterprise/70-design-decisions/23-notification-scope.md` and `30-novu-templates-as-code-and-workflow-families.md` (both partly superseded by this record).

## Context

Before this change a user's notification preferences lived in two places. Postgres held `NotificationPreference` and `OrgWorkspaceProfile.notificationRoutingMode` (removed by decision 12). A mirror then copied the same flags, plus name, email and phone, onto the Novu subscriber, and each Novu workflow step carried a `skip` condition that read the mirrored flags. The server already rechecked Postgres before every trigger, so the mirror could only drop a bell that the server had approved while the outbox recorded the row as `SENT`. Users could also mute a family on Novu's own preference page, which no Familiarise screen showed or controlled. In-app quiet hours deferred a bell while the email for the same event went out at once. Maintenance notices and announcements were Novu broadcasts with no preference check at all.

Three facts shaped the answer:

1. The Novu Free plan allows 10,000 workflow runs a month, and Development and Production runs are counted against the same quota. Every bell recipient is one run.
2. On 2026-11-06 the live database held zero `PENDING` or `RETRY` rows in `FailedEmail`, `FailedEmailBatch` and `NotificationOutbox`, so no queued row needs a data migration.
3. No maintenance window is ever scheduled ahead (`MaintenanceWindow.scheduledAt` is never set), the bell relay is blocked while maintenance is `OFFLINE` (`blockingPhaseFor` in `lib/maintenance-cron.ts`), and users cannot open the app during that phase anyway.

## Decision

1. **Postgres is the single preference owner.** The Novu preference mirror, the step `skip` conditions and the native-mute adoption UI are deleted. The sync writes every family with `preferences` set to `readOnly: true` (`FAMILY_PREFERENCES` in `lib/novu/templates/index.ts`), and the inbox mounts with `hideNav`, so Novu has no preference editor. The server filters recipients when a bell is staged and again at the first attempt (`lib/novu/policy.ts`). Stray native mutes that users saved on Novu's old preference page are cleared once per environment by the `clear-native-mutes` script (dry run first, then `--apply`), before `npm run novu:sync` makes the families read-only.
2. **In-app quiet hours are removed.** The consultant planning reminder (`lib/booking/week-nudge.ts`) still skips a consultant who is inside their quiet hours, for both its email and its bell. When no hours are stored, the defaults of 22:00 to 08:00 apply. The settings control is labelled "Email quiet hours".
3. **Announcement inbox posts are opt-in.** An admin ticks `postToInbox` on create. The audience is capped at `MAX_BELL_AUDIENCE` (2,000 eligible users), and the dialog shows the eligible count and asks for confirmation before posting, because each recipient spends one Novu run.
4. **Maintenance bells are deleted.** `maintenance-scheduled`, `maintenance-started`, `maintenance-ended` and `stageMaintenanceBells` no longer exist. `components/banners/MaintenanceBanner.tsx` covers the `DEGRADED` phase and the middleware maintenance page covers `OFFLINE`. Advance notice goes through an announcement with the opt-in inbox post. The `platform` family now carries only `general-announcement` and `compliance-deadline-alert`.
5. **Email relay status writes are compare-and-set (CAS).** Every status write in `jobs/email/retry-failed-emails.ts`, `settleRow` in `lib/email/deliver.ts` and `settleBatch` in `app/api/admin/waitlist/broadcast/route.ts` is an `updateMany` filtered on `status: UNSETTLED` (`PENDING` or `RETRY`, from `lib/retry/provider-window.ts`). A lost race is skipped and not counted, so a late writer can never overwrite `SENT`. The bell relay already did this (`settleRow` in `lib/novu/outbox.ts`).
6. **The `firstAttemptAt` backfill is skipped.** With zero `PENDING` or `RETRY` rows on 2026-11-06 there is nothing to backfill. The legacy cutover guards (`review:legacy_delivery_format` and `review:legacy_broadcast` in `lib/novu/outbox.ts`) stay until the production release. If a read-only count of `PENDING` or `RETRY` rows is still zero then, a cleanup PR deletes them together with the `clear-native-mutes` script.
7. **Novu capacity: stay on the Free plan with the 2,000 cap.** A follow-up adds a usage guard: an ops-digest line that counts monthly bell runs from our own `NotificationOutbox` rows that are `SENT`, summed over their recipients. Upgrade to Pro once monthly runs pass about 60 % of the quota (6,000 of 10,000). Keep Development testing small, because it spends the same quota.
8. **Novu Production is changed at the release promotion.** `clear-native-mutes`, `novu:sync` and `novu:check` for Production run in the same window as the production release that carries this change. The old production code still writes subscriber preferences, so Production must not be synced earlier.
9. **The organisation routing mode stays the account-wide channel gate (superseded 2026-11-07 by decision 12).** For bells, `EMAIL_ONLY` and `NEITHER` blocked the recipient; for optional email, `BELL_ONLY` and `NEITHER` blocked it. Nothing reads the mode any more.
10. **Follow-ups, in order.** PR 5 is the settings experience: accessible and optimistic controls, categories filtered by role, honest copy, a preferences panel on the organisation workspace page, an inbox-to-settings link and an unsubscribe undo. PR 6 is consent and erasure: DPDP `MARKETING_COMMS` consent drives the Waitlist newsletter status, the dead `marketingEmails` switch is removed, erasure scrubs queued email rows and the Waitlist, and the cookie banner is removed until a non-essential script ships. Until the banner is removed, toasts render behind the cookie bar (react-cookie-consent's default z-index is 999, the toast viewport's is `z-[100]` in `components/ui/toast.tsx`); the work is tracked in #2589. PR 7 is the domain producers: render React Email outside money transactions, and send moderation notices after commit. A P3 ops PR follows. A further follow-up was to make a Novu step that fails after Novu accepted the trigger visible; decision 16 does it with an activity probe.

11. **Required bells always ring (amended 2026-11-07).** A bell template marked `required: true` (verification status, new consultant application, moderation warning, account security notice, content removed, suspension, ban, compliance deadline alert; each has `category: null`) skips the master switch and the In-App switch, exactly as a null-category email does; only erasure stops it. `general-announcement` has no category but stays optional. Both gates enforce it: `isBellAllowedForRecipient` and the SQL `bellAllowedWhere` in `lib/novu/policy.ts`, and the real-Postgres parity test covers it. The settings panel built under decision 10 (PR 5) says so in its copy.

12. **Workspace routing removed (2026-11-07).** It duplicated the personal channel switches for the same user. It reverses decision 9. `OrgWorkspaceProfile` is one-to-one with the operator's own user (`userId @unique`), so the routing mode and the personal In-App and Email switches gated the same channels for the same person, and the server ANDed them. The personal `NotificationPreference` switches are now the only channel control for every user, operators included: neither `lib/novu/policy.ts` nor `lib/email/preferences.ts` reads the mode, the workspace settings page shows only the personal panel, and `PATCH /api/org-workspace/[orgWorkspaceId]/settings` rejects the field (its body is strict). The column stays in the database, unread, until the owner's next schema batch drops it; the two live profiles were both effectively default, so there is no data migration and no backfill.

13. **Vendor configuration is checked, not assumed (2026-11-07).** A read-only audit found three gaps that code review could not show:
    - The Resend webhook never received `email.suppressed` or the domain-health events, although the handler has supported them since it was written. The subscription is now `email.sent`, `delivered`, `delivery_delayed`, `bounced`, `complained`, `failed`, `suppressed`, `domain.updated` and `domain.deleted`. `contact.*` stays unsubscribed because Resend Contacts are unused.
    - Novu Production's In-App HMAC enforcement could not be read through the API, so it is now an owner check plus a post-release tampered-hash test.
    - 333 Development subscribers still held mirrored profile data (email, names, phone, avatar, `data` flags). the `scrub-subscriber-pii` script cleared them on 2026-11-07; Production runs at the release promotion.

    Go-live checklist §4.1 now lists every vendor-side setting to verify against the dashboards. The owner fixes the DNS and Netlify hygiene items there: the DMARC report-authorisation TXT on `practitionist.com`, the Netlify secret flags, the stale `dev`-context Resend key, and a sending-only Resend key.

14. **Consent, the newsletter and erasure (2026-11-07).** This is PR 6 of decision 10. Read-only counts on 2026-11-07 shaped it: 51 `Waitlist` rows (39 `SUBSCRIBED`, 6 `PENDING`, 5 `UNSUBSCRIBED`, 1 `BOUNCED`), none linked to a user or matching a user's address; two active `MARKETING_COMMS` grants; `marketingEmails = true` on one user with no sender reading it; 255 `cookie_preferences` rows while no analytics or marketing script loads; no erased users and no open `FailedEmail` rows for one.
    - **One marketing control.** The DPDP `MARKETING_COMMS` consent is the only marketing switch. Granting it upserts the account's `Waitlist` row by normalised address in the same transaction (`userId`, `SUBSCRIBED`, `confirmedAt` now, `unsubscribedAt` null, hashed IP and user agent); the authenticated consent artifact is the evidence, so no double-opt-in email is sent. Withdrawing it unsubscribes the account's rows. The newsletter's own unsubscribe commits first, then withdraws a linked account's consent in its own best-effort transaction, logged once on failure; the page never says "unsubscribed" after a failed write. The "Marketing & Promotions" switch is deleted, the strict preferences PUT rejects `marketingEmails`, and the column stays, unread, in the data export until the schema batch. Consent is checked by purpose, never by a pinned notice version; no lookup pins one. The `MARKETING_COMMS` description now says it includes the Familiarise newsletter, so `TERMS_VERSION` moved from 2 to 3; every grant path (the personal and organisation consent routes, onboarding, signup) stamps it on the server instead of trusting a client-sent `1`, and older grants stay valid. The onboarding checkboxes render the same `PURPOSE_CODE_META` text, and an onboarding marketing tick subscribes the newsletter like any grant.
    - **Erasure scope.** Inside the erasure transaction, `scrubMailFootprintTx` deletes the user's `Waitlist` rows, dead-letters their queued `FailedEmail` rows as `policy:recipient_erased` (the relay's existing reason) with subject and bodies redacted, replaces `recipient` on every one of their `FailedEmail` rows, settled ones included, with `erased:` plus 16 hex of the SHA-256 of the normalised address, and removes the address from every `FailedEmailBatch` payload. A queued batch the provider never saw keeps sending to the rest; a queued one it may have seen is dead-lettered `review:erased_recipient`, because a narrowed payload cannot reuse the stored idempotency key; a settled batch just loses the messages, since nothing replays it. The send gate and the admin replay both refuse the marker. Bells need nothing new: the relay already drops erased recipients at send time (`unerasedRecipients`). **`EmailSuppression` is retained**: it holds only the address and the reason, and it is the record that keeps a bounced or complaining address unmailable if the address is entered again, so deleting it would let an erasure resume mail to someone who refused it.
    - **Cookie banner removed.** Familiarise sets only essential cookies (sign-in, form protection, checkout), which need no consent, and no non-essential script loads. The banner, the account Cookies section, both cookie-preference APIs, the `--cookie-bar-height` reserve and `react-cookie-consent` are deleted, which also stops toasts rendering behind the bar. The privacy policy says a consent choice will be asked before any non-essential script is added. `CookiePreference` is unread until the schema batch drops it. The privacy policy's section 6 also names the essential third-party services the app loads to deliver features: Novu (the in-app inbox), Stream (video and chat), Razorpay (checkout) and Sentry (error monitoring, with its automatic IP, cookie, header and user collection off and no session replay, as set in `sentry.shared.config.ts`). None sets cookies on our domain or is used for advertising or tracking, and the section links to the processor list in section 4.2.
    - **Analytics consent removed.** No analytics code exists, so the `ANALYTICS` toggle gated nothing. It is no longer offered: the consent UIs drop it, both consent routes refuse to grant it (`PURPOSE_NOT_OFFERED`), and the privacy policy, the data export and the purpose text no longer claim usage analytics or telemetry. `PURPOSE_CODES.ANALYTICS` and stored artifacts stay as evidence, and a stored grant can still be withdrawn. A read-only count on 2026-11-07 found zero `ANALYTICS` grants, active or withdrawn, so removing the UI strands no one.
    - **Provenance and the last address copy.** Onboarding's marketing grant records the hashed middleware-stamped IP and the user agent, like the settings route. Erasure also replaces the `waitlist:<address>` `entityRef` of newsletter mail and the `contact:<address>` `entityRef` of contact-form mail with the `erased:` marker, and section 4.2 of the privacy policy describes Sentry's collection in plain words (no user details, IP address, cookies, headers or page text, and no session replay).

    - **Org operators no longer see a member's personal marketing choices (2026-11-07).** `GET /api/organizations/[orgId]/consent` shows another member's platform-fiduciary artifacts only for the purposes the org relies on (`SIGNUP_PURPOSES`: `PRIMARY_PROCESSING`, `SESSION_BOOKING`, `STREAM_DATA_PROCESSING`), and drops an artifact left with none. Whether someone joined or left the Familiarise newsletter is between them and Familiarise, not their employer. A member's own view and the org's own fiduciary artifacts are unchanged.

15. **Notifications never roll back a money or moderation write (2026-11-07).** A producer inside a payment, refund, payout, dunning or moderation transaction renders its React Email before the transaction opens and only inserts rows inside it: `prepare*Email` (built on `prepareBuiltSpec` in `lib/email/senders/shared.ts`) renders, and the matching `stage*Email(tx, args, pre)` stages through `stagePreparedEmails` (`lib/email/send-to-recipients.ts`). A pre-render is bound to the exact arguments that produced it, so when the inputs read inside the transaction differ (a roster changed, an occurrence moved) the stager renders inside the transaction instead.
    - Where an input exists only inside the transaction (a restored credit amount, the payer a refund or dispute webhook finds, a payout's in-transaction read), the producer still renders there. A render failure then stages nothing and never throws, because `prepareRecipientEmails` catches it.
    - A required notice (`traceRenderFailure: true`: refund processed and refund failed, the payment receipt, organisation billing receipts, the dunning overdue email, the consultant and organisation payout status emails, and the organisation payout-failed email that `org-payout-service.ts` sends after commit when no status email was staged) that fails to render leaves a body-less `FailedEmail` `DEAD_LETTER` row with `lastError` `review:render_failed` (`stageRenderFailure` in `lib/email/deliver.ts`): inside the transaction when the notice is staged, and through the global client when it is sent after commit (`sendToRecipients`). Needs attention and the ops digest count it, and replay refuses it (`render_failed` in `lib/email/replay-policy.ts`). It is not sent to Sentry, so a broken template in a sweep never reports once per row: a payout run with a broken payout template commits every payout and leaves one trace row per payout recipient and no Sentry event (owner decision, 2026-11-07).
    - The dunning claim therefore commits when its email fails to render. This supersedes decision 3 of `2026-11-01-dunning-atomic-notices.md`; a roster or read failure still rolls the claim back.
    - Moderation notices render before the decision's transaction (`prepareModerationNotices` in `lib/moderation/side-effects.ts`) and are still staged inside it, so the outbox keeps its atomicity; the ban and suspension emails were already staged after commit. Bells (`stageBell`) render no React and stay inside the transaction.

16. **Novu step failures are found by an activity probe (2026-11-07).** Before this, a bell that Novu accepted (`processed`) but whose in-app step then failed inside Novu left its outbox row `SENT`. The only trace was Novu's activity feed, which the Free plan keeps for 24 hours. This is how the empty-redirect failure went unseen until a manual check: 45 of 45 jobs failed with "Bridge execution failed".
    - **The probe.** The registry job `novu-step-failure-probe` (`jobs/notifications/novu-step-failure-probe.ts`) runs on the ticker every 2 hours at `:10`. It reads `GET /v1/notifications` with `channels=in_app` over the last 4 hours, so every event is read by two runs. It takes 50 events a page, Novu's maximum, for at most 10 pages. Each event comes back with its jobs inline (`jobs[].type`, `jobs[].status`, `jobs[].executionDetails`), its `transactionId` and `subscriber.subscriberId`, so one call a page is enough.
    - **What counts.** An in-app job whose status is `failed`, on an event whose `transactionId` is one of our per-recipient ids: `recipientTransactionId(row, subscriberId)` must reproduce it exactly. That ignores foreign ids, such as console test triggers, and any id whose recipient hash does not match.
    - **What it writes.** One `SINGLE` `DEAD_LETTER` row beside the parent. It has the transactionId `<row>:stepfailed:<16 hex of the recipient's SHA-256>`, the one recipient, and the parent's workflow, payload and `entityRef`. Its `lastError` is `review:novu_step_failed`, followed by Novu's step detail, the error code and the failing output paths, for example `review:novu_step_failed: Bridge execution failed (ExecutionStateOutputInvalidError at /redirect/url)`. Novu's free-text message is dropped because it can quote the payload.
    - **Idempotent.** The insert is one `createMany` with `skipDuplicates` on the unique `transactionId`, so a re-read is a no-op. The parent row is never written. That holds whatever the parent's status: Novu treats a repeated transactionId as a duplicate (`eventOutcome` in `lib/novu/outbox.ts` counts it accepted), so a step that failed is never re-run, and a `PENDING` or `RETRY` parent settles as usual while the sibling records the failure.
    - **Alerting.** The `review:` prefix is not an expected skip (`DEAD_LETTER_BELL_WHERE` in `lib/backoffice/queue-predicates.ts` excludes only `policy:`), so Needs attention and the ops digest count it. Nothing goes to Sentry per row. A failed Novu read fails the run, and the cleanup route reports that once per window. A `429`, the page cap or the 10-second page budget stops paging, and the run answers `207` with `truncated: true`.
    - **Cadence.** The ticker's cadence test keeps one dispatch slot free on every tick of 400 days, and every 15, 30 and 60-minute phase already peaks one under the cap. Two hours at `:10` is the shortest interval with room. A 2-hour detection delay suits a review queue that the ops digest reports daily.
    - **Cost.** At most 10 `GET` requests per run and 12 runs a day, so at most 120 Novu reads a day, against 30 requests a second on the Free plan's global rate-limit category. Reads spend no workflow runs. The job is in the `watched` tier (`lib/cron/operational-health.ts`): a late run delays a review row and turns the System Jobs console red without paging. It runs only where `jobsMayRunHere()` allows, which is production, and reads Novu with that environment's key (`lib/novu/secret-key.ts`).
    - **The webhook is deleted.** `POST /api/webhooks/novu`, its handler (`processNovuWebhookPayload`), its Svix schema (`verifyNovuWebhook`), the Novu arm of `sweep-stuck-webhook-events` and `NOVU_WEBHOOK_SECRET` are removed. The route had answered `503` everywhere because the secret was never set, and Novu's outbound webhooks could not have closed this gap; see Deprecated & Superseded Approaches.

17. **Clawback, collaborator and referral notices are staged with their write (2026-11-07).** This is PR 8, the domain notices that had no producer on `dev`. The event count goes from 94 to 96 inside the existing 16 families; no family is added, because the Novu Free plan caps an environment at 20 workflows.
    - **Clawback (two new events).** `payout-clawback` joins the `payout` family and `org-payout-clawback` joins `org-billing`. `stageClawbackNotice` (`lib/payments/payouts/clawback-notifications.ts`) stages one bell per newly created clawback journal, inside the transaction that posts it: `RAISED` from both clawback posters in `lib/payments/operations/reversal-engine.ts` and the refund clawback in `lib/payments/payouts/paid-consultant-clawback.ts`, `OFFSET_RESERVED` from `recoverClawbackOnPayout`, `OFFSET_RELEASED` from `releaseClawbackRecovery`, and `VOIDED` from `voidClawbacksRaisedOnPayout`. A journal that already existed (a replayed refund or webhook) stages nothing. The consultant receives it, or the organisation's `VISIBILITY_ROLES` roster, the same people who receive the payout status notices.
    - **Only a raised debt emails.** `ClawbackNoticeEmail` goes out for `RAISED`, the one event that creates something the payee owes. A deduction, release or void rides with a payout that already sends its own status email, so those are bells only, and a bank-returned payout that voids several debts rings one `VOIDED` bell with their sum. The void path never stages `RAISED`.
    - **Classification: optional, traced.** Both clawback bells use their family's switch (`payments`, `orgBilling`), like the payout bells beside them; a family with a category cannot hold a `required` template. The email uses the same category and `traceRenderFailure: true`, like the payout status email. It renders inside the clawback's transaction, because the amount is computed there (the TDS-net share, the recovered or voided paise); per decision 15 a render failure stages a `review:render_failed` row and never rolls the clawback back.
    - **Collaborators (existing events).** `stageCollaboratorNotifications` (`lib/collaborators/notifications.ts`) stages the bell and the emails in the transaction that moves the row: invite, respond, remove and withdraw in `lib/collaborators/service.ts`, the invite-expiry sweep, `removeCollaboratorStanding` (ban, erasure and account deletion) and org member removal. The invitee hears `INVITED` and `REMOVED`, the host every other event, and `EXPIRED` reaches both. Bells keep the `appointments` switch; the emails stay category-free, as before. An org member removal stages one `WITHDRAWN` notice per row for the host and nothing per plan for the member, who already receives the org removal notice; the post-commit `REMOVED` bell in `revokeCollaboratorAccess` is deleted with its `notify` option. A standing removal names the collaborator only as "A collaborator", because the account may be leaving through erasure or a ban.
    - **Referrals (existing events).** `stageReferralReward` and `stageCreditsApplied` (`lib/referrals/referral-notify.ts`) stage inside the referral, vesting, checkout and capture transactions instead of after commit. The capture renders its credits-applied email before the transaction opens and stages it inside; the checkout rails that never see a capture, the vest and the code application render inside, because the amount or the referral row exists only there. All referral notices stay optional (`payments`). The welcome notice now states the programme's real cap (`discountMaxPaise`) as "up to", and the bonus notice says the referee qualified rather than joined, because the reward vests only after a delivered booking.
    - **Go-live.** `npm run novu:sync` must run in Development after this merges and in Production at the release that carries it (go-live step 13), so the two clawback events and the new referral copy reach the existing families. Until then the synced family bodies have no branch for the two new events, so their bells render without text or fail inside Novu while the outbox row reads `SENT`; the email is unaffected.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the mirror and fix its edge cases.** Rejected: a second copy of the flags can only disagree with Postgres, and the server check already makes it redundant.
- **Let users manage preferences in Novu's inbox page.** Rejected: it is a second, invisible owner that no Familiarise screen shows or controls.
- **Upgrade Novu to Pro now.** Rejected for the pre-launch phase: the 2,000 cap keeps one fan-out at a fifth of the month, and the usage guard raises the alarm well before the quota.
- **Keep maintenance bells and fix their audience.** Rejected: they could never fire usefully, for the reasons in Context.

## Consequences

- A preference change applies to the next notification, because both gates read Postgres at stage time and at the first attempt. No Novu write is involved.
- `npm run novu:check` reports a leftover `skip` control or an editable preference as drift, so a console edit cannot silently bring the second owner back.
- Production Novu and the production release are coupled. If the release slips, the Production sync waits with it.
- The Novu activity feed keeps only 24 hours of history on Free, so our own `NotificationOutbox` is the delivery audit trail. The step-failure probe reads it within that window (decision 16).

## Outcome (2026-11-08)

Release "Release 2026-11-08" was promoted to production and the Production steps of decisions 1, 6, 8 and 13 ran in the same window. The native-mute dry run found 0 mutes across 86 subscribers, so nothing was applied. `novu:sync --env production` applied all 16 families, and two `novu:check --env production` runs (08:29 and 08:34 UTC) were clean. The PII scrub cleared 24 of 86 subscribers and the follow-up dry run showed 0. In-App HMAC was enabled and verified by behaviour: no hash or a wrong hash gets 400 "Please provide a valid HMAC hash", and the correct hash gets 201. `novu-step-failure-probe` ran once and completed with 0 failed steps. The recount found 0 `PENDING` or `RETRY` rows in `FailedEmail`, `FailedEmailBatch` and `NotificationOutbox`, so the cleanup PR deleted the legacy guards in `lib/novu/outbox.ts` and both one-time scripts. The decisions above are unchanged.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- The Novu subscriber preference mirror (`updateSubscriberPreferences`, `syncSubscriber`) and the step `skip` rule (`inAppSkipRule`) are superseded by decision 1. Delete any residual reader of `subscriber.data` flags.
- Novu broadcasts for announcements and maintenance (`notifyGeneralAnnouncement` as a broadcast, `notifyMaintenanceScheduled`, `notifyMaintenanceStarted`, `notifyMaintenanceEnded`) are superseded by decisions 3 and 4. The `BROADCAST` outbox kind is unused.
- Bell deferral by quiet hours is superseded by decision 2.
- The workspace routing mode (`OrgWorkspaceProfile.notificationRoutingMode`, `NotificationRoutingSection.tsx`, `routingBlocks`) is superseded by decision 12. The column is unread until the next schema batch drops it; delete any reader that reappears.
- The marketing preference sync (`marketingEmails`, `CookiePreference.marketing` and `analytics` written by the consent route), the "Marketing & Promotions" switch and the cookie banner with its APIs are superseded by decision 14. Both columns are unread until the schema batch; delete any reader that reappears.
- `stageSpecRequired`, which threw on a render failure so the dunning claim rolled back, and `stageToRecipients`, which rendered inside the caller's transaction, are superseded by decision 15.
- The inbound Novu webhook (`POST /api/webhooks/novu`, `processNovuWebhookPayload`, Svix verification in `verifyNovuWebhook`, `NOVU_WEBHOOK_SECRET`) is superseded by decision 16. It could not have worked. Novu's outbound webhooks are "only available in the Team and Enterprise plans" ([docs.novu.co/platform/developer/webhooks](https://docs.novu.co/platform/developer/webhooks)), and we are on Free. Its failure event, `message.failed`, fires when Novu tries to send the message to the delivery provider and that fails ([event types](https://docs.novu.co/platform/developer/webhooks/event-types)), while "In-app (Inbox) does not call an external provider" ([delivery retries](https://docs.novu.co/platform/developer/delivery-retries)). Delete any webhook receiver for Novu that reappears.
- The post-commit collaborator and referral sends (`notifyCollaborator*`, `sendCollaborator*Email`, `notifyCreditsAppliedBestEffort`, `notifyCreditsAppliedForPaymentBestEffort`, the `notify` option of `revokeCollaboratorAccess`) are superseded by decision 17. Delete any residual caller.
- The `clear-native-mutes` script, the `scrub-subscriber-pii` script and the relay guards `review:legacy_delivery_format` and `review:legacy_broadcast` were one-time cutover tools. They ran or were retired on 2026-11-08 and are deleted; the text of decisions 1, 6, 8 and 13 describes how they were used.
