# ADR: Hosts remove attendees for conduct, and support reviews it within 72 hours

- **Status**: Accepted (owner decisions, 2026-11-15). Built on the branch `feat/conduct-removal`.
- **Date**: 2026-11-15
- **Author**: Platform engineering
- **Related**: [Event membership follows seats](2026-11-14-event-membership-follows-seats.md), [Cancellation flow](../booking/08-cancellation-flow.md), [SLA sweep](../support/07-sla-sweep.md), [Video implementation](../stream/05-video-implementation.md)

## Context

Before this change a host had two ways to take an attendee out of a webinar or class, and neither was a conduct removal.

- The roster's **Remove** (`DELETE /api/participants/{webinar|class}/[id]?userId=`, `leaveEventSeat` in `lib/booking/seat-leave.ts`) is a no-fault removal: it ends the seat and refunds it in full at once. Only the plan owner, an organisation operator and staff may use it; a co-host may not.
- The Stream SDK's **Kick** and **Block** in a live call reach the plan owner through the `-owner` grants that the `call_member` and `user` roles hold on the `default` call type. They leave no Postgres record, no reason and no review, and the seat and its money stay as they were.

A disruptive attendee therefore either kept their seat and their access, or was refunded in full by the host they disrupted.

## Decisions

1. **One transaction at removal, with `tx` passed everywhere, and no refund.** `removeAttendeeForConduct` (`lib/booking/conduct-removal.ts`) runs under the event's appointment lock in one Serializable transaction:
   - the seat ends: `releaseParticipant` moves it to `CANCELLED` by compare-and-set on its live statuses, so a repeat finds nothing and returns `removed: false`;
   - a `CONDUCT_REMOVAL` support case opens for the attendee (`openConductRemovalCaseTx` in `lib/support/conduct-removal-case.ts`). It records the removal itself: the attendee is the requester and the submitter, the `CREATED` event's actor is the remover, the description and first message carry the reason and note, and the case's creation time is when;
   - the seat's unpaid earnings are held for the case (`holdSeatEarningsTx`, through `holdPaymentForCase`, a `SUPPORT_CASE` hold with a `HOLD_APPLIED` event). A payout already being sent cannot be held; the case still opens and an internal message says why;
   - the Stream intents are written in the same transaction: `persistSeatRevocationInTx` (event channel, host DMs, call membership) and `persistConductBlockInTx` (the call block, kind `CONDUCT_CALL_BLOCK`).

   After the commit, `syncConductCallBlock` and `revokeEndedSeatAccess` drive both intents inside the request's budget and settle only the generation the transaction wrote. An instance that dies after the commit leaves both rows `PENDING`, and `retry-moderation-enforcement` re-drives them through `redriveAccessRevocation`. The old design called Stream first and queued a retry only when the call returned false, so a timeout between the commit and the queue lost the kick.
   - Options not chosen: driving Stream before the commit (a Stream failure would roll back a valid removal); queueing the retry after the Stream call (the old gap).

2. **Stream blocks; it does not only kick.** `driveConductCallBlock` (`lib/stream/conduct-call-block.ts`) reads the removal from Postgres first, then pages through every room of the appointment whose `Meeting.endedAt` is null, 50 at a time and from the last room its intent finished (`StreamRevocationRetry.resumeAfter`), and in each calls `call.kickUser({ user_id, block: true })`, which disconnects the attendee and blocks them from rejoining, falling back to `call.blockUser({ user_id })` when Stream answers that the user is not in the call. A room whose Stream call does not exist (both answer 404) has nobody to block and settles; a room provisioned later is still closed to the attendee, because its join gate needs a seat that matches `paidParticipant()` and the removal ended it. A genuine failure on the request's own drive is recorded on the intent (`attempts`, `lastError`) for the sweep. The event channel removal comes from the seat intent. When the removal is overturned the same intent kind is written again and the same drive calls `call.unblockUser({ user_id })`. A drive re-reads the removal after its writes and after any failed write (a lost response may still have applied); when the direction changed meanwhile, the removal cannot be read, or a call that outlived the drive's budget settles later either way, it writes a fresh intent, so the sweep puts the rooms back in line with Postgres instead of leaving a stale block or unblock. The calls match `KickUserRequest`, `BlockUserRequest` and `UnblockUserRequest` in the installed `@stream-io/node-sdk` 0.8.15 typings and the Node samples on Stream's "Kicking & Blocking a member from a call" page.
   - Options not chosen: a kick without a block (the attendee could rejoin a room that still lists them); a separate unblock kind (one kind whose direction comes from Postgres cannot drive the wrong way after a re-arm).

3. **The refund on an overturn or a missed deadline is whatever is still unrefunded on the seat's payment, on the rails it came from.** The amount is the payment's refundable balance from `refundableBalancePaise` (`REFUNDABLE_BALANCE_SELECT`): a `FAILED` or `CANCELLED` refund does not count, a `PENDING` or `SUCCEEDED` one does. A free seat (`isPriceFreePayment`) owes nothing. A credit-funded seat owes a credit restoration.
   - Worked example: a ₹1,000 class seat with ₹250 already refunded and a ₹100 refund that failed is owed ₹750.
   - **Overturn: refund first, then decide (owner decision, 2026-11-15).** `OVERTURNED` on a removal case, or on an appeal of one, is refused with `REFUND_FIRST` while anything is still owed (`assertRemovalRefundedFirst` in `lib/support/case-actions.ts`, reading `removalRefundOutstandingTx`). Support refunds through the existing doors: `RECOMMEND_REFUND` for the balance, then `REVIEW_REFUND` through the bounded door (`assertBoundedRefund`). Within the staff limits (₹5,000 a payment, ₹50,000 a rolling 24 hours) the staff reviewer's approval sends it at once and the door's ops-log row counts its `movedPaise`; above them it waits for an admin's approval. Once the refund is sent, the overturn records and writes the unblock intent. No support guard changed. Example: a ₹6,000 seat waits for an admin, then the overturn records; a ₹4,000 seat is refunded by a second staff member at once, then the overturn records. An admin overturn uses the admin authority of the same doors.
   - **An admin correcting a decided removal refunds through the admin Refunds door first (owner decision, 2026-11-15).** Once a removal is `UPHELD`, its case's recommendations can only be declined (`assertUndecidedForApproval`), so the refund-first path above is closed for a correction. An admin superseding `UPHELD` with `OVERTURNED` inside the appeal window is refused with `REFUND_FIRST` until the seat's balance is ₹0, and the refusal names the door: **Issue refund** on the payment's back-office page (`/dashboard/admin/payments/<paymentId>?refund=1`, the same door as Money › Refunds, `POST /api/admin/refunds/issue`, admin-only `refunds.manage`, step-up, audited by `withOpsAction`). The admin refunds there and comes back to record the correction. Example: a ₹1,000 seat upheld on Monday and corrected on Tuesday is refunded ₹1,000 from its payment page, then the overturn records. No support guard changed. Options not chosen: corrections only through an appeal case; an exception on the decided case that lets its recommendation be approved.
   - **Missed deadline: automatic.** The lapse stamps one full-balance refund-owed marker (`amountPaise: null`) keyed `conduct-removal:<caseId>:pay:<paymentId>`; `driveRefundOwed` drives it through the refund front door with no amount, and `retry-auto-refunds` re-drives one a failure or a crash leaves. It is the learner's backstop when an approval is slow: a lapse refund that lands first leaves the later approval nothing to send, because the review re-reads the balance (`assertRefundExecutable` refuses with `AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_REFUNDABLE`), and a staff refund that lands first leaves the lapse marker nothing to refund.
   - An **upheld** removal refunds nothing automatically; support uses its existing refund doors.
   - Options not chosen: decide now with the refund pending as an open recommendation (it needs exempting two existing guards, `assertNoOpenRecommendation` and `assertUndecidedForApproval`, and keeping the earnings hold past the resolution); admin-only overturns above the staff limit; exempting this refund from the staff limits; counting refund rows to decide whether the remedy was paid (a ₹1 partial refund passed as "refunded in full"); a fixed amount from the list price (wrong after a partial refund or a dispute).

4. **The review deadline is 72 hours, enforced by an arm in the existing support SLA sweep.** `DEADLINE_HOURS.CONDUCT_REMOVAL` (`lib/support/deadline-policy.ts`) sets the case's `statutoryDueAt`, so the sweep's existing SLA notices warn staff before it. `sweepConductRemovalDeadlines`, called from `runSupportSlaSweep` (`lib/support/sla-sweep.ts`) with at most 5 cases a run, takes removal cases created 72 hours ago or more with no `REMOVAL_REVIEW_LAPSED` event and no decision made within the 72 hours. A decision recorded after the deadline does not cancel the refund (owner decision, 2026-11-15): a removal at 10:00 Monday is due by 10:00 Thursday, so an uphold at 11:00 Thursday is still followed by the refund on the next run. The query compares each case's first decision with its own deadline, so it is raw SQL (`lapsedCaseIds`). In one transaction per case it first locks the case row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) and only then re-reads eligibility, so a run that committed a lapse meanwhile is seen and this one stands down; it bumps `updatedAt`, so a decision holding the old version is refused as stale; it then writes that event, stamps the refund of decision 3 and posts a message to the attendee, and drives the refund after the commit. The lock and the event make the arm fire once. The block stays until support decides.
   - Options not chosen: a new job, route or scheduler (`lib/cron/AGENTS.md`); 7 days, the old draft's default.

5. **The Stream SDK's kick and block bypass is closed by the call type contract.** `intendedDefaultCallTypeGrants` (`lib/stream/control-plane-contract.ts`) now strips `kick-user` and `block-user` in every scope (`SERVER_ONLY_CALL_MODERATION`) from the `user` and `call_member` roles of the `default` call type. `co_presenter` never held them. The owner applies it with `scripts/stream/ensure-call-type-grants.ts` after the deploy; the steps are in [Video implementation](../stream/05-video-implementation.md). The only thing that relied on those grants is the SDK participant list's Kick and Block items for the call creator; the server's own `kickUser` calls (`revokeCollaboratorAccess` and the conduct drive) run as the server client, which grants do not limit.

6. **Schema: two enum values, no table.** Every fact a removal needs is already recorded by an existing row, so no `ParticipantRemoval` table is added:

   | Fact                       | Recorded by                                                                            |
   | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
   | who was removed, from what | `SupportCase.requesterUserId`, `appointmentId`, the `APPOINTMENT` subject              |
   | who removed them           | the `CREATED` `SupportCaseEvent`'s `actorId`                                           |
   | why                        | the case description and first message (reason label and note)                         |
   | when                       | `SupportCase.createdAt`; the seat's own `CANCELLED` status                             |
   | the outcome                | `SupportCaseDecision` (`UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED`), or the `REMOVAL_REVIEW_LAPSED` event |

   `SupportCase.category` is a free string, so `CONDUCT_REMOVAL` needs no `SupportThreadCategory` value. Two values earn their place: `StreamRevocationKind.CONDUCT_CALL_BLOCK`, because no existing kind blocks or unblocks a call, and `SupportCaseEventKind.REMOVAL_REVIEW_LAPSED`, because no existing row records that the deadline passed, and the arm needs that row to fire once. The owner applies both with the release reconcile push of #2762; `prisma/sql/known-drift.json` lists the two statements.
   - Options not chosen: a `ParticipantRemoval` table with its own status enum (a second copy of the case's facts and outcome); a `SupportThreadCategory` value (the case column is not that enum).

7. **The attendee is told.** After the commit the removal sends the removed-seat notice (`notifySeatRemoved` in `lib/booking/seat-removed-notice.ts`) as a required `SEAT_RELEASED` notice, whatever the attendee's switches say. Its bell and email say the host "removed you", never "cancelled", carry the host's reason and note, the case number and the 72-hour decision line (`removalNote`, the bell's `removalNote` payload field), and open the case. Home lists the open case under Needs you, asking for the learner's side. Support's decision reaches them as a required `SEAT_RELEASED` bell too (`removalDecisionNotice`): an overturn says the seat is not given back, that a price-free seat can be registered for again or that support will help rejoin a paid one, and offers no appeal; an uphold names the 7-day appeal. The case's booking card leads to the learner's Cancelled list, since the booking page no longer admits a released seat, and the page does not read the booking thread the learner is refused. The refunds an overturn or a lapse sends announce themselves through the shared refund path, which says a refund still pending at the gateway has started (`refundStartedText`).

8. **Who may remove.** The plan owner and an accepted presenter-tier co-host (`CO_HOST`, `CO_INSTRUCTOR`), as the owner decided, plus the people who may already remove attendees on the roster: an active organisation operator with `catalog.manage` or `appointments.actForOrg.cancel`, and staff (`canRemoveForConduct` in `lib/api/participants/group-participants-route.ts`). Crew collaborators and attendees may not. The route checks the rule before it takes the appointment lock, and `removeAttendeeForConduct` checks it again on `tx` as the first step of its transaction, so a co-host revoked while the removal waited is refused with 403 `REMOVAL_NOT_ALLOWED` and nothing is written. Three doors share that rule and the one transaction: `POST /api/participants/{webinar|class}/[id]/remove` (the roster and the event chat) and `POST /api/meetings/[meetingId]/remove` (the live room's People tab).

Support decides a removal case as an appeal is decided: `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` (`assertDecisionFitsCase` in `lib/support/case-actions.ts`; the staff panel offers those two through `reviewsRemoval`). An appeal of that decision decided `OVERTURNED` overturns the removal too. An overturned removal cannot be superseded back to upheld, because its refund was sent and its block lifted. Since 2026-11-18 the removal also marks the seat (`removedByUserId`), which blocks a rejoin; an overturn clears the mark in `conductRemovalDecidedTx`, and staff can lift it at any time without a decision, so an upheld finding stays on the record while the learner may come back ([Free seat rejoin](2026-11-18-free-seat-rejoin.md), E4). "The removal stands" (`conductRemovalStands`) needs the mark too, so a lifted removal no longer blocks the calls. A removal case is a sensitive personal category (`ORG_SENSITIVE_PERSONAL_CATEGORIES`), so the host, a presenter and organisation views never read the attendee's side. It files under its own topic, "Conduct removal" (`removal` in `lib/support/case-topic.ts`), and the staff panel shows its decision deadline ("Removal decision due", from `statutoryDueAt`) and the requester's own seat status in the Booking drawer.

Tests: `__tests__/db/booking/conduct-removal.db.test.ts` (the one transaction and its rollback, the crash and the redrive, refund-first overturns of ₹6,000 and ₹4,000 seats, a lapse that lands before the admin, the unblock, the free and credit seats, the ₹750 remainder, the 72-hour arm firing once, a late uphold that still refunds), `__tests__/db/booking/conduct-removal-races.db.test.ts` (overlapping deadline runs, 51 rooms, stale blocks, one report a run, a co-host revoked mid-removal), `__tests__/booking/conduct-removal-route.test.ts` (who may remove), `__tests__/events/conduct-removal-dialog.test.tsx` (what the in-room control sends, parsed by the route's schema).

## Limits that remain

- A `PENDING` refund counts against the balance when the overturn or the lapse is driven. If that refund fails later, its own door restores what it owed (`restoreOwedOnFailedRefund` for a support hand refund, the marker's attempt count for a marker drive); the conduct removal does not refund it again.
- A room that Postgres has stamped ended but Stream has not is not blocked; the seat revocation still removes the attendee from its members.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- **Kick first, queue the retry on failure** (the first draft of this feature): the retry row was written only after Stream answered, so a crash in between lost it. Superseded by intents written in the removal's transaction.
- **Counting refund rows as the deadline remedy** (the first draft): superseded by the refundable balance and one keyed full-balance marker.
