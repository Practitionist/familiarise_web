# ADR: Hosts remove attendees for conduct, and support reviews it within 72 hours

- **Status**: Accepted (owner decisions, 2026-11-15). Built on the branch `feat/conduct-removal`.
- **Date**: 2026-11-15
- **Author**: Platform engineering
- **Related**: [Event membership follows seats](2026-11-14-event-membership-follows-seats.md), [Cancellation flow](../booking/08-cancellation-flow.md), [SLA sweep](../support/07-sla-sweep.md), [Video implementation](../stream/05-video-implementation.md)

## Context

Before this change a host had two ways to take an attendee out of a webinar or class, and neither was a conduct removal.

- The roster's **Remove** (`DELETE /api/participants/{webinar|class}/[id]?userId=`, `leaveEventSeat` in `lib/booking/seat-leave.ts`) is a no-fault removal: it ends the seat and refunds it in full at once. Only the plan owner, an organisation operator and staff may use it; a co-host may not.
- The Stream SDK's **Kick** and **Block** in a live call reach the plan owner through the `-owner` grants that the `call_member` and `user` roles hold on the `default` call type. They leave no Postgres record, no reason and no review, and the seat and its money stay as they were.

A disruptive attendee therefore either kept their seat and their access, or was refunded in full by the host they disrupted.

## Decisions

1. **One transaction at removal, with `tx` passed everywhere, and no refund.** `removeAttendeeForConduct` (`lib/booking/conduct-removal.ts`) runs under the event's appointment lock in one Serializable transaction:
   - the seat ends: `releaseParticipant` moves it to `CANCELLED` by compare-and-set on its live statuses, so a repeat finds nothing and returns `removed: false`;
   - a `CONDUCT_REMOVAL` support case opens for the attendee (`openConductRemovalCaseTx` in `lib/support/conduct-removal-case.ts`). It records the removal itself: the attendee is the requester and the submitter, the `CREATED` event's actor is the remover, the description and first message carry the reason and note, and the case's creation time is when;
   - the seat's unpaid earnings are held for the case (`holdSeatEarningsTx`, through `holdPaymentForCase`, a `SUPPORT_CASE` hold with a `HOLD_APPLIED` event). A price-free seat has no earnings, so nothing is held and the host's toast does not mention a hold. A removal that joins a case which already holds the payment freezes any newer earnings under the same hold and writes no second `HOLD_APPLIED`. A payout already being sent cannot be held; the case still opens and an internal message says why;
   - the Stream intents are written in the same transaction: `persistSeatRevocationInTx` (event channel, host DMs, call membership) and `persistConductBlockInTx` (the call block, kind `CONDUCT_CALL_BLOCK`).

   After the commit, `syncConductCallBlock` and `revokeEndedSeatAccess` drive both intents inside the request's budget and settle only the generation the transaction wrote. An instance that dies after the commit leaves both rows `PENDING`, and `retry-moderation-enforcement` re-drives them through `redriveAccessRevocation`. The old design called Stream first and queued a retry only when the call returned false, so a timeout between the commit and the queue lost the kick.
   - Options not chosen: driving Stream before the commit (a Stream failure would roll back a valid removal); queueing the retry after the Stream call (the old gap).

2. **Stream blocks; it does not only kick.** `driveConductCallBlock` (`lib/stream/conduct-call-block.ts`) reads the removal from Postgres first, then pages through every room of the appointment whose `Meeting.endedAt` is null, 50 at a time and from the last room its intent finished (`StreamRevocationRetry.resumeAfter`), and in each calls `call.kickUser({ user_id, block: true })`, which disconnects the attendee and blocks them from rejoining, falling back to `call.blockUser({ user_id })` when Stream answers that the user is not in the call. A room whose Stream call does not exist (both answer 404) has nobody to block and settles; a room provisioned later is still closed to the attendee, because its join gate needs a seat that matches `paidParticipant()` and the removal ended it. A genuine failure on the request's own drive is recorded on the intent (`attempts`, `lastError`) for the sweep. The event channel removal comes from the seat intent. When the removal is overturned the same intent kind is written again and the same drive calls `call.unblockUser({ user_id })`. A drive re-reads the removal after its writes and after any failed write (a lost response may still have applied); when the direction changed meanwhile, the removal cannot be read, or a call that outlived the drive's budget settles later either way, it writes a fresh intent, so the sweep puts the rooms back in line with Postgres instead of leaving a stale block or unblock. The calls match `KickUserRequest`, `BlockUserRequest` and `UnblockUserRequest` in the installed `@stream-io/node-sdk` 0.8.15 typings and the Node samples on Stream's "Kicking & Blocking a member from a call" page.
   - Options not chosen: a kick without a block (the attendee could rejoin a room that still lists them); a separate unblock kind (one kind whose direction comes from Postgres cannot drive the wrong way after a re-arm).

3. **The refund on an overturn is whatever is still unrefunded on the seat's payment; on a missed deadline it is the sessions the seat was not delivered (amended 2026-11-25). Both go back on the rails the seat was paid from.** The overturn's amount is the payment's refundable balance from `refundableBalancePaise` (`REFUNDABLE_BALANCE_SELECT`): a `FAILED` or `CANCELLED` refund does not count, a `PENDING` or `SUCCEEDED` one does. A free seat (`isPriceFreePayment`) owes nothing. A credit-funded seat owes a credit restoration.
   - Worked example: a ₹1,000 class seat with ₹250 already refunded and a ₹100 refund that failed is owed ₹750.
   - **Overturn: refund first, then decide (owner decision, 2026-11-15).** `OVERTURNED` on a removal case, or on an appeal of one, is refused with `REFUND_FIRST` while anything is still owed (`assertRemovalRefundedFirst` in `lib/support/case-actions.ts`, reading `removalRefundOutstandingTx`). Support refunds through the existing doors: `RECOMMEND_REFUND` for the balance, then `REVIEW_REFUND` through the bounded door (`assertBoundedRefund`). Within the staff limits (₹5,000 a payment, ₹50,000 a rolling 24 hours) the staff reviewer's approval sends it at once and the door's ops-log row counts its `movedPaise`; above them it waits for an admin's approval. Once the refund is sent, the overturn records and writes the unblock intent. No support guard changed. Example: a ₹6,000 seat waits for an admin, then the overturn records; a ₹4,000 seat is refunded by a second staff member at once, then the overturn records. An admin overturn uses the admin authority of the same doors.
   - **An admin correcting a decided removal refunds through the admin Refunds door first (owner decision, 2026-11-15).** Once a removal is `UPHELD`, its case's recommendations can only be declined (`assertUndecidedForApproval`), so the refund-first path above is closed for a correction. An admin superseding `UPHELD` with `OVERTURNED` inside the appeal window is refused with `REFUND_FIRST` until the seat's balance is ₹0, and the refusal names the door: **Issue refund** on the payment's back-office page (`/dashboard/admin/payments/<paymentId>?refund=1`, the same door as Money › Refunds, `POST /api/admin/refunds/issue`, admin-only `refunds.manage`, step-up, audited by `withOpsAction`). The admin refunds there and comes back to record the correction. Example: a ₹1,000 seat upheld on Monday and corrected on Tuesday is refunded ₹1,000 from its payment page, then the overturn records. No support guard changed. Options not chosen: corrections only through an appeal case; an exception on the decided case that lets its recommendation be approved.
   - **Missed deadline: automatic, undelivered sessions only (owner decision, 2026-11-25).** The lapse stamps one refund-owed marker keyed `conduct-removal:<caseId>:pay:<paymentId>` for the seat's amount less one unit for each session it holds that had ended by the removal (`releasedSeatOwedTx` in `lib/payments/operations/event-refunds.ts`, over `releasedSeatRefundPaise` in `lib/booking/class-series.ts`), less any session refunded on its own, capped at the refundable balance. It is measured at the removal, not at the lapse, because the class runs on without the learner. A session under way at the removal is not delivered: support did not rule against the learner in time, and a host must not keep a session's pay by removing someone five minutes in. A credit-funded seat is restored whole when nothing was delivered and is queued for a person (`partial-credit:<paymentId>`) otherwise. `driveRefundOwed` drives the marker through the refund front door, and `retry-auto-refunds` re-drives one a failure or a crash leaves. It is the learner's backstop when an approval is slow: a lapse refund that lands first leaves a later overturn only the remainder to send, because the review re-reads the balance (`assertRefundExecutable` refuses with `AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_REFUNDABLE`), and a staff refund that lands first caps the lapse at what is left. Examples: an ₹8,000 seat on an 8-session class removed after session 3, or during session 4, is refunded ₹5,000; removed before session 1, ₹8,000; a ₹1,000 webinar seat removed mid-webinar, ₹1,000, and removed after it ended, nothing.
   - An **upheld** removal refunds nothing automatically; support uses its existing refund doors.
   - Options not chosen: decide now with the refund pending as an open recommendation (it needs exempting two existing guards, `assertNoOpenRecommendation` and `assertUndecidedForApproval`, and keeping the earnings hold past the resolution); admin-only overturns above the staff limit; exempting this refund from the staff limits; counting refund rows to decide whether the remedy was paid (a ₹1 partial refund passed as "refunded in full"); a fixed amount from the list price (wrong after a partial refund or a dispute).

4. **The review deadline is 72 hours, enforced by an arm in the existing support SLA sweep.** `DEADLINE_HOURS.CONDUCT_REMOVAL` (`lib/support/deadline-policy.ts`) sets the case's `statutoryDueAt`, so the sweep's existing SLA notices warn staff before it. `sweepConductRemovalDeadlines`, called from `runSupportSlaSweep` (`lib/support/sla-sweep.ts`) with at most 5 cases a run, takes removal cases whose `statutoryDueAt` has passed with no `REMOVAL_REVIEW_LAPSED` event and no decision made since the case's latest removal (decision 9 restarts the deadline). A decision recorded after the deadline does not cancel the refund (owner decision, 2026-11-15): a removal at 10:00 Monday is due by 10:00 Thursday, so an uphold at 11:00 Thursday is still followed by the refund on the next run. The query compares each case's first decision with its own deadline, so it is raw SQL (`lapsedCaseIds`). In one transaction per case it first locks the case row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) and only then re-reads eligibility, so a run that committed a lapse meanwhile is seen and this one stands down; it bumps `updatedAt`, so a decision holding the old version is refused as stale; it then writes that event, stamps the refund of decision 3 and posts a message to the attendee, and drives the refund after the commit. The lock and the event make the arm fire once. The block stays until support decides.
   - Options not chosen: a new job, route or scheduler (`lib/cron/AGENTS.md`); 7 days, the old draft's default.

5. **The Stream SDK's kick and block bypass is closed by the call type contract.** `intendedDefaultCallTypeGrants` (`lib/stream/control-plane-contract.ts`) now strips `kick-user` and `block-user` in every scope (`SERVER_ONLY_CALL_MODERATION`) from the `user` and `call_member` roles of the `default` call type. `co_presenter` never held them. The owner applies it with `scripts/stream/ensure-call-type-grants.ts` after the deploy; the steps are in [Video implementation](../stream/05-video-implementation.md). The only thing that relied on those grants is the SDK participant list's Kick and Block items for the call creator; the server's own `kickUser` calls (`revokeCollaboratorAccess` and the conduct drive) run as the server client, which grants do not limit.

6. **Schema: two enum values, no table.** Every fact a removal needs is already recorded by an existing row, so no `ParticipantRemoval` table is added:

   | Fact                       | Recorded by                                                                            |
   | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
   | who was removed, from what | `SupportCase.requesterUserId`, `appointmentId`, the `APPOINTMENT` subject              |
   | who removed them           | the `CREATED` `SupportCaseEvent`'s `actorId`                                           |
   | why                        | the case description and first message (reason label and note)                         |
   | when                       | `SupportCase.createdAt`; the seat's own `CANCELLED` status                             |
   | the outcome                | `SupportCaseDecision` (`UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED`), or the `REMOVAL_REVIEW_LAPSED` event |

   `SupportCase.category` is a free string, so `CONDUCT_REMOVAL` needs no `SupportThreadCategory` value. Two values earn their place: `StreamRevocationKind.CONDUCT_CALL_BLOCK`, because no existing kind blocks or unblocks a call, and `SupportCaseEventKind.REMOVAL_REVIEW_LAPSED`, because no existing row records that the deadline passed, and the arm needs that row to fire once. The owner applies both with the release reconcile push of #2762; `prisma/sql/known-drift.json` lists the two statements.
   - Options not chosen: a `ParticipantRemoval` table with its own status enum (a second copy of the case's facts and outcome); a `SupportThreadCategory` value (the case column is not that enum).

7. **The attendee is told.** After the commit the removal sends the removed-seat notice (`notifySeatRemoved` in `lib/booking/seat-removed-notice.ts`) as a required `SEAT_RELEASED` notice, whatever the attendee's switches say. Its bell and email say the host "removed you", never "cancelled", carry the host's reason and note, the case number and the 72-hour decision line (`removalNote`, the bell's `removalNote` payload field), and open the case. Home lists the open case under Needs you, asking for the learner's side. Support's decision reaches them as a required `SEAT_RELEASED` bell too (`removalDecisionNotice`): an overturn says the seat is not given back, that a price-free seat can be registered for again or that support will help rejoin a paid one, and offers no appeal; an uphold names the 7-day appeal. The case's booking card leads to the learner's Cancelled list, since the booking page no longer admits a released seat, and the page does not read the booking thread the learner is refused. The refunds an overturn or a lapse sends announce themselves through the shared refund path, which says a refund still pending at the gateway has started (`refundStartedText`).

8. **Who may remove.** The plan owner and an accepted presenter-tier co-host (`CO_HOST`, `CO_INSTRUCTOR`), as the owner decided, plus the people who may already remove attendees on the roster: an active organisation operator with `catalog.manage` or `appointments.actForOrg.cancel`, and staff (`canRemoveForConduct` in `lib/api/participants/group-participants-route.ts`). Crew collaborators and attendees may not. The route checks the rule before it takes the appointment lock, and `removeAttendeeForConduct` checks it again on `tx` as the first step of its transaction, so a co-host revoked while the removal waited is refused with 403 `REMOVAL_NOT_ALLOWED` and nothing is written. Three doors share that rule and the one transaction: `POST /api/participants/{webinar|class}/[id]/remove` (the roster and the event chat) and `POST /api/meetings/[meetingId]/remove` (the live room's People tab).

9. **A second removal joins an undecided case, or closes a decided one and opens afresh (owner decision, 2026-11-19).** Once a lift and a rejoin can follow a removal, the same learner can be removed for conduct again while the first case is still unclosed, and the open-case key (`support_case_open_scope_key`) allows only one such case. The removal goes through in full (seat, mark, call block, earnings hold and SEAT row). If the unclosed case already has a decision, `openOrJoinConductRemovalCaseTx` closes it by compare-and-set on its status, category and `closedAt IS NULL` (the system's RESOLVED to CLOSED edge, ending its chat grants and releasing its payout holds as an auto-close does; holds a refund recommendation awaiting review, or closed money doors, still need stay on the closed case with an internal note, for staff to release there), keeps its history and decision, and opens a fresh case for the new removal, which gets its own verdict, appeal window, refund and 72-hour clock. If it has no decision, the removal joins it: a message to the learner with the host's reason, which also becomes the case summary (it says the review covers both removals and that the earlier one is in the first message), a support-case bell to the learner through the existing case-update notice saying the 72-hour review restarts (`stageCaseNotice`, `lib/support/case-notice.ts`), a `REOPENED` event with the remover as actor, and a deadline restarted from the new removal, with the status kept; that one review and its verdict cover both removals. Inside a joined case the lapse arm, the stands rule and an overturn's mark clearing read the latest removal as the deadline less 72 hours, so a lift that followed the first removal does not end the second. The removal takes its timestamp inside the event's seat lock, which both lift doors take too. Any other unique violation, or a lost close, answers 409 `REMOVAL_CONFLICT`. The participant routes report an unexpected failure through `reportSentryError`, which flushes before a Netlify function freezes; a bare `Sentry.captureException` there never reached Sentry.

10. **A case refunds only the order its removal ended (owner decision, 2026-11-20).** The removal records, inside its transaction, the seat's paid gateway order id (`Payment.paymentIntent`, the field a re-mint replaces) on the case's `CREATED` event, and a joined removal on its `REOPENED` event, as `fromValue` `removal-order:<order id>`; the latest such event is the order the case reviews, and a free seat or a seat with no payment records nothing. The marker is never shown: the staff case timeline and the entity trace read it as "Removal case opened" or "A new removal joined this review", and the learner's own case read drops it (`lib/support/case-event-copy.ts`). The lapse (`stampRemovalRefundTx`), the refund-first gate of an overturn or an appeal (`removalRefundOutstandingTx`) and support's recommendation, review and execution (`removalCaseRefundsPaymentTx`, checked by `quoteCaseRefund`, `recommendRefundTx` and `executeApprovedRefund` in `lib/support/case-actions.ts`, refusing with `PAYMENT_AFTER_REMOVAL`) act on the seat's payment only while it still carries that order, so the parked replacement order of a lapsed re-buy is never the case's. A case opened before the record falls back to the seat's history, as follows. A seat removed while its card order was unpaid can be lifted, and its learner can buy again once that order lapses: the checkout re-mints the same `Payment` row with a new gateway order and moves the old one to an `EXPIRED` tombstone, so the `Payment` id no longer names the order the removal ended. The checkout's revive to `HELD` writes no SEAT row, because a held seat is a checkout, but the capture that confirms the seat writes `registered` (`handlePaymentSuccess`), and a free rejoin writes `rejoined`; after a removal only a new checkout can lead there, so that row marks a new order on the seat. When one follows the case's latest removal, `stampRemovalRefundTx` (the lapse) and `removalRefundOutstandingTx` (the refund-first gate of an overturn or an appeal) owe nothing on the seat's current order; the fresh case of a later removal reviews it, and a late capture of the old order lands on its tombstone, which auto-refunds it. With no such row the current order is still the removed one, so an order unpaid at the removal that captures later is still refunded at the deadline. Worked example: Meera is removed at 10:00 Monday while her ₹1,000 order is unpaid; support upholds it on Friday, after the deadline, and lifts the block; her order expires, she buys again on Saturday and pays, and on Sunday the host removes her again. The Monday case lapses with nothing to refund, and the Sunday case alone owes the ₹1,000 if it is overturned or lapses.

Support decides a removal case as an appeal is decided: `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` (`assertDecisionFitsCase` in `lib/support/case-actions.ts`; the staff panel offers those two through `reviewsRemoval`). An appeal of that decision decided `OVERTURNED` overturns the removal too. An overturned removal cannot be superseded back to upheld, because its refund was sent and its block lifted. Since 2026-11-18 the removal also marks the seat (`removedByUserId`), which blocks a rejoin; an overturn clears the mark in `conductRemovalDecidedTx`, and staff can lift it at any time without a decision, so an upheld finding stays on the record while the learner may come back ([Free seat rejoin](2026-11-18-free-seat-rejoin.md), E4). "The removal stands" (`conductRemovalStands`) is read from the case: not overturned and no lift since, so a lifted removal no longer blocks the calls and a removal from before the mark existed still does. A removal case is a sensitive personal category (`ORG_SENSITIVE_PERSONAL_CATEGORIES`), so the host, a presenter and organisation views never read the attendee's side. It files under its own topic, "Conduct removal" (`removal` in `lib/support/case-topic.ts`), and the staff panel shows its decision deadline ("Removal decision due", from `statutoryDueAt`, only while the case is unclosed and undecided, `removalReviewDueAt`) and the requester's own seat status in the Booking drawer.

Tests: `__tests__/db/booking/conduct-removal.db.test.ts` (the one transaction and its rollback, the crash and the redrive, refund-first overturns of ₹6,000 and ₹4,000 seats, a lapse that lands before the admin, the unblock, the free and credit seats, the ₹750 remainder, the 72-hour arm firing once, a late uphold that still refunds, a first case that never refunds the re-minted order of a later sale, a late capture of the removed order that still does, support refused a recommendation and an approval against the later order, a parked replacement order the first case never reads as its own), `__tests__/db/booking/conduct-removal-races.db.test.ts` (overlapping deadline runs, 51 rooms, stale blocks, one report a run, a co-host revoked mid-removal), `__tests__/booking/conduct-removal-route.test.ts` (who may remove), `__tests__/events/conduct-removal-dialog.test.tsx` (what the in-room control sends, parsed by the route's schema).

## Limits that remain

- A paid seat removed twice on one case would reuse the first lapse's refund key (`conduct-removal:<caseId>:pay:<paymentId>`) at its second lapse, so a second refund on that payment is not stamped anew. The owner accepted this on 2026-11-19: a paid seat cannot rejoin through checkout on its own, so a second removal of one is unlikely.
- Two cases of a paid seat removed twice with no checkout between them read the same order, so an overturn or appeal of the closed first case refunds whatever is still unrefunded on it, which the fresh case would otherwise owe. That needs support to re-seat a paid seat by hand, because a paid seat does not rejoin through checkout. A checkout between the removals re-mints the order and binds each case to its own (decision 10).

- A case opened before the removal's order was recorded falls back to the seat's history, so for it a replacement order that captures after its seat hold lapsed and finds no place (refunded by the capture path, confirming no seat and writing no `registered` row) still reads as the case's own. The refund front door never sends more than the balance, so no extra money moves, but such a lapse may record a hand top-up and an earnings hold on an order the capture path is already refunding. Cases opened since record their order and are bound exactly.
- A `PENDING` refund counts against the balance when the overturn or the lapse is driven. If that refund fails later, its own door restores what it owed (`restoreOwedOnFailedRefund` for a support hand refund, the marker's attempt count for a marker drive); the conduct removal does not refund it again.
- A room that Postgres has stamped ended but Stream has not is not blocked; the seat revocation still removes the attendee from its members.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- **A lapse that refunded the seat's whole unrefunded balance** (until 2026-11-25): the marker was stamped with no amount, so a learner removed after three of eight sessions got the whole seat back and the host lost the pay for sessions taught because support missed its deadline. Superseded by the undelivered-sessions amount in decision 3.
- **Kick first, queue the retry on failure** (the first draft of this feature): the retry row was written only after Stream answered, so a crash in between lost it. Superseded by intents written in the removal's transaction.
- **Counting refund rows as the deadline remedy** (the first draft): superseded by the refundable balance and one keyed full-balance marker.
- **A REMOVAL actor that reopened a decided removal case** (2026-11-19): superseded the same day by closing a decided case and opening a fresh one, so a case never holds two verdicts; the transition table is back to its customer, staff and system edges.
