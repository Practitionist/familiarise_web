# ADR: A learner can rejoin a free event seat, removals block it until lifted, and every seat change is recorded

- **Status**: Accepted (owner decisions, 2026-11-18, two rounds). Built on the branch `feat/free-seat-rejoin-2849`.
- **Date**: 2026-11-18
- **Author**: Platform engineering
- **Related**: [Train 7 decisions](2026-11-17-train-7.md) (D1), [Conduct removal](2026-11-15-conduct-removal.md), [Event seat waitlist](2026-11-07-event-seat-waitlist.md), [Checkout and payment integration](../booking/10-checkout-payment-integration.md), [Cancellation flow](../booking/08-cancellation-flow.md), [Train 6 back office](2026-11-16-train-6-back-office.md)

## Context

Train 7 made a learner who left an event seat hear the honest refusal, `SEAT_REBUY_UNSUPPORTED` ("You left this event earlier…"), instead of being told they were still registered. That closed the door on everyone who left, including people who left a free webinar by mistake. Three gaps sat beside it:

- **Nothing recorded who removed a seat.** An organiser removal and a self-leave both ended `CANCELLED`, so a removed learner could not be told apart from one who left, and nothing could block a rejoin after a removal.
- **A participant move wrote no history.** `transitionParticipant` (`lib/booking/participants.ts`) changed the status and nothing else, so neither staff nor the organiser could see that a learner left on Monday and came back on Tuesday.
- **A free registration sent no booked notice.** The "you're registered" bell and email are staged by the capture path (`handlePaymentSuccess`), and a free seat has no capture.

A price-free leave leaves the `Payment` `SUCCEEDED` at 0 paise with no refund, no consumer invoice and no `booking:` journal, and the participant row `CANCELLED` (`lib/booking/seat-leave.ts`; `refundRemovedAttendeeSeat` answers `freeBooking`). So a rejoin needs no money change at all: the same payment can stand behind the revived seat.

## Decisions

1. **D1: only a price-free seat its holder left can be rejoined.** `priorSeatStanding` (`lib/payments/checkout/seats.ts`) answers `REJOINABLE` when the prior payment is `SUCCEEDED`, `isPriceFreePayment` (a `free_` intent with an amount and a list price of 0), every seat it funded is `CANCELLED`, none carries a removal mark, and the seat's latest SEAT row is `self_leave` or `block_lifted` (F3). Pricing, the payment write and the waitlist share it, so a rejoinable learner may also wait for a seat and take an offer. A credit-funded ₹0 seat and a paid seat keep "You left this event…"; their rebuy is #2867.
   - The commit revives the seat on its earlier payment. The webinar and class handlers read the prior payment before `recordParticipants` revives the row, so the classification is made on what the learner left, then passed to `writeCheckoutPayment`. A rejoin inserts no payment, re-mints nothing and writes no tombstone, and the commit skips the legs, the utilisation meter and the synchronous earnings, which the first registration already wrote.
   - Capacity stays the commit's own recount (`getWebinarCapacity` and `getClassCapacity` over `seatHoldingParticipant`, under `lockEventCheckout` and Serializable), so a full event answers `EVENT_FULL`, a rejoin and a new buyer race for the last seat like any two buyers, and only `CANCELLED` is revived (`reviveFromIn: ["CANCELLED"]`). The order itself must still be price-free: an event whose host has since set a price is refused with the left refusal, even when referral credit or a discount brings the order to ₹0, because the rejoin settles no credit. The price checked is the one the handler read inside the commit, under the checkout lock (`eventPricePaise` in `writeCheckoutPayment`), so a host's price edit that won the lock is seen even when the quote before it read ₹0. The checkout answers with the reused payment's id and its own stored intent.
   - Chat and the call follow the seat. A leave's removal from the event channel, the host DMs and the rooms belongs to its durable seat revoker (`revokeEndedSeatAccess` in `lib/stream/access-revocation.ts`); the participant `DELETE` makes no second removal of its own. After its vendor removals the revoker re-reads the seats it removed and syncs chat and the call again (`ensureChannelsForAppointment`) for anyone a rejoin re-admitted meanwhile, because that rejoin's grant may have landed before the removal did. The re-check and the re-sync keep to the drive's budget; whatever the budget cannot reach leaves the intent owed, and the sweep's re-drive re-grants an owed event learner the join gate admits again.
   - Worked example: Riya registers for Arjun's free "Intro to Python" webinar on 12 November and leaves the same afternoon. Her `Payment` stays `SUCCEEDED` at 0 paise and her seat is `CANCELLED`. On 13 November she registers again: the same participant row goes back to `CONFIRMED` on the same payment, she gets the booked notice again, and Arjun's roster shows "Left 12 Nov · rejoined 13 Nov" under her name.
   - Options not chosen: re-minting a fresh payment for the rejoin (a second ₹0 money row for one seat); rejoining credit or paid seats now (#2867).

2. **D2: a removal blocks a rejoin, and staff or the organiser can lift it.** `AppointmentParticipant.removedByUserId` records who removed the seat. `leaveEventSeat` sets it on an organiser removal (`isSelfLeave: false`) and `removeAttendeeForConduct` sets it, each inside its own transaction, on the same compare-and-set that releases the seat. A marked seat answers `REMOVED`, refused with `SEAT_REBUY_UNSUPPORTED` and "The organiser removed you from this event. Contact support if you think this was a mistake." That sentence covers both kinds; the refusal copy never told a conduct removal apart.
   - A removal outlasts the removed seat's checkout. `priorSeatStanding` answers `REMOVED` before it reads the payment status, so a card seat removed while its order was still open is refused in pricing, before order reuse, and its lapsed order is never re-minted. An unmarked released seat on an expired, failed or open order also reads `conductRemovalStands`, so a conduct removal from before the mark existed blocks the re-mint too. A checkout that re-mints a lapsed or failed order also takes over the seat that order left `HELD`, so a zero-amount or sponsored confirmation moves it to `CONFIRMED` with its `registered` SEAT row. The webinar and class handlers then stamp the seat's terms only on an unmarked row in the status the checkout set (`stampRecordedSeat`), and refuse the commit when the seat write did not reach it, so no payable order is written for a seat that stayed cancelled.
   - **Staff** lift either kind, or a seat with no recorded leave, through `POST /api/staff/appointments/[appointmentId]/seats/lift`, a `withOpsAction` door on the new surface `appointments.liftSeatRemoval` (ADMIN and STAFF, like `appointments.cancelForPlatform`), with step-up and a reason, run Serializable. Its audit row's `before` keeps the cleared `removedByUserId` and the kind of removal. The Ops actions panel lists removed attendees with **Allow back**. The lift and the list read only webinar and class seats, so the learner row of a cancelled 1:1 booking is never lifted or listed.
   - **The organiser** lifts a plain removal they made themselves, on their own event, through `POST /api/participants/{webinar|class}/[eventId]/allow-back`. A removal another organiser made, and a conduct removal that still stands, are refused with 403 `LIFT_NOT_ALLOWED`; the other organiser or support decides those. The door gives platform staff no bypass, so every staff lift goes through the staff door, with its step-up and its audit row.
   - Both doors clear the mark by compare-and-set, the staff door on `removedByUserId: { not: null }` and the organiser's on `removedByUserId` equal to the organiser, so a second lift answers 409 `REMOVAL_ALREADY_LIFTED` and writes nothing.

3. **D3 and E4: lifting only lifts the block, and it is not a verdict.** A lift clears the mark, writes a SEAT `block_lifted` row with its actor, and tells the learner. It never seats anyone, so a full event still says sold out. It never writes or supersedes a `SupportCaseDecision`: an `UPHELD` finding stays true in the record, and staff may lift at any time, with no appeal window.
   - "The removal stands" is read from the case (F1, owner decision of round 3): `conductRemovalStands` (`lib/support/conduct-removal-state.ts`) is true while the learner's latest `CONDUCT_REMOVAL` case has no `OVERTURNED` decision in force and no `block_lifted` SEAT row follows it, mark or no mark. So a removal from before the mark existed still holds its call block and its refusal, and a later plain removal never revives an old conduct block. The call block follows: a staff lift of a conduct removal writes a fresh `CONDUCT_CALL_BLOCK` intent in its transaction, and the drive, which reads the direction from Postgres, unblocks the learner.
   - An `OVERTURNED` decision from the case panel lifts the mark too, in `conductRemovalDecidedTx`, through the same `clearRemovalMarkTx`, so both doors agree. It clears only the mark its own removal set: when a `block_lifted` row already follows the removal case, the seat's mark belongs to a later plain removal and stays. Its notice then points the learner to support rather than promising a free re-registration the later removal still blocks. `removalDecisionNotice` now says the learner can register for the event again (price-free) or that support will help them rejoin (otherwise).
   - A second conduct removal joins the first one's case while that case is unclosed (owner decision, 2026-11-19). The open-case key (`support_case_open_scope_key` in `prisma/sql/check-constraints.sql`) allows one unclosed case per learner, event and category, and before a lift and a rejoin were possible no learner could be removed twice. The removal still releases the seat, sets the mark, writes the call block, holds the earnings and records its SEAT row. `openOrJoinConductRemovalCaseTx` (`lib/support/conduct-removal-case.ts`) then posts the new removal to the learner as a case message, logs it as a `REOPENED` event with the remover as actor and the time, and restarts the review deadline (`statutoryDueAt` = this removal + 72 hours). A case that was settled (RESOLVED, or ESCALATED) goes back to OPEN for the new review (owner decision, 2026-11-19): the join moves it through the REMOVAL actor of `CASE_TRANSITIONS`, by compare-and-set on its prior status and category, decided or not, and applies the reopen clock (`reopenedClockPatch`). Its decisions stay in force, and a customer's or staff reopen of a decided case is still refused. Any other unique violation on the removal answers 409 `REMOVAL_CONFLICT`, never a 500.
   - Removals and lifts are ordered by time. The case's latest removal is its deadline less the 72-hour window (`removalReviewStart`). `conductRemovalStands` is true unless a `block_lifted` SEAT row, or an `OVERTURNED` decision in force, came after that latest removal; the overturn's mark clearing (`liftedSinceRemoval`) and the 72-hour lapse (`lapsedCaseIds`, which counts only a lapse event or a decision since the latest removal) read the same time. So a lift or an overturn of the first removal never ends the second.
   - Worked example: Arjun removes Daniel for conduct on 18 November, support upholds it, and a support agent lifts the block that evening. Daniel registers again on 19 November and Arjun removes him for conduct again. His first case stays the one case: it goes back from RESOLVED to OPEN, gains the new message and a `REOPENED` event naming Arjun, its deadline moves to 22 November, the `UPHELD` decision stays in force, and Daniel's rejoin is refused until the new removal is overturned or lifted.
   - Worked example: Arjun removes Riya for conduct on 14 November and support upholds it on 15 November. On 20 November a support agent lifts the block after Riya apologises. The `UPHELD` decision stays in force, the call block is lifted, and Riya registers again while seats are left.

4. **E5: a paid or credit-funded seat can be lifted, with an honest notice.** The learner's bell, staged in the lift's transaction through the existing `event-seat-opened` event (no new Novu workflow), says "You can register for <event> again." for a price-free seat and "Support will help you rejoin <event>." for any other. The staff door's dialog says a paid seat cannot be bought again on its own yet. The checkout refusal for a lifted paid seat stays `SEAT_REBUY_UNSUPPORTED`. The owner confirmed on 2026-11-18 that the bell is not a required notice, so a learner who switched appointment bells off does not receive it; the template's new `rejoinText` branch reaches production only after the owner's Novu sync.
   - Worked example: Arjun removes Dev from his ₹500 class and the seat is refunded in full. Support lifts the block; Dev is told support will help him rejoin, and a checkout still answers "Your earlier seat here was refunded…".

5. **D4: every free registration sends the booked notice, once per join.** After a zero-amount webinar or class seat commits, `notifyFreeSeatRegistered` (`lib/payments/checkout/free-seat-notice.ts`) stages the existing booked bell and email (`stageBookedBells`, `stageBookedEmails`) for the learner, the host and the presenters, keyed `seat-registered:<participantId>:<joinId>`, so a rejoin is not swallowed by the first registration's key. `joinId` is the id of the `registered` or `rejoined` SEAT row the commit wrote, read in the commit (`readSeatJoin`) and carried out of checkout, so the notice is staged for the join that committed even when a removal releases the seat before the staging runs. The staging claims the key once (`IdempotencyRecord` scope `seat-registered`) in the transaction that stages the bell and the email, so a re-stage of one join sends neither twice. Paid seats keep the capture path's notice and get no second one. The owner confirmed on 2026-11-18 that a credit-funded ₹0 seat gets the notice too, because no zero-amount seat passes through a capture.

6. **D5 and E3: every event seat change writes a SEAT history row.** `BookingHistoryEntity` gains `SEAT`. A SEAT row has `entityId` = the participant id, the `appointmentId`, the participant statuses as `fromStatus` and `toStatus`, the `actorUserId`, and one reason from the closed list `SeatHistoryReasonSchema` (`lib/booking/seat-history.ts`): `registered`, `self_leave`, `organiser_removal`, `conduct_removal`, `block_lifted`, `rejoined`, `refunded`.
   - Only learner seats on webinars and classes are recorded. The reason is passed only by event callers, so a 1:1 path writes nothing and pays nothing. When a caller's compare-and-set pins one from-status, the helper skips the pre-read; with several, its pre-read keeps only event learner seats, so a refund that ends a 1:1 seat still writes none.
   - A `HELD` seat is a checkout, not a registration, so it writes nothing until its capture (`registered`) or its free confirmation.
   - Staff read it in the booking timeline (`getBookingTimeline`, `AppointmentTimeline`) with the seat holder and the actor. The organiser reads a short trail on each attendee row and on the Removed attendees list. The learner reads their own trail on their booking page (`GET /api/appointments/[appointmentId]/seat-trail`). Both read the same words from `seatTrailText` (`lib/booking/seat-trail-copy.ts`): each day in the reader's own zone, the line ending with that zone's label ("Left 12 Nov · rejoined 13 Nov (IST)"), and a trail of more than three steps showing its last three after a count ("3 earlier changes, then …"), with every step in the hover title. Staff read India time, and a lift reads as its reason ("Allowed back: may register again"), never "Cancelled → Cancelled".
   - Statement cost on the three hot paths: a free first registration adds one in the commit, the join read for its notice (the insert's `createManyAndReturn` replaces `createMany`, and its history insert replaces the revive's update, which is skipped for a fresh row), and its notice transaction adds the claim; a self-leave and an organiser removal add one (the history insert; the seat's status is already read, so it pins the from-status). A rejoin adds the revive's history and the join read and skips the payment insert. A re-buy on a lapsed, failed or open order whose seat is released and unmarked adds the conduct case read.

7. **E1: one database serves every deployment, so seat rows stay out of booking-level reads.** `lib/booking/history-entities.ts` splits by meaning: `BOOKING_HISTORY_ENTITIES` (the seven booking-level entities) serves the document retention match and the document date rule, so one attendee leaving never reads as the booking ending; `TIMELINE_HISTORY_ENTITIES` adds `SEAT` for the staff timeline. The guard release that names the seven entities in every deployed reader ships to prod before the owner pushes `SEAT`, because Prisma throws on an enum value its client lacks.

## Schema

- `AppointmentParticipant.removedByUserId String?`, a relation to `User` (`AppointmentParticipantRemovedBy`, `onDelete: Restrict` since round 3; it shipped as `SetNull`) with an index.
- `enum BookingHistoryEntity` gains `SEAT`, placed last.
- No sidecar changes: no `CHECK`, partial unique index or trigger reads either column, and `booking_status_history_immutable` already covers SEAT rows.

## Limits that remain

- A rejoin does not reuse the first registration's checkout idempotency key, so a double-submitted rejoin answers `ALREADY_REGISTERED` the second time instead of replaying the first answer.
- Event seat moves outside the seven reasons (a whole-event cancel, a lapsed hold, attendance) write no SEAT row.
- A seat released before seat history was kept carries no mark and no SEAT row, so nothing records whether its holder left or was removed. F3 (owner decision of round 3) keeps every such seat closed by a rule in code, with no data write: a left price-free seat is rejoinable only when its latest SEAT row is `self_leave` or `block_lifted`, so a seat with no SEAT row (37 on the live database on 2026-11-18), or one released by a door that writes none, answers the neutral "You left this event…" refusal. Staff open it through the same lift door, which writes `block_lifted` with its actor and records the kind `LEGACY` in the audit row's `before` (`CONDUCT` when an unmarked conduct removal still stands); the organiser's Allow back refuses it with `LIFT_NOT_ALLOWED`. The staff Ops panel lists such free seats as "left with no recorded leave". It also lists an unmarked seat, paid or free, whose learner's conduct removal still stands, as a conduct removal, so staff can lift it.
- The removal mark's foreign key is `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (F2), so a remover's account cannot be hard-deleted while a mark they set stands. `DELETE /api/user/[id]` counts those marks as retained history and erases the account in place (the user row stays, pseudonymised), as it does for a held seat; a mark written between that check and the delete answers 409 `ERASURE_BLOCKED_RETAINED_RECORDS`, never a raw foreign-key 500. The owner applies the constraint change with `temp/2849-fk-restrict.sql`.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- **Every left seat refused alike** (Train 7 D1): superseded for price-free seats by D1 here; paid and credit seats keep it until #2867.
- **"The removal stands" read only from the support decision**: superseded by E4, which also needs the seat's mark, so a lift ends the block without a verdict.
