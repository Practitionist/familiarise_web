# ADR: Retention windows for every log-like and audit table

- **Status**: Accepted on 2026-11-01 by the owner (Kaustav). Ships in the pull request that adds this record.
- **Date**: 2026-11-01
- **Amended**: 2026-11-02 by the owner: `EmailEvent` and `WebhookEvent` use calendar-year cutoffs, and `archive-webhook-events` deletes in whole batches (section 4). Also 2026-11-02: the CERT-In session trail moves to a `SessionAccessLog` row per session, and expired `Session` rows are deleted at expiry again; an app revocation inside a transaction stamps the log only after that transaction commits (section 7, issue #2506).
- **Author**: Platform engineering
- **Related**: [DPDP and privacy](../compliance/02-dpdp-and-privacy.md#retention-windows) (the retention table), [Job machinery catalog](../maintenance/11-job-machinery-catalog.md), [The ticker skips targets that have no work](2026-11-01-ticker-skip-idle-targets.md), [Admin background runs are durable requests](2026-11-01-admin-run-requests.md), issue #2496 (retention audit)

## Context

Before this change, most log-like tables either had no prune at all (`OpsActionLog`, `ActivityLog`, `BookingStatusHistory`, `LedgerReconciliationReport`, `OrgDataExportJob`, anonymous `CookiePreference` rows, `MeetingPresence`, `MeetingAttendance`) or a window chosen for disk rather than for the law (`SystemJobExecution` 90 days, `EmailEvent` 30 days, `WebhookEvent` 30 or 90 days, expired `Session` rows deleted at expiry). The audit measured the live database at 184 MB, so disk is not the driver for any table except `SystemJobExecution`. For every other table the driver is data minimisation and the legal duty to keep evidence.

The windows below are the owner's decision. Each has a driver. The full table, which is the single source, is in [DPDP and privacy, Retention windows](../compliance/02-dpdp-and-privacy.md#retention-windows) and is repeated for agents in the compliance skill.

## Decision

### 1. Audit tables

| Data                            | Window                                | Driver                                                                                                                                                                                | Enforced by                                                |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `OrgAuditLog`, money categories | 8 years                               | Companies Act 2013 s.128(5) keeps books for eight financial years. The categories are INVOICE, PAYOUT, WALLET, CONTRACT and CONSENT (was 7 years).                                    | `prune-audit-logs` (`scripts/cleanup/prune-audit-logs.ts`) |
| `OrgAuditLog`, other categories | 2 years                               | DPDP Rules 2025 Rule 8(3) one-year floor for logs of processing, plus a year of margin.                                                                                               | `prune-audit-logs`                                         |
| `OpsActionLog`, money surfaces  | 8 years                               | The same books horizon: a refund, payout or hold decision is audit evidence for the accounts.                                                                                         | `prune-audit-logs`, through `OPS_SURFACE_WINDOW`           |
| `OpsActionLog`, other surfaces  | 2 years                               | Rule 8(3) floor plus margin.                                                                                                                                                          | `prune-audit-logs`                                         |
| `OpsActionProposal`             | 8 years                               | Only the second-approver money doors open proposals, so every proposal is money evidence.                                                                                             | `prune-audit-logs`                                         |
| `BookingStatusHistory`          | 3 years after the booking is terminal | Consumer Protection Act 2019 s.69 (2 years to complain) and Limitation Act 1963 Articles 55 and 113 (3 years to sue). DPDP s.17(1)(a) allows keeping data to establish a legal claim. | `prune-audit-logs`, through `bookingHistoryPruneWhere`     |
| `LedgerReconciliationReport`    | 2 years by `runAt`                    | A report is a derived check, not the ledger. The ledger itself is never pruned.                                                                                                       | `prune-audit-logs`                                         |

The money-audit horizon of eight years is the owner's choice, drawn from Companies Act s.128(5). It is longer than CGST Act s.36 (72 months) and Income-tax Rules 2026 Rule 46(9) (seven tax years). **It should be confirmed with the chartered accountant.** If the CA names a shorter or longer period, change the `MONEY_AUDIT_YEARS` constant in `scripts/cleanup/prune-audit-logs.ts` and, in the same change, the three written copies of the window: `.claude/skills/compliance/references/retention-windows.md`, the retention table in `docs/compliance/02-dpdp-and-privacy.md` and this record.

The year-based windows are UTC calendar years, not N times 365 days: `yearsBefore` (`lib/cron/retention-cutoff.ts`) subtracts N from the UTC year, so the 8-, 2-, 3- and 2-year audit windows and the one-year `EmailEvent` and `WebhookEvent` windows keep their leap days. The day-based windows elsewhere (14 and 180 days, 365 days for `OrgDataExportJob`, 3 times 365 days for meeting presence) are plain day counts.

An `OpsActionLog` row's `surface` decides its window. `OPS_SURFACE_WINDOW` is a `Record<BackofficeSurface, "money" | "other">`, so adding a new back-office surface without classifying it is a compile error. A row whose `surface` string is not a known surface (a surface removed from the code later) keeps the 8-year window, because the safe default for an unclassified action is the longer one.

**BookingStatusHistory never touches a live booking.** A history row is selected only when it is linked to an appointment, and every parent of that appointment (consultation, subscription, webinar, class and trial) is terminal, and the parents and the appointment were last updated more than three years ago, and no occurrence of the appointment ended in the last three years. Rows with no `appointmentId` are never selected. The reason is that `readRescheduleOrigin` (`lib/booking/reschedule-restore.ts`) reads a live booking's `PENDING` history row at any age to restore a rescheduled session, so an age-only rule would break a long-running subscription's reschedule.

**CERT-In does not require the logs to stay in India.** The CERT-In FAQs of May 2022, question 35, say: "Is it required to store copy of logs in India only? Ans.: The logs may be stored outside India also as long as the obligation to produce logs to CERT-In is adhered to by the entities in a reasonable time." The 180-day period binds. The location does not. Question 36, which concerns records of financial transactions, is a separate point.

### 3. State-bearing `SystemEvent` rows are kept

A `SystemEvent` row is usually a finding that can age out. Some rows are state: the code reads "the newest event for this key" to decide what to do next. Pruning those at 400 days would silently flip the state back (a frozen wallet would thaw; a once-only alert would fire again). `prune-system-events` therefore keeps the newest event per `(correlationId, category)` for every correlation id starting with one of `STATE_BEARING_EVENT_PREFIXES`: `wallet-freeze:`, `class-reliability:`, `partial-credit:`, `partial-credit-closed:`, `held-paid-seat:`, `orphaned-payment:`, `orphan-escrow:`, `double-booking:`, `double-booking-blocked:` and `capture-unseated-seat:`.

The readers that depend on this are `isWalletFrozen` and `latestDriftFreezeIds` (`lib/payments/wallet-freeze.ts`), class reliability (`lib/booking/class-sessions.ts`, `lib/backoffice/class-series-read.ts` and the class-series reliability route), the needs-human lists (`lib/backoffice/needs-human.ts`), and the once-only guards in `scripts/alerts/alert-orphaned-payments.ts`, `scripts/payments/reconcile-orphaned-confirmations.ts`, `scripts/appointments/reconcile-occurrence-availability.ts`, `scripts/appointments/settle-cancelled-sessions.ts` and `lib/payments/webhooks/handlers.ts`. Expert strikes (`lib/booking/expert-reliability.ts`) read a 90-day window, standing findings a 24-hour window, and the unaccrued-earnings page a per-day key, so they need no exemption.

### 4. Ticker capacity: no new target

`MAX_TARGETS_PER_TICK` is 12 and the busiest tick holds 11, so there is room for one more target at most. Every new prune is folded into an existing job instead. The audit-class tables (tens of rows a month) go into `prune-audit-logs`, which is daily with bounded 500-row pages. Telemetry and secondary tables go into `prune-system-job-executions`, which runs every 30 minutes at +25 inside the 20:00 to 23:30 UTC window, seven runs a day, with a 16-second work budget under the ticker's 24-second timeout.

**Budget arithmetic for `SystemJobExecution`.** Inflow is about 3,100 run rows a day plus 288 tick-claim rows a day, so about 3,400 a day. The old capacity was 7 runs times 500 rows, 3,500 a day: barely above inflow and no margin to drain a backlog. The new run drains run history with raw `DELETE ... WHERE id IN (SELECT ... ORDER BY startedAt LIMIT 1000)` batches until a batch comes back short or the shared deadline passes. A 2,000-row candidate read measured 102 ms on the live table (`EXPLAIN ANALYZE`) and a statement round trip costs about 62 ms, so a 1,000-row batch costs roughly 0.15 to 0.3 seconds. After about 25 bounded page statements (about 1.6 seconds) a run has about 14 of its 16 seconds left: about 45 batches, 45,000 rows a run, about 300,000 a day, which is roughly 90 times inflow. At a pessimistic one second per batch it is still 14,000 a run and 98,000 a day, about 29 times inflow. The overrun past the deadline is at most one statement, so a run ends well inside the 24-second ticker timeout and Netlify's roughly 26-second function limit.

**The existing backlog drains in the first evening.** The live table held 175,031 rows (84 MB), about 150,000 of them already older than 14 days and almost all no-op. At 45,000 rows a run that is about three runs. Locally, 2,498 no-op rows and one aged row drained in 136 ms. The 180-day tier runs only once the no-op tier is drained, so a backlog never starves the cheap deletes.

**Budget arithmetic for `archive-webhook-events`.** The job keeps its weekly Sunday slot (00:20 UTC) and its 20-second ticker timeout, so it adds no target. It used to delete one 100-row page per phase: at most 300 `WebhookEvent` rows a week, about 43 a day. It now runs whole `DELETE ... WHERE id IN (SELECT ... ORDER BY "receivedAt" LIMIT 2000)` batches, terminal outbound deliveries first and then processed or failed events, until a batch comes back short or 16 seconds have passed since the request began. The candidate read walks the `receivedAt` index (`EXPLAIN ANALYZE` on the live table read all 245 rows in 0.3 ms), a row carries about 1.3 KB of payload for Stream and 0.5 KB for Razorpay, and a statement round trip costs about 62 ms, so a 2,000-row batch costs roughly 0.2 to 0.4 seconds. With about 14 of the 16 seconds left after the lease and the delivery phase, that is about 35 batches: 70,000 rows a week, 10,000 a day. At a pessimistic one second per batch it is still 28,000 a week, 4,000 a day. Inflow today is about 5 events a day (245 rows since 18 September 2026, 213 of them from Stream); 4,000 a day covers, for example, 500 sessions a day at six Stream events each plus 300 payments at three Razorpay events each. The overrun past the budget is at most one statement, so a run ends inside the 20-second timeout and Netlify's roughly 26-second limit. A run that cannot finish reports `hasMore`, and the rest waits for the next Sunday.

**What counts as a no-op run.** A run is deleted at 14 days only when it is `COMPLETED`, has zero or null items and errors, has no `errorLog`, was not triggered by `admin:*`, has no `checkpoint` key in its `result`, has no non-empty `idle` array (a tick-claim row that recorded idle skips is kept for the full 180 days), and is not the newest `COMPLETED` row of its job. The newest row is kept so "when did this job last succeed" always has an answer.

### 5. `SupportCaseEvent` gets no separate prune

Events live and die with their case. `purgeEligibleWhere` in `lib/support/evidence-retention.ts` selects cases whose evidence is not yet purged with `events: { none: { kind: "EVIDENCE_PURGED" } }`. Deleting events on their own would delete that marker and make the purge re-run on the case. The personal free text on a case is scrubbed 180 days after it closes, which is the minimisation that matters.

### 6. `ActivityLog` is dropped

The `ActivityLog` model, the `ActivityType` enum, `lib/activity/` and every writer are deleted. The table had no reader (`getRecentActivities` was dead code), it copied `actorName` and `actorImage` into every row where erasure never scrubbed them, and `BookingStatusHistory` already records the same transitions. The code is gone in this change. The database table is dropped later. Production still runs the previous code, which calls `logActivity` and reports every failed insert to Sentry, so the three drop statements (the foreign key, `DROP TABLE "ActivityLog"` and `DROP TYPE "ActivityType"`) sit under `knownDivergences` in `prisma/sql/known-drift.json` (expires 2026-12-31): the drift check tolerates them and the `db:push` preflight refuses them. Once the release carrying this change is on production, move the three entries to `destructiveStatementsAllowed` and push. The live database is also ahead of `dev` with the digital-products tables and `RecordingPurchase.reconciledAt`, which the preflight refuses to drop, so the digital-products change must merge first.

### 7. The session access log (amended 2026-11-02)

**Every session leaves a 180-day record, however it ends.** `SessionAccessLog` (`prisma/schema.prisma`, table `session_access_logs`) holds one row per session: `sessionId` (unique, no foreign key, so the row outlives the `Session`), a nullable `userId`, the raw `ipAddress` and `userAgent`, `startedAt`, `endedAt` and `endReason`. CERT-In direction (iv) needs the source IP to be producible, so the start write stores the full client IP the middleware stamped (`x-familiarise-client-ip`, `lib/request-ip.ts`): Better Auth masks an IPv6 `Session.ipAddress` to its /64 rate-limit key. Only a row created at end time, for a session whose start was never logged, falls back to the `Session` value. Failed sign-ins are not logged here. All writes live in `lib/auth/session-access-log.ts`.

| How the session ends                                                                                                                                       | Who deletes the `Session` row                                                                                                                                 | How the end is stamped                                                              | `endReason`                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sign-in (any method)                                                                                                                                       | -                                                                                                                                                             | `databaseHooks.session.create.after` inserts the row                                | -                                                              |
| Sign-out                                                                                                                                                   | Better Auth, path `/sign-out`                                                                                                                                 | `databaseHooks.session.delete.after`                                                | `SIGN_OUT`                                                     |
| A browser presents an expired cookie                                                                                                                       | Better Auth's `getSession`, path `/get-session`                                                                                                               | `delete.after`; `endedAt` is the row's `expiresAt`                                  | `EXPIRED_COOKIE`                                               |
| Better Auth's own revocations (password reset, change-password, 2FA re-issue, the session a pending 2FA challenge withholds, admin plugin ban, SSO logout) | Better Auth, any other path                                                                                                                                   | `delete.after`                                                                      | `REVOKED`                                                      |
| The app's revocations                                                                                                                                      | `lib/auth/session-revoke.ts` (device and staff revoke, ban, erasure, account deletion, SSO enforcement, a superseded session, 2FA enrolment, staff 2FA reset) | `endSessions` reads and deletes; the caller runs `stampSessionEnds` after commit    | `REVOKED`; `EXPIRED` when the operator lifetime policy ends it |
| Natural expiry                                                                                                                                             | `auth-tokens` (`lib/auth/cleanup-auth-tokens.ts`)                                                                                                             | deleted, then stamped in the same transaction; a failed stamp rolls back the delete | `EXPIRED`, `endedAt` is `expiresAt`                            |
| A Better Auth delete with no endpoint context                                                                                                              | Better Auth                                                                                                                                                   | `delete.after`                                                                      | `UNKNOWN`                                                      |

The end write is an `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT ("sessionId") DO UPDATE ... WHERE "endedAt" IS NULL` statement per 1,000 sessions (8 bind parameters a row, well under Postgres's 65,535), so a whole-domain SSO sweep never overflows one statement; any failed chunk makes the call report failure. It creates the row when the start was never logged (a session opened before this change), and the first stamp wins. A row it creates links `userId` only to an account that still exists and is not erased, read with `FOR SHARE` on the `users` row: an erasure locks that row before it detaches the log, so a stamp racing the erasure either commits first and is detached, or waits and writes `NULL`. A row already past `expiresAt` is stamped `EXPIRED` with `endedAt = expiresAt` whichever path deletes it, except Better Auth's expired-cookie delete, which keeps `EXPIRED_COOKIE`.

**Writes never block authentication.** A failed write is caught, logged and reported to Sentry once per process; sign-in, sign-out and revocation carry on. The cost is one insert on sign-in and one statement on sign-out (about 62 ms each on Netlify); an app revocation adds one read and one write. `deferSessionRefresh` stays off and no refresh or login behaviour changes.

**Logging never blocks erasure or a ban.** Inside a transaction, a revocation only reads the `Session` rows it is about to delete and deletes them; it never touches `session_access_logs`. The caller hands the returned `ended` batches to `stampSessionEnds` after the transaction commits, which writes on the global client. A Postgres error inside a transaction aborts the whole transaction even when the application catches it, so a stamp written there could have rolled back an erasure or a ban. The transactional callers are erasure (`scrubUser`), the account hard delete, the moderation ban and suspension (the stamp runs first in `applyBestEffortEffects`), the staff 2FA reset (`afterCommit`), the SSO enforcement sweeps (`revokeEnforcedDomainSessions`, from four routes) and an org member's suspension or removal (`revokeOrgManagedUserSessions`). Callers outside a transaction stamp straight after the delete with the same call. A failed stamp returns the count of unstamped sessions and reports through the same once-per-process Sentry guard; the row stays open, and the prune below ages it from its start.

**Until the owner applies the table, nothing breaks.** `sessionAccessLogReady` asks Postgres `to_regclass('session_access_logs')`, which cannot fail on a missing table, so the guard is safe inside the erasure and ban transactions, and caches a positive answer per process. While the table is missing, the writes are skipped, the export lists no rows, and `auth-tokens` leaves expired sessions in place, so the old trail survives until the table exists.

**Retention.** `auth-tokens` deletes a row 180 days after `endedAt`. A row never stamped (its end write failed, or a session created between an app revocation's read and its delete) is deleted 180 days after `startedAt` plus one 30-day session length (`SESSION_EXPIRES_IN_SECONDS`). A sliding session alive longer than that loses its start row early, and its end write recreates the full row from the `Session` when it ends. Both prunes drain 100-row pages until a page comes back short or the shared 16-second budget is spent, inside the daily `auth-tokens` run, so there is no new ticker target.

**`Session` rows go back to being deleted at expiry.** Once the access log carries the trail, a second 180-day copy of the IP in `Session` adds nothing, and an expired `Session` row kept for 180 days was a row every device list, admin endpoint and erasure had to reason about. `auth-tokens` now deletes each expired session and stamps it `EXPIRED` in one transaction. The delete re-checks `expiresAt < now`, so a session Better Auth's `get-session` refreshed after the read (it reads the row, then updates `expiresAt` by token without an expiry guard) survives, and only the rows the delete removed are stamped; a failed stamp rolls the delete back for the next run. That stamp reads the user row without a lock, because it already holds the deleted sessions' row locks and erasure deletes those sessions before it detaches; the previous keep was on `dev` only and never reached production. The operator list's "last active" time (`app/api/admin/team/members/route.ts`) reads the newest `updatedAt` of the operator's remaining sessions, as it did before the keep.

**Erasure keeps the log and unlinks it.** `scrubUser` (`lib/compliance/erasure/scrub-user.ts`) and the account hard delete (`app/api/user/[id]/route.ts`) delete the sessions and set `userId` to null on the user's existing access-log rows inside the transaction. After it commits they stamp the ended sessions with `forgetUser`, so a row the stamp has to create carries no `userId` either. The hard delete removes the `users` row before it detaches, so the share lock above orders it the same way. The IP, user agent and times stay until the 180-day prune: keeping them is a legal obligation under DPDP Act s.17 and the CERT-In Directions.

**The DPDP export includes it.** `app/api/user/privacy/export/route.ts` returns the user's rows (start, end, end reason, IP address, user agent; newest 500) as `personalDataSummary.activitySummary.sessionAccessLog`, sets `sessionAccessLogTruncated` when older rows exist, and states both under `statutoryRetentionAndRightsInfo`.

### 8. Never pruned

Invoices, credit notes, the ledger, `Payment`, `Refund` and TDS records are never pruned. They are the books (Companies Act s.128(5), CGST Act s.36, Income-tax Rules 2026 Rule 46) and Razorpay's terms, clause 2.18, treat transaction records as ten-year records. `ConsentArtifact` rows are held 7 years (`auditRetainedUntil`) and the consent sweeper only counts them.

### 9. Fraud signals (added 2026-11-09)

The hashed request and payer signals used for fraud review had no clock. The owner decided they expire, inside the existing `prune-audit-logs` job rather than a new one (a fifth ticker target would breach `MAX_TARGETS_PER_TICK`, and the job already runs once a day with bounded pages):

| Data                                             | Window                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Driver                             | Enforced by                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| `Referral.ipHash`, `Referral.userAgent`          | 180 days after the referral is terminal (`VESTED` by `vestedAt`; `VOID` and the legacy `REWARDED`, `EXPIRED`, `FRAUDULENT` by `updatedAt`, since they record no timestamp), or 180 days after `createdAt` for a referral that never settles (`SIGNED_UP`, `QUALIFIED`, `QUALIFYING`, `HELD`); also nulled by `scrubUser` | Review evidence only; minimisation | `prune-audit-logs` (`referralSignalPruneWhere`) |
| `Payment.payerVpaHash`, `Payment.payerPhoneHash` | 2 years after `capturedAt` (`createdAt` when null); kept through erasure until then                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Self-referral and chargeback basis | `prune-audit-logs` (`paymentSignalPruneWhere`)  |

The `Payment` row itself is still never pruned; only these two columns are cleared. The constants and predicates live in `lib/compliance/signal-retention.ts`.

## Options considered

| Option                                      | Verdict      | Reason                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Fold every new prune into two existing jobs | **Selected** | The ticker is at 11 of 12 targets on its busiest tick; the new volumes are tiny.                             |
| One new ticker target per table             | Rejected     | It would push the busiest tick over the cap and add an invocation for tables that gain tens of rows a month. |
| Age-only rule for `BookingStatusHistory`    | Rejected     | It would delete the `PENDING` row a live booking's reschedule restore reads.                                 |

| A separate prune for `SupportCaseEvent`     | Rejected     | It would delete the `EVIDENCE_PURGED` marker and re-trigger the evidence purge.                              |
| Keep `ActivityLog` with a one-year prune    | Rejected     | It has no reader and holds copied personal data that erasure never scrubbed.                                 |
| Keep expired `Session` rows for 180 days    | Superseded   | The access log covers every session end; a kept row covered only sessions that lapsed unseen.                |
| `deferSessionRefresh` to keep cookie rows   | Rejected     | It changes login behaviour; the `delete.after` hook records the expired-cookie delete instead.               |

## Consequences

- **Schema.** Removing the `ActivityLog` model leaves the table in the database until the release is on production and the owner moves its drop statements into `destructiveStatementsAllowed`. Until then production's previous code still writes it; nothing on `dev` reads or writes it.
- **First run after deploy.** The first evening's `prune-system-job-executions` runs delete the backlog described above.
- **Storage.** Longer windows (`SystemJobExecution` 180 days, `EmailEvent` and `WebhookEvent` 1 year (calendar)) grow the tables. No-op rows, the bulk of the inflow, still go after 14 days, and the audit measured the whole database at 184 MB.
- **Confirm with the CA.** The 8-year money-audit horizon is the owner's choice and awaits the chartered accountant's confirmation.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- A 7-year window for money `OrgAuditLog` categories, a 90-day `SystemJobExecution` window, a 30-day `EmailEvent` window and 30- or 90-day `WebhookEvent` windows are superseded by the table above. Delete any doc or test that still states them.
- A 365-day cutoff for `EmailEvent` and `WebhookEvent`, a day short of a year across a leap day, and the 100-row page per phase in `archive-webhook-events` are superseded by calendar-year cutoffs and whole-batch deletes.
- Stamping an app revocation inside the caller's transaction (the first version of the 2026-11-02 amendment) is superseded by the post-commit `stampSessionEnds`: a log write that failed there aborted the erasure or ban with it. Do not stamp inside an erasure, ban or revocation transaction; the expiry prune is the one in-transaction stamp, because rolling its delete back is the intended outcome.
- Keeping expired `Session` rows for 180 days after `expiresAt` (the first version of this record) is superseded by `SessionAccessLog`. Sessions are deleted at expiry again; do not restore the 180-day `Session` cutoff in `cleanup-auth-tokens.ts`.
- The `ActivityLog` table, `ActivityType` enum and `lib/activity/` are gone. Do not reintroduce a per-user activity feed table; `BookingStatusHistory` is the record of booking transitions.
- The wording "180 days inside India" for CERT-In logs was wrong and is corrected in the compliance docs. Do not restore it.
