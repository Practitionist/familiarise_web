# ADR: The money doors after the bug hunt — the recover door, late wallet top-ups, credit restoration limits and second-tab checkout

- **Status**: Accepted (owner decisions, 2026-11-12). Every decision below is **built** on the branch that carries this record, except decision 9, which was considered and **not built**.
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Author**: Platform engineering
- **Related**: [Capture confirmation and webhooks](../finance/10-money-in/02-capture-confirmation-and-webhooks.md), [Checkout and pricing](../finance/10-money-in/01-checkout-and-pricing.md), [Wallet and top-ups](../finance/50-b2b/02-wallet-and-top-ups.md), [Admin money doors](../finance/70-operations/02-admin-money-doors.md), [Money remedies](../support/05-money-remedies.md), [Referral credits](../finance/20-ledger/07-referral-credits.md)

## Context

A bug hunt over the money doors found nine places where an operator, a late payment or a second browser tab could produce a wrong outcome. The owner decided each on 2026-11-12. This record keeps the four decisions that had real alternatives (1, 2, 3 and 7) with the options not chosen, lists the smaller fixes (4, 5, 6 and 8), states the limits that remain, and records decision 9 as considered and not built. File-level mechanics live in the finance docs linked above; this page keeps the reasons. The owner recorded the options not chosen; where a reason follows one, apart from decision 7 option 2, it is the engineering rationale and not a quoted reason.

Two terms first. A _capture_ is the card payment the gateway has actually taken. A _late capture_ is one that arrives after the platform has already given up on the order (the hold lapsed, the top-up was marked abandoned). A _credit restoration_ is a refund of a payment that was paid with referral credit and no card money: nothing goes back to a card, the credit goes back to the buyer.

## Decisions

1. **The admin recover door refuses a payment whose money has been returned or is contested** (decided, built).
   - **Background.** There is no `REFUNDED` payment status. A late capture that was refunded automatically still reads `SUCCEEDED` with no booking, which looks exactly like a capture waiting for recovery. Recovering it would create a booking and expert earnings on money that already went back to the buyer. For example, Charlotte Brown's late capture of ₹1,724.00 (172,400 paise) is refunded automatically; an operator then presses recover and the platform pays Hannah Anderson's earnings on money Charlotte already holds.
   - **Decided.** Refuse on any pending or successful refund, on an owed-refund marker (`refundOwedKey`) or an auto-refund marker (`Auto-refund pending:`, `Auto-refunded:`, `Auto-refund stuck:`), and on a dispute that is open or lost (a dispute that was won or closed leaves the money with the platform and does not block).
   - **Enforced by** `unreturnedCaptureWhere` (`lib/payments/webhooks/auto-refund-marker.ts`), used by the recover route (`app/api/payments/[paymentId]/recover/route.ts`, typed refusal `PAYMENT_RETURNED`, HTTP 409) and read again inside the capture handler's Serializable transaction (`RecoveryRefusedError`), so a refund or dispute that lands between the two aborts the booking.
   - **Options considered.** Refusing only when the payment is fully refunded was rejected because a partial refund still means part of the money went back, and booking the session would then pay out on a payment the buyer has been partly repaid for. Leaving it to the operator was rejected because the operator sees `SUCCEEDED` with no booking and nothing that says the money went back.

2. **An abandoned wallet top-up is kept, and a late payment on it is credited** (decided, built).
   - **Background.** A gateway order cannot be cancelled and has no expiry, so the order of an abandoned top-up can be paid days later. The abandoned-top-ups job used to delete the row; the money then had nothing to land on.
   - **Decided.** `abandoned-org-top-ups` (`scripts/cleanup/cleanup-abandoned-org-top-ups.ts`) marks the row `FAILED` with a conditional write instead of deleting it. `confirmTopUp` (`lib/api/organizations/wallet.ts`) claims `PENDING` to `CONFIRMED`, and when that matches nothing, `FAILED` to `CONFIRMED`, under the same journal key. When the wallet is frozen or the organisation is closed, the late capture is refunded to the card instead (`refundTopUpCapture` in `lib/payments/operations/wallet-refund.ts`). The orphaned-captures sweep and the top-up verify route accept `FAILED` too.
   - **Honest limits.** `FAILED` for a top-up now means only "abandoned". Replaying the same client key on a `FAILED` top-up answers 409 `TOPUP_NOT_PENDING`. In the frozen or closed case, a failed refund call is not retried automatically: the top-up's note and the audit row say a refund is owed and an operator sends it (from the gateway dashboard for any part that settled an open shortfall receivable rather than reaching the wallet).
   - **Options considered.** Refunding every late capture was rejected because the organisation did intend to pay, and a refund forces them to start again. Handling late payments by hand was rejected because it relies on someone noticing a captured payment that has no row.

3. **Staff refund limits count a credit restoration at its credit value** (decided, built).
   - **Today.** `remedyValuePaise` (`lib/support/case-actions.ts`) returns the credit still in use on a credit-funded payment (`creditStillUsedPaise`), and the cash amount otherwise. It feeds the per-payment limit, the rolling 24-hour limit and the second-approver price.
   - **Decided together with** the wallet refund-to-card door returning only the organisation's own top-up money. `grantedWalletCreditPaise` and the cap are in `lib/payments/operations/wallet-refund.ts`, re-read with the balance before each refund; the refund-remaining route answers `retainedGrantedCreditPaise`.
   - **Consequences, plainly.** An ADMIN credit restoration above the ₹25,000 second-approver threshold now also needs a second admin when two admins exist. Operator-granted wallet credit is treated as spent last and stays in the wallet. The wallet freeze lifts once only granted credit remains.
   - **Deferred, not rejected.** Step-up and a second admin on issuing referral credit; step-up on releasing an earnings hold.

4. **A resumed replay or digital-product purchase never charges a stale price** (decided, built). The routes resume a pending purchase only when its stored amount equals the price derived in the same request; otherwise the old row is retired (`PENDING` to `FAILED`, count checked) and a new order is minted. Honest limit: a late capture on the retired order is refunded when the captured amount differs from the stored one, the item is no longer purchasable or the buyer already holds it. If the buyer holds nothing, they get the item at the price the old tab showed. Refunding it every time would need a new status value, which is a schema change and was not built.

5. **The support refund door keeps the per-staff lock when the outcome is unknown** (decided, built). It releases the lock only for a refusal decided before money could move (a typed 4xx), matching the platform-cancel door (`refusedBeforeMoney` in `lib/support/refund-door.ts`).

6. **Every unpaid exit gives back what checkout consumed** (decided, built). `releaseDeclinedRequestHold` (`lib/booking/request-route-guards.ts`) now goes through `expirePendingPaymentsInTx`; the abandoned-payments sweep, `handlePaymentFailure`, the payment-status reconcile and `releaseSupersededHolds` now also reverse the organisation engagement (`reverseBookingUtilization`). Before, declining a direct-checkout request whose hold had lapsed lost the buyer's referral credits and discount-code use, and four exits never returned the metered engagement, so abandoned checkouts ate a member's programme cap.

7. **A reload or second tab at checkout counts what the buyer's own open order holds** (decided, built; the owner chose option 1).
   - **Background.** Checkout spends the buyer's referral credit and a use of a limited code when it mints an order. The open order keeps holding them. A reload was therefore priced from a smaller picture than the first attempt. Observed on a preview before the fix: a ₹2,990.47 (299,047 paise) plan with ₹200.00 (20,000 paise) of referral credit. The first order was ₹2,790.47 (279,047 paise). After a reload the page showed no credit and charged ₹2,990.47, while the ₹200.00 sat unused on the open order. Now the reload shows the same ₹200.00 and ₹2,790.47, and the open order is reused.
   - **Decided (option 1, "count own reserved credits").** Price the request as if the buyer's own open order for the same item were released. Code: `classifyOpenOrders`, `ownOpenOrderHolds` and `openOrderTargetOf` in `lib/payments/checkout/order-reuse.ts`; `releasableHeldCreditsPaise` in `lib/referrals/service.ts`; the pricing in `lib/payments/checkout/pricing.ts`; and, on the page side, `POST /api/checkout/own-open-order` (`lib/payments/checkout/own-open-order.ts`), the optional `checkout` item on `POST /api/payments/discounts/validate`, and the `useOwnOpenOrderCredits` hook.
   - **"The same item"** is, per booking type: the same plan and exactly the same time for a 1:1; the same plan and first cycle for a subscription; the same event with the seat still held for a webinar or class. A different time is a different booking and is never counted or superseded.
   - **Guards.** The commit-time conditional write on the credit balance still decides. If the open order is paid in between, the new attempt is refused with `CREDIT_SHORTFALL` or `DISCOUNT_EXHAUSTED`. Another buyer's pending use of a one-use code still exhausts it.
   - **Options considered.**
     - **Option 2, not chosen: always reuse the open order for the same item**, whatever the page now says. It is the simplest rule and right for a plain reload. It was rejected because a buyer who wants a different code, or to stop using credits, cannot get that until the hold lapses, and because it leaves the "code exhausted" refusal in place for the buyer's own use of a one-use code.
     - **Option 3, not chosen: leave it as it is.** Rejected because the reload overcharges the buyer by the credit the open order holds, which was the observed defect.
   - **Honest limits.** The "That time was just taken" turn-away for a second tab is avoided only in the same browser, because the hold marker moved from `sessionStorage` to `localStorage`; a second device still gets it. A subscription page that sends "now" as its start can resolve a different first cycle on reload, and then it is not the same item.

8. **A replay or product purchase is never inserted against a row being withdrawn** (decided, built). Inside the purchase transaction, a conditional write on the recording (still published, priced and durably stored) or the product (still published, not taken down, at the price the order was derived from) precedes the insert. When it matches nothing the purchase is refused (`NOT_ELIGIBLE` for a replay; `NOT_FOR_SALE` for a product, or `PRICE_CHANGED` when only its price moved).

9. **Making the staff per-payment cap exact across the support door and the platform-cancel door** (considered, **not built**). It needs both doors to lock the payment row, the committed total to count owed-refund markers, and the event-refund path to check per payment. It is a rare two-operator race bounded by the payment amount, so the cost was judged larger than the exposure. Today the two doors serialise per staff member, not per payment.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- **Hard-deleting an abandoned top-up row.** Replaced by `FAILED` plus a late-capture credit in decision 2. Delete any code that deletes a `WalletTopUp` row or that assumes `FAILED` is never written.
- **A recover door that books any `SUCCEEDED` payment with no appointment.** Replaced by `unreturnedCaptureWhere` in decision 1.
- **Counting a credit-funded refund at its cash amount, and returning the whole wallet balance to the card.** Replaced by decision 3.
- **Resuming an open purchase at its stored price whatever the current price.** Replaced by decision 4; `quoteDigitalProductForBuyer` no longer reads the pending row.
- **A decline that flips the payment to `EXPIRED` by itself.** Replaced by `expirePendingPaymentsInTx` in decision 6.
