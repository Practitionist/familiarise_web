## What it is

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, with the **Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025** notified by MeitY as **G.S.R. 846(E) on 13 Nov 2025** (the 14 Nov 2025 date seen in some trackers is the PIB press release, not the notification). The Act's own commencement is G.S.R. 843(E) of the same day. The Rules apply to any digital personal data processed in India. _(Verified as of 2026-06-05 against the PIB notification + MeitY Rules text.)_

**Phased rollout** (measured from the 13 Nov 2025 notification; Rule 4 after one year, Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 after eighteen months, per G.S.R. 846(E)):

| Phase       | Deadline                     | What's enforceable                                                                                                                    |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Phase 1** | Immediate — **13 Nov 2025**  | Definitions + Data Protection Board provisions (Board constituted)                                                                    |
| **Phase 2** | **13 Nov 2026** (12 months)  | Rule 4: Consent Manager registration framework live                                                                                   |
| **Phase 3** | **~13 May 2027** (18 months) | All other substantive obligations enforceable: consent notice, data-principal rights, breach reporting, retention/erasure, SDF duties |

**Key obligations on a Data Fiduciary (us):**

| #   | Obligation                     | Detail                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Granular consent at signup** | Itemised purposes (booking, payment, recordings, marketing, analytics) — each independently toggleable. Plain English + 22 Schedule VIII languages.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 2   | **Withdrawal mechanism**       | One-click revocation; cascade to revoke downstream processing.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| 3   | **Data principal rights**      | DPDP Act **§11** (right to access information — summary of processing + list of recipients), **§12** (right to **correction, completion, updating AND erasure** — both correction and erasure live in §12, titled "Right to correction and erasure of personal data"), **§13** (right of grievance redressal), **§14** (right to nominate). Endpoints with response SLAs. _(Section mapping verified 2026-06-05 against the Act text.)_ |

| 5   | **Retention limits**           | Auto-erasure per the Rules' Third Schedule — e.g., the prescribed inactivity rule for e-commerce (≥ 2 cr users) / online gaming (≥ 50 lakh) / social media (≥ 2 cr): erase if the data principal has not approached the fiduciary for the specified period.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| 6   | **Breach reporting**           | DPDP **Rules 2025 Rule 7**: notify the Board AND affected data principals **"without delay"** on becoming aware; a **detailed report to the Board within 72 hours** (extendable on written request). > 🟡 **Law-vs-code — breach notification window.** `lib/compliance/databreach-deadline-alerts.ts` (`BREACH_REPORTING_DEADLINE_HOURS = 72`) and `DataBreach.reportedAt` ("must be ≤72h after detectedAt") model breach reporting as a single 72-hour deadline. The current rule (DPDP **Rules 2025, Rule 7**, in force from Phase 3 / 13 May 2027) is two-staged: notify the Board **and** affected data principals **"without delay"** on becoming aware, with a **detailed report to the Board within 72 hours** (extendable). The code's 72h clock maps to the _detailed report_, not the _initial_ "without delay" intimation. No code change made — flagged for the live-impl PR. _(Source: DPDP Rules 2025 Rule 7; verified 2026-06-05.)_

## When it applies

### B2C (consumer marketplace)

- **Applies on every consumer signup, every booking, every recording, every refund.** No exceptions.
- Currently consumer signup at `app/auth/signup/page.tsx` has **no granular consent** — just email/password.
- Privacy policy page exists at `app/(pages)/privacy/page.tsx` but isn't linked from a consent gate.

### B2B (org-sponsored)

- **Applies on org member signup, on org operator data, on consultant onboarding.**
- Org-side `ConsentArtifact` schema + endpoint at `app/api/organizations/[orgId]/consent/route.ts` exists. Consents are typically captured at the org level (org consents on behalf of members for purposes like attendance recording, video sessions).
- Operators (OWNER, MAINTAINER, etc.) need their own consent for org-admin processing.

### Consultants

- Same as B2C consumers for personal-data processing.
- Plus profession-specific data (PAN, GSTIN, bank details, Aadhaar masked) which the platform processes for tax-compliance purposes — has its own purpose code ("payout disbursement + statutory reporting") with retention extending beyond account closure for the TDS and GST audit trail (see Retention windows below; the invoice and TDS rows are never pruned).

### Marketing consent, the newsletter and cookies

- **One marketing control.** An account's DPDP `MARKETING_COMMS` consent is the only marketing switch. Granting it in Settings › Account › Data consent & privacy rights (`app/api/user/privacy/consent/route.ts`) writes the `ConsentArtifact` and, in the same transaction, upserts the account's `NewsletterSubscriber` row as `SUBSCRIBED` with `confirmedAt` now (`lib/newsletter/consent-link.ts`). The authenticated artifact is the consent evidence, so no double-opt-in email is sent. Withdrawing it marks the account's `PENDING` and `SUBSCRIBED` rows `UNSUBSCRIBED` in the withdrawal's transaction. A visitor without an account still joins through the footer form with double opt-in.
- **A newsletter confirmation writes consent too.** Confirming a footer sign-up (`confirmSubscription`, `lib/newsletter/service.ts`) writes a `MARKETING_COMMS` `ConsentArtifact` in the same transaction as the `SUBSCRIBED` status, stamped with `NEWSLETTER_NOTICE_VERSION` (`lib/newsletter/notice.ts`), the version of the purpose notice the footer form shows. When the normalised address belongs to an account the artifact and the row are linked to that user; otherwise the artifact has no `userId` and carries `subjectPseudonymousId`, an HMAC of the normalised address under a key derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` with its own label (`newsletterSubjectId`, `lib/newsletter/consent-link.ts`). The raw address is never stored on the artifact. The id is kept on the row as `consentSubjectId`, and withdrawal and erasure read that stored value, so an auth-secret rotation never separates a subscriber from their artifact. A live artifact for the same subject is reused, so one consent ledger covers account holders and visitors alike.
- **The newsletter's unsubscribe withdraws the consent.** `POST /api/newsletter/unsubscribe` writes `UNSUBSCRIBED` and withdraws the address's `MARKETING_COMMS` artifacts, the linked account's and the pseudonymous subject's, in one transaction (`withdrawNewsletterConsent`), so withdrawal is as easy as the grant (DPDP s.6(4)). A Resend complaint or vendor-side unsubscribe does the same inside the webhook's transaction; a hard bounce only marks the row `BOUNCED`. The page never reports "unsubscribed" when the write failed.
- **Erasure and retention of newsletter consent.** Erasure withdraws a live pseudonymous newsletter artifact for the erased address inside `scrubMailFootprintTx`, the same way `scrubUser` withdraws the account's own artifacts. The existing retention rule applies unchanged: a withdrawal restarts `auditRetainedUntil` at seven years and never shortens it (`withdrawPseudonymousConsent` shares that logic with `withdrawConsent` in `lib/compliance/dpdp.ts`), and the weekly `consent-retention-sweeper` is the only path that deletes an artifact.
- **Consent is checked by purpose.** `checkConsent`, `checkConsentBatch`, `withdrawConsent` and the operator gate match `purposeCodes` (with legacy aliases), `withdrawnAt` and `auditRetainedUntil`; none filters on a notice `version`. Every grant path stamps the current notice version on the server: `TERMS_VERSION` (`lib/compliance/purpose-codes.ts`) in the personal and organisation consent routes, onboarding and signup; a version the client sends is ignored. `TERMS_VERSION` became 3 on 2026-11-07, when the `MARKETING_COMMS` description gained "including the Familiarise newsletter". Grants stamped 1 or 2 stay valid because the check is by purpose (`__tests__/db/consent-version.db.test.ts`).
- **What an org operator sees.** The org consent page and `GET /api/organizations/[orgId]/consent` show the org's own artifacts, plus another member's platform artifacts only for `SIGNUP_PURPOSES`. A member's marketing consent is never shown to their org's operators.
- **One copy of the purpose text.** `PURPOSE_CODE_META` in `lib/compliance/purpose-codes.ts` is the only source of each purpose's label and description; the account consent section, the organisation consent page and all three onboarding checkboxes (`TermsAndPrivacyAgreement`, `ConsulteeAgreementForm` and `OnboardingGateForm`) render it. Onboarding's marketing tick also subscribes the newsletter, like any other grant (`recordOnboardingConsent`, `utils/onboarding-completion.ts`), and records the same evidence as the settings route: the hashed middleware-stamped IP and the user agent (`consentProvenance` in `lib/newsletter/consent-link.ts`).
- **Essential cookies only.** No analytics, advertising or other non-essential script loads, so there is no cookie banner, no cookie-preference API and no account Cookies section. The privacy policy (section 6) says so and promises a consent prompt before any non-essential script runs. `CookiePreference` is unread and is dropped with the next schema batch; its rows stay in the self-serve export until then. The privacy policy (section 6) also names the essential third-party services the app loads (Novu for the inbox, Stream for video and chat, Razorpay for checkout, Sentry for error monitoring with its IP, cookie, header and user collection off and no session replay) and links to the processor list in section 4.2.
- **No analytics consent.** No analytics tool exists, so `ANALYTICS` is not offered: it is outside `OFFERED_PURPOSE_CODES` (`lib/compliance/purpose-codes.ts`), the consent UIs do not list it, and both consent routes refuse to grant it with `400 PURPOSE_NOT_OFFERED`. A stored grant stays as evidence and can still be withdrawn through `DELETE /api/user/privacy/consent?purposeCode=ANALYTICS`. The privacy policy, the self-serve export and the purpose list no longer claim usage analytics or product telemetry.
- `NotificationPreference.marketingEmails` is unread for the same reason; no sender ever read it.

### Age gate: 18+ only

Familiarise serves adults only (owner decision, 2026-11-09). DPDP s.2(f) makes anyone under 18 a child, and processing a child's data would need verifiable parental consent (s.9, Rule 10), so the product refuses minors instead of building that flow. The date of birth is collected only to confirm the person is not a child, which Part B item 6 of the Fourth Schedule permits.

- **One rule, enforced on the server.** `DateOfBirthSchema` in `lib/compliance/age.ts` parses a Date or a `YYYY-MM-DD` string and refuses a future, invalid or under-18 date with `UNDER_AGE_MESSAGE`; `AdultConfirmationSchema` accepts only the literal `true`. `OnboardingConsentSchema` (`utils/onboarding.ts`) requires `adultConfirmed` next to `termsAccepted` and `privacyAccepted`, so the wizard payload, the gate (`completeOnboardingGateAction`) and the organisation-create `onboarding` block all refuse a missing confirmation, and each carries `dateOfBirth: DateOfBirthSchema`.
- **Typed refusal.** A failure comes back as `{ success: false, code: "VALIDATION", field: "dateOfBirth" | "adultConfirmed" }` (`refusalFromIssues` in `utils/onboarding-shared.ts`; the field routes to the wizard's personal-info step through `app/form/onboarding/field-map.ts`). Nothing is written, because validation runs before the transaction opens.
- **Every other writer.** `PUT /api/user/[id]` parses any submitted date of birth with the same schema and answers 400 `VALIDATION`, and it no longer accepts `onboardingCompleted`, so onboarding completes only through `claimOnboardingCompletion`. Operator accounts are created by the back office with no date of birth and are not members of the public.
- **What the person sees.** The wizard's personal-info step and the gate form show a disclaimer under the date field and a required "I confirm I am 18 years old or older" checkbox (`ADULT_CONFIRMATION_LABEL`). The Terms (section 3.1, Eligibility) and the privacy policy (section 8) say the same.
- **Not stored.** The confirmation is a request-time check, not a column; the stored evidence is the date of birth itself and `termsAcceptedAt`.

### Fraud-signal retention

Four hashed columns exist only to stop self-referral and repeated reward abuse and to support chargeback review. They are cleared on a fixed clock by the existing daily `prune-audit-logs` job (the audit-class retention job: once a day, bounded pages, a start budget), not by a new job.

| Column                                           | Cleared                                                                    | Clock                                                                                                                                                | Also cleared on erasure                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Referral.ipHash`, `Referral.userAgent`          | 180 days after the referral is terminal (`REFERRAL_SIGNAL_RETENTION_DAYS`) | `vestedAt` for `VESTED`; `updatedAt` for `VOID` and the legacy `REWARDED`, `EXPIRED` and `FRAUDULENT`, because those transitions record no timestamp | Yes: `scrubUser` nulls them for the referred user                         |
| `Payment.payerVpaHash`, `Payment.payerPhoneHash` | 2 years after capture (`PAYER_SIGNAL_RETENTION_YEARS`)                     | `capturedAt`, or `createdAt` when it is null                                                                                                         | No: they stay for their two-year window as the fraud and chargeback basis |

The windows, the `where` builders and the null payloads live in `lib/compliance/signal-retention.ts`. `pruneAuditLogs` runs each as one bounded page (`referralSignalsNulled` and `payerSignalsNulled` in its result, counted once per run) and writes `AND [predicate, id in picked ids]`, so a row that changed since the pick is skipped. A live referral (`SIGNED_UP`, `QUALIFYING`, `QUALIFIED`, `HELD`) is never selected. One consequence: the same-IP and same-device review rules (`reviewHoldReason` in `lib/referrals/vesting.ts`) count other referrals by their `ipHash`, so a referral whose signals were cleared no longer counts toward a later one.

## Required

### A. Consumer signup consent (PR 1)

1. **Schema**: `ConsumerConsentArtifact` model — analogous to org-side `ConsentArtifact`. Fields: userId, version, purposeCodes[], grantedAt, withdrawnAt, language, hash (SHA-256 of consent text + timestamp), retainUntil.
2. **Signup form**: granular toggles per purpose:
   - `BOOKING` — required (cannot complete signup without it)
   - `PAYMENT_PROCESSING` — required
   - `SESSION_RECORDING` — optional, asked again per-session
   - `MARKETING_EMAILS` — optional, default off
   - Analytics — not offered; no analytics tool exists (see "Marketing consent, the newsletter and cookies")
3. **Persist on signup** + on every consent change.
4. **Privacy policy** must list each purpose explicitly.

### B. DSAR endpoints (PR 2)

1. `GET /api/me/data-export` — generate a JSON dump of all the user's records across the schema. Returns within 7 days (Phase 3 standard SLA).
2. `POST /api/me/data-correction` — user submits correction request; admin actions it.
3. `DELETE /api/me/account` — initiates erasure; cascade described below.
4. `POST /api/me/grievance` — submits a DPDP grievance to the DPO.
5. `POST /api/me/nominate` — designates a nominee for the user's data post-mortem.

### C. Erasure cascade (PR 3)

When a user requests deletion:

1. Mark `User.erasureRequestedAt`.
2. Run within 30 days (Phase 3 standard).
3. Cascade:
   - Anonymise `User` record (keep ID + hashed email for tax-record integrity; remove name, profile pic, etc.).
   - Delete `ConsumerProfile` / `ConsultantProfile` PII.
   - Anonymise message threads (replace user's messages with `[deleted]`).
   - Recordings: per-recording delete option; default retention rule applies.
   - **Tax records (TDSRecord, Invoice)**: cannot be deleted. They are never pruned, and the statutory periods are 72 months for GST (CGST Act s.36), seven tax years for income tax (Income-tax Rules 2026 Rule 46(9)) and eight financial years for books (Companies Act s.128(5)). User is informed.
   - **Session access log (`SessionAccessLog`)**: `scrubUser` deletes the sessions and sets `userId` to null inside its transaction, then stamps the ended sessions without the user link after commit, so a failed log write never fails the erasure; the IP address, user agent and times stay until the 180-day prune, because CERT-In direction (iv) makes keeping them a legal obligation (DPDP s.17). Until erasure, the self-serve export (`app/api/user/privacy/export/route.ts`) lists the user's rows under `activitySummary.sessionAccessLog`.
   - **Mail**: `scrubUser` calls `scrubMailFootprintTx` (`lib/compliance/erasure/mail-footprint.ts`) inside its transaction. It deletes the user's `NewsletterSubscriber` rows (by `userId` or address), dead-letters their `PENDING`/`RETRY` `FailedEmail` rows as `policy:recipient_erased` with subject and bodies redacted, replaces `recipient` on every one of their `FailedEmail` rows (settled ones included) with a non-identifying `erased:<digest>` marker that no send or replay accepts, replaces the `newsletter:<address>` and `contact:<address>` `entityRef` of newsletter and contact-form mail with the same marker, and removes the address from every `FailedEmailBatch` payload, settled or queued (a queued batch the provider may already have seen is dead-lettered `review:erased_recipient`). **`EmailSuppression` is retained**: it holds only the address and the reason, and it is what keeps a bounced or complaining address unmailable if it is entered again; deleting it would turn erasure into a way to resume mail to someone who refused it. Bells need no scrub, because the bell relay drops erased recipients at send time (`unerasedRecipients`, `currentBellRecipients` in `lib/novu/policy.ts`).
   - **Notify-me interest**: `scrubUser` deletes the user's `AppointmentWaitlist` and `WindowBackupInterest` rows inside its transaction, because users are scrubbed rather than deleted and the foreign-key cascade never fires.
   - **Error monitoring (Sentry)**: **cannot be erased in place.** `clearSentryIdentity` stops future events carrying the id; historical events keep the pseudonym until the retention window elapses or they are deleted. There is no Sentry step in `scrubUser` today. This is the one processor whose copy we cannot pull back — see Gap #3. What the user must be _told_ about this is Required H.8, not decided here.
4. Email user a confirmation with what was retained and why.

### D. Retention sweeper (PR 4)

The `consent-retention-sweeper` registry job **already exists** (two-mode: count-only by default, `?delete=1` deletes; weekly on the Netlify ticker, which only counts). Remaining work:

- Restore Actions capacity or review a single scheduler cutover, then verify completion; the weekly schedule is already declared.
- Land the hash-only archival pipeline (dispute-resolution copy) upstream; deletion (`?delete=1` / `--delete`) must stay off until it exists, because deleting the only consent proof is unsafe.
- Extend it (or add a sibling) for `ConsumerConsentArtifact` once that model lands, and for inactive-user erasure (no login in the prescribed period) with a prior notice email.

### E. Multilingual notices (PR 5)

- All consent text + privacy policy + grievance form available in: English, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil at minimum. Telugu / Marathi / Gujarati / Kannada / Malayalam / Punjabi as growth markets.
- Detect language from browser; allow user override.
- The 22-Schedule-VIII full set is aspirational; ship the top 10 by population.

### F. Age gate (PR 6)

Done as 18+ only; see _Age gate: 18+ only_ above. No parental-consent flow, guardian email or minor flag is planned, and none must be built.

### G. checkConsent enforcement (PR 7)

`checkConsent` itself is **already real** (fail-closed: returns `false` if the latest grant is missing, withdrawn, or past retention). Remaining work is _wiring_, not the predicate:

- Call `checkConsent({ userId, purposeCode })` from every data-touching code path (booking, payment, recording, analytics, Stream.io handoff).
- Caller short-circuits with a 403 + UX prompt to re-grant.
- Seed real consent artifacts at signup (PR 1) so the guard has something to read — until then it fail-closes everything for consumers who never granted.

## Acceptance

- A new consumer cannot complete signup without ticking the required consent boxes.
- Consumer can withdraw any optional consent in <3 clicks from settings.
- `GET /api/me/data-export` returns a JSON dump within 7 days.
- `DELETE /api/me/account` triggers anonymisation in 30 days.
- A breach detected at T fires an alert email at T + 60h (12h before deadline) and again at T + 72h.
- Inactive users (3+ years) get a 60-day notice email then auto-anonymisation.
- Privacy policy + signup form available in 4+ languages.
- An under-18 date of birth is refused on the server on every path that stores one or completes onboarding.

Every session end is in the trail: sign-out, revocation, Better Auth deleting an expired row a browser presents, and natural expiry each stamp `SessionAccessLog` (see [the decision record, section 7](../decisions/2026-11-01-data-retention-windows.md#7-the-session-access-log-amended-2026-11-02)). One limit is worth knowing: a `BookingStatusHistory` row of a live booking is never deleted at any age, because the reschedule restore (`readRescheduleOrigin` in `lib/booking/reschedule-restore.ts`) reads it.

## Don't build

| Don't build                                                           | Reason                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Custom consent management platform                                    | A Phase-2 Consent Manager framework will be third-party-provided; integrate when available.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Any parental-consent, guardian or minor flow                          | Familiarise is 18+ only; the server refuses an under-18 date of birth.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Aadhaar-based parental verification                                   | Aadhaar UIDAI auth is restricted; use a digital signature or PAN-based verification.                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Cross-product data sharing without separate consent                   | Each new purpose requires a fresh consent prompt; don't piggyback.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| A second error-monitoring processor, or self-hosting one "to fix §16" | Every processor needs its own inventory entry, sub-processor listing and transfer determination. Adding a second before the first is determined multiplies open questions instead of closing any. Re-platforming the existing one is likewise a determination for `TODO(DPDP-OWNER)`, not an engineering task. |

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- **Verifiable parental consent for under-18 users (dropped 2026-11-09).** The plan was a guardian email, a signed consent and a `MINOR` flag, with payment and recording blocked until verified. The owner chose 18+ only instead, so onboarding refuses an under-18 date of birth and no minor status exists. Delete any residual reference.
- **Backup interest deleted only on erasure (corrected 2026-11-09).** This page once said `WindowBackupInterest` rows were removed only by `scrubUser`. `purgeSettledBackupInterest` also deletes settled rows 30 days after `windowEnd`.
- **Stripe and Neon in the privacy policy and the data export (removed 2026-11-09).** Neither is integrated. Razorpay is the only gateway and Supabase the only database host.
- **Hashed fraud signals kept indefinitely (replaced 2026-11-09).** `Referral.ipHash`, `Referral.userAgent` and the payer hashes on `Payment` had no clock. They now expire as set out in _Fraud-signal retention_.
- **Cookie banner and cookie preferences (removed 2026-11-07).** The `CookieConsentBanner` component (react-cookie-consent) offered essential, analytics, marketing and functional toggles, persisted through `/api/cookie-preferences` and `/api/user/cookie-preferences`, and the account page had a Cookies section. No non-essential script ever loaded, so the choices gated nothing, and the bar covered toasts. Delete any residual reference; a future non-essential script needs a fresh consent design.
- **Analytics consent (removed 2026-11-07).** `ANALYTICS` was offered in the account and organisation consent sections and the policy described "product interaction telemetry", but no analytics code ever ran, so the toggle gated nothing.
- **Marketing preference sync (removed 2026-11-07).** The consent route copied `MARKETING_COMMS` into `NotificationPreference.marketingEmails` and `CookiePreference.marketing`, and `ANALYTICS` into `CookiePreference.analytics`; no reader used any of them. The consent now drives the `NewsletterSubscriber` row directly.
- **A newsletter confirmation without consent evidence, and a best-effort withdrawal (replaced 2026-11-08).** A confirmed footer sign-up once wrote no `ConsentArtifact`, and the newsletter unsubscribe withdrew a linked account's consent in a separate, best-effort transaction after committing. Confirmation now writes the artifact and the unsubscribe withdraws it in the same transaction.
