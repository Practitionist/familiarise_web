> **Critical conclusion:** Familiarise currently describes itself as **principal supplier for GST** (100% outward invoice; no CGST s.52 TCS) but simultaneously says independent experts set prices, render services, receive a net commission-adjusted split, and constitute **e-commerce participants for income-tax withholding**. These positions are **not categorically legally impossible together**, because the definitions are in different Acts, but they are **factually and legally difficult to reconcile** without genuinely documenting which person supplies which service to whom. A GST invoice headed “Familiarise” does **not by itself** prove a genuine principal-to-principal acquisition and resale. **Obtain a written, transaction-type-specific CA/indirect-tax-counsel opinion before implementing the withholding and GST split.**

## Q1. GST principal supplier vs 194-O, 194J or 194C

**1. Short answer.** **Unclear on the supplied facts**—the decisive question is whether independent experts really supply through a digital platform to buyers, or instead supply **to Familiarise**, which independently resells to buyers. If **194-O applies, it generally takes priority over 194J/194C**; a GST invoice in Familiarise's name alone does not settle it. Different GST and income-tax classifications can theoretically coexist, but they must describe an economically defensible arrangement.

**2. Provisions (old → new; GST).** Old **194-O(1)–(3)** → **s.393(1) Table 8(v), Note 3(a)–(e)** and **s.402(12)–(14), (34)**; old **194J** → **s.393(1) Table 6(iii)** and s.402(15),(28); old **194C** → **Table 6(i)** and s.402(47); possibly old **194H** → **Table 1(ii)** for standalone agency/commission. GST: **CGST ss.2(31), 2(44)–(45), 7, 9(5), 15, 22/24, 31 and 52; IGST s.2(13)** (intermediary vs own-account supply definition). Not every consultation is “professional services” under 194J: **s.402(28) is an enumerated definition** (legal, medical, engineering, accountancy, technical consultancy, advertising, etc.), and non-professional technical services under Table 6(iii) have a **2%**, not 10%, rate. A mere contract does **not** establish s.194C “work.”

**3. Guidance and cases.** [CBDT Circular 17/2020](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/communications/circular/circular_17_2020.pdf) clarifies 194-O for platform/payment-gateway situations; it **does not** announce that the GST supplier of record is exempt from 194-O. [CBDT Circular 20/2023](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/ciruclar-20-2023-pdf) (questions 3.1–3.5) addresses multiple ECOs, gross amount, tax components, returns and discounts; the clarifications operate **within** a qualifying 194-O scenario. [**ACIT (TDS) v Riya Travel and Tours (India) Pvt. Ltd**, ITAT Mumbai, 4 Mar 2025, ITA 4172/4191/4193/Mum/2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/197241875/) held **mere use** of a third party's CRS ticket-booking platform did not make the travel intermediary an owner/operator/manager of _that CRS_. Familiarise **owns its own platform**; this judgment is **distinguishable**, not a safe harbor. [**Balat** Maharashtra GST AAR](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/AAR/balat-aar_order.pdf) illustrates the GST fact-specific operator-versus-principal assessment, but a GST AAR is applicant-specific and **cannot conclusively resolve 194-O**. [CBIC e-commerce FAQ Q24/Q25](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/sectoral-faq.html) distinguishes own-account trading from others' supplies.

**Practical test:** Who may set and alter price? Is buyer's contractual counterparty explicitly Familiarise alone? Who warrants clinical/technical competence and bears service-performance liability? Who must make a refund if expert disappears? Does the expert raise a **B2B tax invoice to Familiarise** (where registered)? Does Familiarise purchase at a defined wholesale rate independent of any customer's receipt, or merely hold collected client proceeds and deduct commission? Is revenue booked **gross as seller** or **net as facilitator**? What do Razorpay's merchant-of-record documents say? If the seller is genuinely Familiarise, a correctly classified **194J/194C/other/no TDS** branch may replace 194-O. If the expert is e-commerce participant, classify as **194-O**, even for professional fees, and reassess GST s.52.

**4. Confidence:** **Medium** for statutory precedence; **low** for Familiarise's actual classification absent contracts, supplier invoices and revenue-recognition evidence. An on-point binding jurisdictional court ruling, ruling specific to these terms, or different contract/transaction facts changes the answer.

**5. Ask the CA:** “Please issue a written **two-tax, transaction-by-transaction principal-versus-marketplace opinion** on our expert/host/buyer agreements, actual invoice chain, RazorpayX settlement and accounting. Can we lawfully use GST own-account turnover/no s.52 TCS while withholding expert payouts under s.393(1) Table 8(v)? If not, what exact s.393 Table 6 / Table 1 treatment applies to each expert service category?”

## Q2. ₹5 lakh threshold: annual cliff or excess-only?

**1. Short answer.** **Entire amount, not merely the excess.** ₹5,00,000 is the **annual eligibility condition** for no 194-O deduction for eligible resident I/HUF. The 2025 Act specifically says deduct on the **entire amount** once a table threshold is exceeded (except expressly carved out purchases under Table 8(ii)).

**2. Provisions.** Old **194-O(2)** → new **s.393(4) Table 11**, alongside **s.393(1)(a)** (“on the entire amount ... where ... aggregate ... exceeds”) and **Table 8(v), Note 3**. The 0.1% rate applies to eligible taxable gross sales, not only the ₹1 above ₹5 lakh. Old **194Q** → **Table 8(ii) Note 1(b)** expressly says “sum exceeding ₹50 lakh”—a different rule.

**Example:** Eligible resident individual with PAN and ₹4,90,000 gross YTD has no TDS under this provision yet. A ₹20,000 booking takes YTD to ₹5,10,000. Statutory **full-year gross base** is ₹5,10,000 and a 0.1% full-year amount is **₹510**, not ₹10 (0.1% of the ₹10,000 excess). The **mechanics/timing of catching up on previously exempt bookings** rather than re-opening an already filed quarter should be CA-confirmed; capture the catch-up against the first triggering or subsequent settlement without paying out money needed for withholding.

**3. Guidance.** [Act, s.393(1)(a) and s.393(4) Table 11](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-393-5), [legacy 194-O operative text](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-194-o-7). No specific binding circular setting out a reverse-quarter catch-up procedure for this threshold was identified.

**4. Confidence:** **High** for full-amount rather than marginal excess; **medium** for precise retrospective catch-up procedure across earlier settled/returned quarters. Outcome changes if the supplier is a company, no valid qualifying PAN/Aadhaar is furnished, or the payment is outside 194-O altogether.

**5. Ask the CA:** “When expert gross first crosses ₹5 lakh in tax year 2026–27, do we deduct **0.1% of cumulative eligible gross** at that settlement (less previously deposited 194-O TDS), and what exactly goes into Form 140 for the trigger quarter versus earlier exempt quarters?”

## Q3. Does separately stated GST leave the 194-O base?

**1. Short answer.** **Generally yes if GST is separately indicated and TDS is made at the time of credit** of the sale to the participant. **Not unconditionally:** if tax must be deducted upon **advance payment earlier than credit/invoicing**, Circular 20/2023 says deduct on the **whole payment**, since its GST component cannot then reliably be identified.

**2. Provisions.** Old **194-O(1)** → **s.393(1) Table 8(v)** with earlier-of-credit-or-payment timing in **s.393(1)(c)**. GST component arises under **CGST s.15** and applicable CGST/SGST/IGST invoices. Excluding the GST portion is a **CBDT administrative clarification**, not explicit wording within s.393 Table 8(v).

**3. Guidance.** [CBDT Circular 20/2023, question 3.3](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/ciruclar-20-2023-pdf) directly discusses separately stated GST and advance-payment exception; [Circular 13/2021 under old s.194Q](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/communications/circular/circular_13_2021.pdf) provides analogous tax-component treatment but **194-O-specific Circular 20/2023 is stronger**. **Principal-model caveat:** if the GST invoice is solely Familiarise's own outward supply while the expert's sale consideration differs, assess which invoice legally reflects the 194-O participant sale; do not simply exclude Familiarise's output GST against an unrelated expert invoice.

**4. Confidence:** **High** on the circular's rule; **medium** on current supply-chain base until Q1 resolved. Timing/invoice availability changes the result.

**5. Ask the CA:** “Where gross buyer tax invoice is ₹1,180 (₹1,000 price + ₹180 GST), and expert is credited ₹800, is the proper 194-O gross **₹1,000** or a different amount under our actual supply chain, and does TDS timing precede invoice credit?”

## Q4. Refund drops YTD below ₹5 lakh after TDS: reversal, Form 140, crossing year end

**1. Short answer.** **Do not automatically repay deposited TDS to the expert or erase the challan.** CBDT permits an **adjustment of prior TDS against a subsequent transaction with the same deductee in the same FY** on a refunded sale; tax **already deducted and deposited** continues to be available as seller's tax credit. Across FYs, no express _same-FY_ carryover permission applies.

**2. Provisions.** Old **194-O(1)–(2)** → **s.393(1) Table 8(v), s.393(4) Table 11**; old **s.200(3), Rule 31A/Form 26Q** → **s.397(3)(b), Rule 219/Form 140**; statutory TDS-deposit/statement/correction regime under new **s.397** and Income-tax Rules, 2026. A refund's effect on the annual ₹5L test needs **net eligible actual sales** analysis, but **threshold requalification does not independently license undoing historic government tax deposits**.

**3. Guidance.** [CBDT Circular 20/2023, question 3.4](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/ciruclar-20-2023-pdf) expressly allows **same deductor/same deductee/same financial year** next-transaction adjustment for a returned purchase; says deposited TDS is **seller credit**. [Form 140 official notes](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/fn-140) and [Form 140 FAQ](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/form-140-faqs) govern reporting and correction, replacing Form 26Q. We did **not** identify authoritative 2026 guidance authorising a **negative 194-O challan**, an on-demand operator-to-expert reimbursement of tax already deposited, or cross-FY reallocation.

**Form 140 operational treatment:** report **actual gross credits, deductions, deposits and supported corrections/adjustments**, keyed to deductee PAN, credit/transaction dates and quarter. For any adjustment, preserve original and refunded booking IDs, refund date, original TDS challan, amount already reported, next-booking adjustment, and avoid duplicating a TDS credit for expert. A correction statement is **not** a free-standing claim for the company to recover tax the seller can already claim; have the CA model the permitted utility fields and default/refund workflow.

Treat the refund as its tax-year event and let the seller's 2026–27 TDS credit / income-tax return reflect the tax; seek specific AO/refund/correction remedy if necessary.

**4. Confidence:** **High** for the same-FY/same-deductee next-transaction rule and deposited credit; **medium/low** on the special scenario of crossing **back below** ₹5L, because the CBDT's explicit example is purchase-returns generally, not that precise threshold reversal and quarter reporting.

## Q5. Host company share: gross vs net and ₹5L exemption

**1. Short answer.** **No ₹5 lakh exemption for a company.** But the host's withholding base depends on whether it is a **seller/e-commerce participant** of a booking or merely receives a **10% hosting/marketing service fee**; one buyer's ₹100 booking should **not automatically generate two independent 194-O gross-₹100 bases** for expert and host.

**2. Provisions.** Old **194-O(1)–(2)** → **s.393(1) Table 8(v), Note 3; s.393(4) Table 11** (expressly **only individual/HUF**). If host is paid commission for hosting/promoting, old **194H** → **s.393(1) Table 1(ii)** (2%, ₹20k), or old **194J** → **Table 6(iii)**, old **194C** → **Table 6(i)**, as actual nature requires. Old resident-company 194-O gross is **consideration attributable to its sale**, not automatically platform-wide order gross merely because it receives a portion.

**Example:** ₹1,000 ex-GST booking, contractual split Familiarise ₹100 / host company ₹100 / expert ₹800. **Case A:** expert is actual seller of ₹1,000 service and host supplies a separate ₹100 promotion/organisation service → **expert's 194-O gross may be ₹1,000**, host's separate ₹100 fee classified on merits; do not label host a co-seller of the same entire ₹1,000 without basis. **Case B:** host company is actual seller/provider of ₹1,000 corporate class and pays expert as its subcontractor → host's 194-O gross may be ₹1,000, with **host** responsible for its own 194C/194J/expert relationship (or whatever legally applies). **Case C:** distinct legally priced supplies/shared consideration → allocation requires documentary substantiation and could create separate bases. The 10% _net settlement_ is **not per se** the 194-O base where the host is the actual seller.

**3. Guidance.** [CBDT Circular 20/2023, questions 3.1–3.2](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/ciruclar-20-2023-pdf) on multiple operators and **gross amount including operator commissions/fees**; [new s.393 Table 11](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-393-5). No authoritative ruling directly resolves this exact **10/10/80 co-host** payout model.

**4. Confidence:** **High** that companies have no ₹5L exclusion; **low/medium** on host gross base until host's legal status is fixed.

**5. Ask the CA:** “In our ₹100/₹100/₹800 split per ₹1,000 order, is the corporate host (i) the **seller**; (ii) a **separate commission recipient**; or (iii) jointly rendering a separable supply? ## Q6. Familiarise-funded referral credit: discount or third-party consideration?

**1. Short answer.** If **Familiarise is genuinely the supplier** and grants a **pre-supply/in-invoice price discount borne by itself**, the taxable value generally falls under **CGST s.15(3)(a)**. If **another person compensates the supplier** so the supplier actually receives the old price, the payment may be **third-party consideration / a price-linked subsidy**, not a qualifying value reduction. **Who funds and legally bears the credit matters**, not the UI label.

**2. Provisions.** **CGST s.2(31)** (consideration can be paid by recipient _or any other person_), **s.15(2)(e)** (non-government subsidies directly linked to price), **s.15(3)(a)** (discount recorded in invoice at/before supply), **s.15(3)(b)** (post-supply discounts, operative pre-notification version), and **s.34** for genuine after-sale tax credit notes. **Income tax old 194-O → new s.393(1) Table 8(v)**: the 194-O TDS result can **differ** from outward GST taxable value because ECO-funded cashback which leaves participant entitled to original price stays in participant's gross sale amount.

**Illustration:** Standalone sale price ₹1,000, Familiarise checkout pre-invoice referral discount ₹100 **at Familiarise's expense**, expert's contract price re-priced to reflect discounted sale: if true own-account supplier, ₹900 GST value and ₹162 GST (assuming 18%). Contrast buyer pays ₹900 but sponsoring entity pays a further ₹100 **toward an unchanged ₹1,000 supply**: generally examine ₹1,000 value. If marketplace arrangement gives expert its full original ₹1,000 despite buyer's ₹900 payment, **CBDT Circular 20/2023 Q3.5** treats its **194-O gross as ₹1,000**, irrespective of platform promo.

**3. Guidance/cases.** [CGST s.15](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000284/1000001), [CBDT Circular 20/2023 Q3.5](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/ciruclar-20-2023-pdf), [**Supreme Paradise v Assistant Commissioner (ST)**, Madras HC (2024)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/140816415/) (third-party price-linked subsidy distinction; **fact-specific**). **2026 legislation flag:** Finance Act 2026 **ss.153–154** amends post-sale discounts under CGST ss.15 and 34 **from a separately notified date**—it was **not automatically effective on 1 April 2026**; a specific commencement notification must be checked before using amended post-sale mechanics. See [Finance Act amendment discussion](https://gstpragyan.in/articles/gst-changes-april-2026) and [GST Council policy rationale](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555&lang=2&reg=3). **No on-point ruling was found** resolving this exact platform-funded incentive design.

**4. Confidence:** **High** for statutory distinction; **medium** applying it to wallet/referral flows and **low** if the supplier model is contradictory or the 2026 commencement position subsequently changes.

## Q10. Added B2B overage on already-paid invoice: credit note?

**1. Short answer.** **A genuine additional overage/underbilling is increased consideration: ordinarily an invoice or **debit note**, not a credit note.** A **credit note** reduces/reverses a previously **overstated** charge or qualifying returned/deficient supply. The **s.34(2) tax-credit-note deadline** is **30 November after the financial year of the original supply, or annual-return furnishing, whichever is earlier**.

**2. Provisions.** **CGST s.31** (invoice), **s.34(1)** (credit note: actual taxable value/tax charged less than invoiced? Careful: s.34(1) operates when amount invoiced **exceeds** actual taxable value/tax payable, goods returned, or service deficient); **s.34(3)** (debit note when original value/tax **less** than payable), **s.34(2)** (declare credit notes by Nov 30 or filing annual return, earlier), **ss.16/37** (ITC/invoice statements), **s.13** time of service tax supply. **Old/new income-tax:** not itself a deduction provision; for B2B payer assess old 194J/194C etc → new s.393 Table 6 where the separate transaction warrants it.

**Example:** July 2026 invoice ₹10,000 (+GST), fully paid; permitted additional usage ₹2,000. Issue valid **debit note or separate taxable usage invoice** ₹2,000 (+applicable tax) and report when law requires. If ₹2,000 overage was **wrongly billed**, then **credit note** that erroneous amount; for original FY 2026–27 the **last ordinary GST tax-reduction note reporting date is 30 Nov 2027 or earlier annual return date**. A **commercial/financial credit note** after statutory cutoff might be possible but **cannot ordinarily reduce GST output tax** after the cutoff. Identify whether a later event is actually a **new supply** rather than back-pricing an old one.

**3. Guidance.** [CGST s.34 (CBIC statutory portal)](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000304/1000001), [GSTR-1 manual](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR_1.htm). **2026 watch:** Finance Act 2026 changed certain post-sale discount/credit-note rules under ss.153–154 **subject to notification**; confirm commencement before relying on amended s.15/34 language. [GST Council explanation](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555&lang=2&reg=3).

## Q13. Lost-card chargeback after invoicing: GST credit note and gateway fee

**1. Short answer.** A **card-network chargeback is a reversal of the _payment_**, not necessarily a reversal of the **taxable supply**. ## Q14. Furnished but unverified PAN: mandatory higher no-PAN withholding?

Note that a genuine valid Aadhaar furnished in terms of the exclusion may also matter; assess actual higher-rate rules separately.

**3. Guidance.** [2025 Act ss.262, 397](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf), [CBDT Circular 9/2025 concerning PAN-inoperative TDS relief](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/communications/circular/circular-9-2025.pdf) (review exact conditions and transition to new Act), [official income-tax interest/fee help referencing higher-rate relief](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/interest-and-fees). **Relief timing:** CBDT's transitional relaxation concerning PAN becoming operative **within specified later time** does **not** equal universal permission to skip verification; test its scope against deduction date, year and 2026 regime. **Unclear:** no identified definitive 2026 CBDT circular says a PAN **merely recorded but never authenticated** is always sufficient **regardless** of outcome.

**4. Confidence:** **High** for invalid/inoperative PAN distinction; **medium/low** for operational handling of unvalidated names/pending government confirmation. PAN actual legal status and official verification result change the answer.

**5. Ask the CA:** “Do we treat **PAN furnished but name-match API pending** as normal 0.1% under s.393(1) Table 8(v), or **hold payout until verification**? Which official inoperative-PAN response requires **5%** under s.397(2), how do we handle Aadhaar in s.393(4) Table 11, and what relief if PAN becomes operative within a permitted period?”

## Recommended data model / compliance implementation

Create a **versioned tax determination** for **each seller and each sale leg**, not one global `tdsRate=0.1%` flag. These are design recommendations, **not statutory field names**.

| :--------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `PartyTaxProfile`      | party ID, **resident/nonresident**, legal type (I/HUF/company/firm), validated PAN and `panStatus` (verified/pending/inoperative/invalid), Aadhaar furnished flag where legitimately collected, GSTIN, jurisdiction, treaty/TRC data, validity period                                                         |
| `SupplyLeg`            | provider, buyer, **legal supplier**, principal vs disclosed-agent classification and opinion version, service category/SAC, performance/recipient state, chargeable price and original contract price, GST rate, seller invoice, purchaser invoice, host fee agreement                                        |
| `BookingTaxSnapshot`   | pre-invoice discount origin, recipient-paid component, third-party consideration/sponsorship, GST taxable value separately from **194-O gross**, applicable section **s.393 Table row**, PAN-dependent TDS rate/threshold status, date of credit vs payment, controlling tax-year, original/refund references |
| `TdsLedger`            | **per PAN per tax year** gross eligible turnover and refunds, annual ₹5L eligibility history, tax withheld, tax deposited, challan, Form 140/144 quarter and filing ID, **same deductee same-FY adjustment** with source/refund/next-sale linkage, CA-approved correction record                              |
| `GstLedger`            | outward invoice/note issue date, **time-of-supply event** date, advance receipt/allocation, reported GSTR-1 period, GSTR-3B liability month, original note link, note's **s.34 deadline**, ITC-reversal confirmation if relevant, POS state and SAC                                                           |
| `RemittanceCompliance` | country, chargeability/DTAA memo, old s.195/new **s.393(2) Table 17** rate/order, Form **145 Part** and **146** acknowledgment, Form **144**, FX remittance bank confirmations, RCM note                                                                                                                      |

**Automated hold conditions** (proposed): missing buyer state for online B2C; unresolved resident **supplier legal status**; nonresident expert without chargeability/DTAA review; TDS threshold crossing where balance insufficient for full catch-up; PAN inoperative/invalid; refund attempted across year boundary as an automatic TDS deduction reversal; refund/chargeback incorrectly producing automatic GST credit note; discount funding inconsistent with expert entitlement.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- **194-O at 1%.** The 2020 circular (17/2020) prints 1%. The rate is 0.1% from 1 October 2024 and stays 0.1% in tax year 2026–27. Delete any copy of the old figure from payout code or docs.
- **Old s.206AA.** The higher no-PAN rate is now s.397(2), and for 194-O it is 5%, not 20%.
- **Old forms.** Forms 26Q, 27Q, 16A, 15CA and 15CB are Forms 140, 144, 131, 145 and 146 for tax year 2026–27 onward. Earlier years keep the old form names.
