## 2. Legal basis

Two instruments apply.

1. **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023**, issued by the Central Consumer Protection Authority on 30 November 2023. Annexure 1 names 13 specific dark patterns. They bind every platform, advertiser and seller.
2. **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026**, G.S.R. 789(E), in force from 1 January 2027. Per the secondary sources (Khaitan & Co. ERGO of 18 September 2026, Bar & Bench and Taxheal), they require an e-commerce entity to comply with the 2023 Guidelines, to run a yearly self-audit and prominently display a certificate of compliance, to show the lowest price of the previous 30 days beside an announced reduction, to print the seller's name on the invoice at the platform's own prominence, to give the complainant a copy of the complaint as recorded, to disclose ranking parameters in plain language and label sponsored listings, and to disclose the entity's name, address and customer and grievance contacts.

The Rules prescribe no method, scope, format or signatory for the self-audit. The method in section 3 is therefore our own. The rule numbers (reported as 4(13) to 4(15)), the National Consumer Helpline convergence duty and the scope of the seller-identifier display are unverified; the Gazette text must be read before this record is signed. The wider context is in [consumer protection and grievance](./03-consumer-protection-and-grievance.md), section 5.

## 3. Method

1. List every flow in which a buyer sees a price, chooses, pays, cancels, consents or is contacted (section 4).
2. For each of the 13 patterns, read the code that implements the flow and record a verdict (pass, finding or not applicable), the file and function that is the evidence, and the reason.
3. Run seven extra checks the Rules add (A to G in section 5.2).
4. Record each finding in the register (section 6), fix it where it is cheap and in scope, and name the owner and reason where it is not.
5. Walk the flows in a browser for what code cannot show (section 8).
6. The director signs; the certificate is published by filling `DARK_PATTERN_AUDIT` in `lib/company.ts`.

## 9. Limitations

- The legal basis is read from secondary sources until the Gazette text is checked (section 2).
- The review is of code and a browser walk, not of every email a vendor may send.
- The Razorpay payment sheet is the vendor's interface; only what a buyer sees of it can be reviewed.
- Organisation contracts auto-renew. That is a business-to-business term between organisations and is outside the consumer-protection scope of this audit.
- A self-audit is not an approval by any authority, and the certificate claims nothing beyond what sections 4 and 5 cover.

## Deprecated & Superseded Approaches

- **"Monthly Fee" label on a months plan (replaced 2026-11-09).** `planPriceLabel` named a months plan's price per month, but the price is charged once and covers the whole plan. It now reads "Plan price (N months)".
- **Day 7 urgency and FOMO follow-up emails (deleted 2026-11-09).** `consultant-followup-v2.md` and `consultee-followup-v2.md` used closing-soon wording, invented sign-up counts and unattributed quotes. The rules that replace them are in `emails/promotional/README.md`.
- **An organisation case's intake email without its text (replaced 2026-11-09).** It said only that a case was received. It now carries the description, as a personal case always did.
- **Withholding the complaint from an anonymous complainant.** The receipt carried only a reference and a confirmation link. The recorded complaint is now sent when the address is confirmed, never before, so the platform cannot be used to send text to an address its owner has not confirmed.
