# Medicine & Health Vertical Playbook

**Last updated:** March 2026
**Launch window:** Month 9-12 (highest regulatory complexity — save for last)
**Strategy:** Start with non-doctor health experts (lighter regulation), add doctors later

---

## Section 1: Market Size & Opportunity

- 1.2M+ doctors registered with National Medical Commission (NMC) in India
- 500K+ wellness professionals (nutritionists, dietitians, fitness coaches, yoga instructors)
- India telemedicine market: $3.6-4.2B (2025), growing rapidly post-COVID
- Mental health: Fastest-growing segment at 25.55% CAGR
- Practo dominates telemedicine: ₹350-400Cr revenue, 15-25% commission, ₹200-500 avg consultation
- Amaha (mental health): Raised ₹50Cr, Wysa raised $30M — mental health is the most funded health-tech sub-segment
- Wellness coaches/nutritionists: ₹2,000-10,000 per session — underserved segment with lighter regulation
- Post-COVID: Telemedicine permanently normalized. 80% of patients who tried teleconsultation during COVID continued using it.
- Government push: Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission making telemedicine infrastructure widespread
- 150K+ AYUSH practitioners (Ayurveda, Yoga, Naturopathy) — emerging digital segment

**Key Insight: We do NOT need to compete with Practo.** Practo is for quick doctor consultations (₹200-500, 10-15 minutes). We're for expert consultations (₹1,000-10,000, 30-60 minutes). Different market. Think: specialist second opinions, ongoing wellness coaching, mental health therapy sessions, nutrition planning.

| Nutritionists/Dietitians | Certified, 3+ years practice, active on Instagram | Huge demand, light regulation, digital-native audience |

**Phase 2 (Month 11-12): Specialist Doctors**

| Type | Target Profile | Why Later |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| Specialist doctors | 10+ years, willing to do second opinion consultations | Higher regulatory bar, but very high value per session |
| Dermatologists | For skin consultations (photo + video) | High demand, works well via video |
| Psychiatrists | For medication reviews (NOT initial prescriptions online) | Complex regulation, but subscription model works |
| Pediatricians | For parenting advice, child health questions | High demand from new parents |

**Disqualified:**
- General practitioners (GPs) — Practo territory, commodity pricing
- Anyone without proper certification/registration
- Doctors wanting to prescribe controlled substances online (regulatory violation)
- "Wellness influencers" without certifications (brand risk)

## Section 6: Messaging That Resonates

**For Nutritionists/Wellness Coaches:**
Pain point: "You're creating free content on Instagram for 10K followers but earning nothing from it."
Value prop: "Turn followers into paying clients. Offer 1:1 nutrition sessions, monthly wellness subscriptions, and group webinars on your own terms. Keep 90% of every rupee."

**For Psychologists:**
Pain point: "Your therapy practice is limited by geography. Clients in Tier 2-3 cities can't access quality mental health support."
Value prop: "Offer therapy sessions via integrated video with automatic recording (with consent), session notes, and subscription-based ongoing support. Extend your reach beyond your city."

**For Specialist Doctors:**
Pain point: "Practo pays you ₹200-500 for a 10-minute consultation. Your expertise is worth more."
Value prop: "Offer 30-60 minute specialist consultations at ₹2,000-10,000. Second opinions, treatment plan reviews, and ongoing patient management. Not quick-fix primary care — expert-level advisory."

**Objection handling:**

1. "Telemedicine has regulatory restrictions" → "Our platform is for expert consultation and second opinions, not primary diagnosis or prescribing. Nutritionists, psychologists, and wellness coaches have lighter regulatory requirements. For doctors, we comply with Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 including informed consent and registration display."

2. "Practo already dominates this space" → "Practo is for ₹200-500 quick consultations. We're for ₹1,000-10,000 expert consultations. Different market. A nutritionist on Practo gets commodity pricing. On Familiarise, they build a premium practice with subscriptions and webinars."

3. "My patients need in-person examination" → "We complement in-person practice, not replace it. Initial consultations, second opinions, follow-ups, and ongoing coaching all work beautifully via video. The physical examination happens at their local doctor — you provide the expertise."

4. "I'm concerned about liability" → "We include medical disclaimers, informed consent framework, and clear Terms of Service. You control the scope of your consultations. Our platform is for advisory, not emergency care."

## Section 8: Platform Features to Highlight

| Priority | Feature | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| 1 (KILLER) | **Built-in video** | Medical consultations REQUIRE video. Not a phone call. HD, stable, with recording capability. |
| 2 | **Subscriptions** | Perfect for ongoing wellness coaching, therapy, nutrition follow-ups. Recurring revenue. |
| 3 | **Session recording** | With consent — therapists can review sessions, nutritionists can reference past plans. (MUST be opt-in with explicit consent) |
| 4 | **Document review** | Lab reports, diet logs, medical records shared before consultation. |
| 5 | **Webinars** | "Healthy Eating on a Budget" free webinar → funnel to paid 1:1 nutrition. |
| 6 | **Multi-week classes** | 4-week wellness programs, yoga courses, meditation programs. |
| 7 | **UPI payments** | Instant, no forex. |

**Critical: Recording MUST be opt-in with explicit consent for medical sessions. Default OFF for this vertical.**

## Section 9: Onboarding Friction Points

| Friction | Solution |
|----------|----------|
| Regulatory compliance | Provide Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 summary. Include informed consent template. Display registration number on profile. |
| "I'm not tech-savvy" | Phone-assisted onboarding for doctors. Simpler profile setup. |
| "My patients don't use online platforms" | Target digitally-savvy patients (25-45, urban). These are the ones already searching "nutritionist near me" on Google. |
| Medical liability concerns | Clear disclaimers. "Advisory consultation" positioning. Informed consent flow. |
| Certification verification | Manual verification of medical registration, RCI registration, or nutrition certification before profile goes live. |

## Section 10: Digital Adoption Barriers

- 20-30% of doctors are digitally active for consultations (post-COVID, up from <5% pre-COVID)
- Nutritionists/wellness coaches: 40-50% digitally active (Instagram-native)
- Psychologists: 30-40% (growing rapidly with mental health awareness)
- Age: Younger practitioners (25-45) are 3x more likely to adopt digital platforms
- Infrastructure: Video calls require stable internet. Patients in rural areas may struggle.
- Trust: Patients are skeptical of online medical advice. Reviews and credentials are critical trust signals.
- Cultural: Mental health still carries stigma in India (improving but barrier to demand)
- Solution: Start with consultants who already have digital presence (Instagram, YouTube). They know how to attract digital-native patients.

- [ ] Review Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 in detail
- [ ] Draft informed consent template for platform use
- [ ] Add registration number display field to consultant profiles
- [ ] Create "Medical Consultation Disclaimer" for ToS

### Why This Vertical Is Last

1. Highest regulatory complexity of any vertical
2. Liability risk is real (even for advisory)
3. Credential verification requires manual process
4. Competition (Practo, Amaha) is well-funded
5. But: The gap in premium expert consultation (not quick-fix primary care) is massive. Nobody serves the ₹2,000-10,000 specialist consultation market well in India.
