# Engineering log: the dashboard overhaul (PR #1842, part of #1527)

**Date:** 2026-09-27 · **PR:** #1842, `feat/dashboard-mega-audit-1527` → `dev` · **Related ADRs:** `2026-09-27-dashboard-shell-and-context-switcher.md`, `2026-09-27-backoffice-single-tree-and-capability.md`, `2026-09-27-org-role-matrix.md`, `2026-09-27-offering-plan-draft-status.md`, `2026-09-27-support-inbox-and-case-pages.md`.

This log records what changed across every dashboard (consultee, consultant, organization, org-workspace, admin and staff) in PR #1842, and why, at the level of the mechanisms rather than the full page-by-page inventory (that lives in the PR body). It also records the review rounds, the QA waves and what they found, so the next person touching this code does not repeat the same defects.

## Why this shipped as one PR

A fresh product, design and engineering audit of every dashboard found that the #1527 decision set was mostly unbuilt, that mobile navigation was broken on every tree, and that live bugs (a "Save draft" button that published, a payout list four statuses short of complete, an org role that got a 403 on its own home page) sat in code nobody had re-read since it was written. Fixing the architecture and the bugs together, rather than in sequence, avoided rebuilding the same pages twice.

## One shell, one switcher

Before this PR, four hand-rolled shells (`PersonalDashboardShell`, an inline `OrgDashboardShell`, an inline `OrgWorkspaceShell`, `OperatorDashboardShell`) each carried their own chrome, padding, canvas colour and breadcrumb logic, and five different menus handled context switching, none of them shared. `components/dashboard/DashboardShell.tsx` now renders the chrome for all four. `PersonalDashboardShell` is deleted, and `OrgDashboardShell` and `OperatorDashboardShell` remain only as thin adapters that build their tree's nav and pass it in. The shell owns the Novu provider, the single error boundary, the `bg-zinc-50` canvas and the `p-4 sm:p-6 lg:p-8` gutter, and every tree now passes it a `DashboardNav` and renders inside it.

`components/dashboard/ContextSwitcher.tsx`, fed by `resolveDashboardFacets()` (in `lib/labels/personal-dashboard.ts`), renders at the top of every sidebar and again inside the mobile Menu sheet. It groups facets as "You" (Expert, Learner), "Organizations" (every membership, including PENDING and SUSPENDED ones, fetched lazily from `/api/user/org-memberships?all=1` rather than widening the session), and "Platform" (Admin, Staff), plus footer actions for creating an organization or becoming an expert when the viewer is entitled. This closed the platform's dual-role gap: previously a consultant who booked another expert had a consultee profile created for them at checkout with no way to reach it, because nothing in the UI linked the two identities.

The header (owner-redesigned twice after this PR opened, once after studying a Cloudflare dashboard screenshot) carries, right to left: the persona CTA, a **Help ▾** menu (Help Center · Support requests · Send feedback), the notification bell, and an avatar menu (name, email, role, Settings, Sign out). A standalone collapse toggle sits to the left of the breadcrumb (Ctrl/⌘ \\). Settings moved out of the sidebar into that avatar menu for the personal and back-office trees at this point in the PR; a later commit (Round 4, below) moved organization and workspace settings out of their sidebars into the same avatar menu, so there is no settings row left in any dashboard's sidebar.

`MobileNav` shows up to four configured tabs with badges, plus a **Menu** tab that opens a sheet with the switcher, the full grouped nav, the header's utility block and Sign out. The same component serves every tree, including the back office, whose sidebar previously took a fixed 256px on a phone regardless of viewport.

## The `/dashboard/go` resolver

B2C notifications send one payload to several recipients (a consultant and the consultee they are meeting, for example), so 37 links across the codebase pointed at a bare `/dashboard`, which redirects by the signed-in viewer's role — landing a dual-role viewer on the wrong side. `app/dashboard/go/[facet]/[[...path]]/page.tsx` and `lib/dashboard/go.ts` resolve a facet-relative link (`expert`, `client`, or `auto`) to the viewer's own dashboard URL. The `auto` facet reads one appointment (`readAppointmentDetail`) to decide which side the viewer is on before handing off to `resolveGoHref`; every other segment resolves without a database read. The destination page re-checks access on its own terms, so a wrong resolution is a redirect, not an exposure. `goHref()` gives call sites a typed builder instead of hand-spelling the URL.

## The back-office merged tree

Admin and staff previously lived in two URL trees (`app/dashboard/admin/**` and `app/dashboard/staff/[staffId]/**`, the latter's `[staffId]` segment doing nothing), with `isAdmin`/`canManage` boolean props threaded through roughly 26 files. They now share one route tree, `app/dashboard/(backoffice)/[tree]/**`, where `[tree]` is the literal segment `admin` or `staff`. `lib/backoffice/capability.ts` exports `resolveBackofficeCapability(role, tree)`, which returns `null` for any role that may not open that tree (a non-operator, or STAFF opening the admin tree), and `can(cap, surface)`, which requires both the tree's audience and the viewer's own role to hold a `BackofficeSurface` permission — so an admin who opens the staff tree sees exactly what staff see, never more. `BackofficeCapabilityProvider` exposes the same object to client components via `useBackofficeCapability()`.

Retired URLs (the old `/dashboard/staff/<uuid>/...` shape, `approval-payments`, `documents`, `admin/feedbacks`, `data-breaches`) are not deleted outright: `lib/backoffice/legacy-routes.ts` is a pure, jest-pinned function that maps each one to its new location (for example, `approval-payments` now redirects to the Appointments "Awaiting payment" tab, carrying the incoming query string), and a catch-all `[tree]/[...legacy]/page.tsx` answers a 308 for anything the function resolves, or a 404 for anything it does not. Of the roughly 24 `/api/staff/**` API routes only two (`/api/staff/payouts`, `/api/staff/moderation/profiles[/:id]`) were true duplicates of an admin API and were deleted; the rest were kept as-is, because they serve different data shapes for the two audiences.

## Design primitives

Before this PR the dashboards had 11 different page-title treatments, roughly 40 double-padded pages, 22 hand-rolled paginations, about 33 local status-colour maps, and 6 different settings layouts. This PR did not invent a new design system; it consolidated the ones already in use into shared primitives and moved every page it touched onto them:

- **`PageHeader`** (evolved from `PageScaffold.DashboardHeader`, its 65 existing importers): one `h1` per page, a two-line-clamped description, right-aligned actions that stack on mobile, an optional back link, and no double gutter.
- **`Stat`** (evolved from `StatCard`, replacing 13 inline copies): a label, a `tabular-nums` value, an optional hint, and an optional `href` that makes the whole tile a link.
- **`Section`** and **`KeyValueList`**: a labelled `h2` block with a `card`/`plain` variant, and a `<dl>`-based label/value list with a fixed label column.
- **`lib/ui/tone.ts`** and **`StatusBadge`**: six tones only — success, info, caution, warning, critical, neutral — moved out of `lib/dashboard/money-state.ts` (which keeps a re-export so its existing importers do not change) into a dependency-free module both server and client components can import. `StatusBadge` takes either a `tone` directly or a legacy `className`/`dotClassName` pair from an existing `lib/labels` map, so callers migrated incrementally rather than all at once.
- **`ConfirmDialog`**, replacing roughly 18 separate `AlertDialog` compositions and absorbing the older admin `ReasonDialog`: a centred dialog at `sm`+ and a bottom sheet on mobile, an optional `requireReason` (a textarea with a minimum length, recorded in the audit log), an optional `requireTyped` string the user must type exactly, and failures shown inline in the dialog rather than in a toast that can be missed. `TYPED_CONFIRM_THRESHOLD_PAISE` (`lib/ui/typed-confirm.ts`), set to `1_000_000` (₹10,000), is the one constant every money-action call site reads to decide whether to require a typed confirmation; the ADR on the org role matrix (below) is not where this threshold lives, but every backoffice refund and payout-approval dialog above it reads the same constant.
- **List primitives**: `ResponsiveTable`, `FilterBar` (with a built-in debounced search), `TablePagination` and `useListParams`, which put pagination, filters and sort state in the URL instead of component state — closing 7 local-state tab sets and 2 "read once, never refetch" query patterns the audit found.
- **`SettingsLayout`**: one grouped-left-nav-at-`md`+, list-then-detail-on-mobile settings hub, shared by the consultee, consultant, organization and back-office trees in place of 6 separate settings layouts. Each section is its own URL, so the browser's back/forward buttons and a shared deep link both work.

## The hydration fix and its trade-off

React hydration error #418 (a server/client markup mismatch) showed up repeatedly during QA on any page that formatted a date or time, because the server rendered in the deploy's runtime zone while the browser rendered in the viewer's local zone. `lib/time/zoned-format.tsx` introduces a `DisplayZoneProvider` that pins every nested `useZonedFormat()` call to the zone the originating RSC page already resolved (`getViewerZone`), so the server and the client agree on one wall clock. The fix pins display formatting to a fixed `en-IN` locale rather than deriving the viewer's own locale from a request-time signal. This is a real trade-off, not an oversight: a viewer outside India sees Indian date and time conventions (for example, a 24-hour clock rendered in the `en-IN` style rather than their own locale's convention). It is tracked, not silently accepted, in issue #1845.

## The ErrorBoundary fix

`components/ErrorBoundary.tsx` previously treated every thrown error inside a dashboard page, including Next.js's own `notFound()` and `redirect()` control-flow signals, as an application crash, rendering "Dashboard Error" instead of a real 404 or the intended redirect. It now calls `unstable_rethrow(error)` inside both `componentDidCatch` and `render()`; when that call itself throws (meaning the original error was a genuine crash), the boundary shows its fallback UI as before, but when `unstable_rethrow` swallows it (meaning the original error was Next.js's own navigation signal), the boundary re-throws it so Next's own boundary handles it. A 404 inside a dashboard route now renders as a 404.

## The late-join rule in the consultee resources read

The consultee Recordings page bypassed the #1819 late-join rule: `/api/consultees/[id]/recordings` filtered recordings by the caller's entitlement to the underlying webinar or class, but never checked whether the caller joined the specific session late (a host can hide the pre-join portion of a recording from a late joiner). The route now calls `lateJoinRecordingAccess(session.user.id)` and filters the entitled list through `hiddenFromLateJoiner(recording, lateJoin)` before returning it, matching the enforcement the rest of the recordings surface already had.

## QA rounds and lessons learned

Four Sonnet/Opus QA waves ran case-by-case against the Netlify deploy preview with real Supabase data, one browser session at a time, alongside a SonarQube Cloud scan (91 findings) and a CodeRabbit CLI pass (57 findings across 16 directories). Two findings are worth recording as lessons rather than just as fixed bugs:

- **A fixed element covering a bottom-pinned button looks "inert," not broken.** QA wave 4 found that the back-office newsletter's "Send to subscribers" button appeared to do nothing on click. The button worked; the cookie consent bar, fixed to the bottom of the viewport, sat on top of it and intercepted the click. The fix moved the send form above the recipient list and made the cookie bar publish its live height as a CSS variable, so sticky and fixed page elements can reserve space for it instead of being silently covered. Any future bottom-pinned action (a save bar, a confirm button) should check its stacking position against the cookie bar rather than assume click-through was tested.
- **A crash boundary can swallow a framework's own control flow.** The `notFound()`-as-"Dashboard Error" bug (above) existed because nothing distinguished "this component threw because something broke" from "this component threw because Next.js wants a different render path." Any custom error boundary in this codebase should call `unstable_rethrow` before treating a caught error as a real failure.

Other defects the QA waves and review rounds found and fixed are catalogued in the PR body's "Changes after the PR opened" and "Review rounds" sections rather than repeated here, to avoid two documents drifting apart on the same list.

## Round 4: Library, org-tagged support, settings in the avatar menu, and Find

Four more commits landed on PR #1842 after the rest of this log was written, closing gaps the QA waves had flagged but not yet built. They are recorded here rather than folded into the sections above, so the history of what shipped when stays legible.

**Org member Library.** Every org member, SUSPENDED included, now sees a Library group in the sidebar with Documents and Recordings, replacing the pages that used to live only under Operations for `operations.read` holders. Each page is a page of sessions rather than a page of files, and carries `Mine · Everyone` URL tabs (`?scope=`): Mine is the caller's own org sessions (attended, seat held, or delivered), with materials, uploads and responses, URLs included; Everyone stays the pre-existing `operations.read` oversight view, metadata only per ADR 20. `GET /api/organizations/[orgId]/documents` and `.../recordings` both accept `scope`, `q`, `kind`, `from`, `to`, `source` and `page`, implemented against the shared, prisma-free `lib/library/library-query.ts` contract so the client browser and the server read agree on the same filters. Pagination pages by session (`LIBRARY_PAGE_SIZE = 10`), not by file, so a session with many uploads does not split across pages. The Recordings read's Mine scope now calls the same `lib/stream/session-recordings.ts` extractor the consultee resources route uses, so the #1819 late-join rule (a host can hide the pre-join portion of a recording from a late joiner) applies there too. The Operations sidebar group keeps only Support; Documents and Recordings moved out of it entirely.

**Org-tagged support requests.** The new-request form gained an "About" select — Me by default, plus each org where the viewer holds an ACTIVE membership with the new `supportRequests.org` matrix key (`operations.read` OR `billing.read`); the org Billing page's "Generate invoice" support link preselects that org. The create route validates the membership and the key server-side before setting `organizationId`, and no longer infers an org from the caller's first ACTIVE membership — the #1021 behaviour that could tag a personal request onto an org's Support page by accident. A new route, `GET /api/organizations/[orgId]/support-tickets`, backs the org Support page's new "Organization requests" tab (subject, category, status and requester name only, paged; the thread itself stays with the requester and the platform team per ADR 20), sitting alongside the existing "Session conversations" tab gated on `operations.read`.

**Settings moved to the avatar menu.** The org and workspace sidebars lost their settings row. The header avatar menu now offers "Settings" (personal, unchanged) plus "<Org name> settings" — shown only to a role holding at least one section on `ORG_SETTINGS_SECTIONS` (`lib/dashboard/org-settings-sections.ts`), so OWNER, MAINTAINER and BILLING_ADMIN see the entry and a plain member does not — or "Workspace settings" inside an org-workspace shell. Both settings surfaces render through the shared `SettingsLayout` primitive: a grouped left sub-menu with one URL per section, rather than the previous single page with internal `?tab=` state. Old `?tab=<key>` links 308-redirect to the matching section URL, including the retired per-org "Your notifications" tab, which now redirects to the viewer's own personal Settings › Notifications — its Organization card lists every membership the preference covers, since notification preferences are per person, not per org. Inside the org-workspace tree, the avatar menu shows only "Workspace settings"; there is no separate personal Settings entry there.

**Find.** A "Find" box now sits under the context switcher at the top of every sidebar (a search icon on the collapsed rail, a row in the mobile Menu sheet), and Ctrl/⌘ K opens the same dialog from anywhere; Ctrl/⌘ \\ still toggles the rail. `lib/dashboard/nav/find-index.ts` builds a pure, jest-pinned index from the same nav-builder output every sidebar renders plus each tree's settings registry and the avatar menu's settings links, so Find can never offer a destination the sidebar or the settings gate would refuse. It is a pages-only quick-jump — no record search — matched on label plus a small synonym table (for example "invoice" finds Billing) and grouped the same way the sidebar and the settings menu already group. `components/dashboard/FindDialog.tsx` renders it as a `ResponsiveModal` with a labelled combobox/listbox: arrow keys move, Home/End jump, Enter opens, Esc closes.

The same round also shipped three unrelated fixes queued from QA. `6f8568df0` fixed a double-toast: raising `TOAST_LIMIT` from 1 to 3 earlier in this PR meant a progress toast (`"Signing in..."`) no longer got evicted by its own outcome toast, so both showed stacked on screen; `pendingToast()` now returns a settle function that updates the progress toast in place, and sign-in, sign-up, forgot-password and reset-password all use it. `f9095b52b` ended React hydration error #418 on every admin Appointments load: the header bell (`NotificationInbox`) rendered on the server as nothing (no session there) but Better Auth could resolve a session before the shell's Suspense boundary finished hydrating on a slow-streaming page, so the client briefly rendered a bell the server never had; it now gates on the same `useHydrated` hook already used by `ContextSwitcher`, lifted out into `hooks/use-hydrated.ts` so both share one implementation. `c53a66b96` fixed a case where a COMPLETED org booking hid its own documents, because the org member detail page only rendered them for a confirmed-status booking; both the consultee and the org member detail pages now render a single shared `ConsulteeDocuments` block that shows read-only documents once a booking is completed-like, the upload control while it is confirmed, and nothing for a kind that carries no documents at all.

## Round 5: support workspace

Four more commits (`dec787d1e`, `2be8c69fd`, `54cbabb31`, `f4b5d9117`) replaced the back office's separate Tickets and Conversations pages with one Support inbox, gave each case its own three-pane workspace, and gave every user-side support conversation a full page of its own instead of a drawer. The table below names the pieces and what each one is.

| Layer | What it is | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Back-office Support inbox | One list of cases, replacing the Tickets and Conversations pages; Needs reply, Mine, Unassigned, Self-serve only and All views, plus a Session/Platform filter, all as URL state | `/dashboard/<tree>/support`, `app/api/staff/support-inbox/route.ts` |
| Inbox stats | Open cases, SLA breaches, and the 7-day average first-response time, derived from the stored clocks | `app/api/staff/support-inbox/stats/route.ts` |
| A case | Either a `SupportTicket` (platform, or an escalated conversation folded in) or a not-yet-escalated `AppointmentSupportThread` | `lib/support/case-key.ts` (`t_<ticketId>` / `s_<threadId>`) |
| Case workspace | Two panes: the case list and the conversation (Reply / Private note, with a locally auto-saved note draft). A non-modal Details panel (person, booking, payment, org, recent cases) opens on demand; quick actions sit in the case header; saved replies and Help Center articles live in the composer's Insert menu (round 5b) | `/support/[caseKey]`, `app/api/staff/support-inbox/[caseKey]/route.ts`, `lib/support/case-workspace.ts`, `components/dashboard/backoffice/support/` |
| Per-request page (user side) | A full page per support request in the consultee, consultant, organization and org-workspace trees, keyed by booking (`b_<appointmentId>`) until the thread exists | `…/support/requests/[caseKey]/page.tsx`, `app/api/user/support-tickets/[ticketId]/route.ts` |

All three staff routes are gated on `tickets.manage`; the email in the context pane is shown only to a viewer who also holds `users.read`, and the payment card only to one who holds `payments.read`. `GET /api/user/support-tickets/[ticketId]` is owner-scoped in its query and never reads private notes, since the requester must never see staff-only notes. The old `tickets` and `threads` URLs, including `tickets/<id>`, `tickets?id=` and `threads/<id>`, 308-redirect to the matching case through `lib/backoffice/legacy-routes.ts`, and staff now land on the inbox (`/dashboard/staff/support`) rather than a Tickets queue. `SupportThreadSheet.tsx`, `SupportThreadsPage.tsx` and `types/tickets.ts` are deleted; `PlatformSupportSheet` stays for its chat-unavailable caller.

The domain model is worth stating plainly, since the two names are easy to conflate. A *conversation* is an `AppointmentSupportThread`: session-level, self-serve first and human second (its `activeChannel` field carries an unused `AI` value alongside `SELF_SERVE` and `HUMAN`). A *ticket* is a `SupportTicket`: platform-level on its own, or linked from a conversation's `supportTicketId` once a human handoff escalates it. The Help Center remains the public article source the assist pane and the user-side page both read, and product feedback stays a separate subsystem documented under `docs/feedback/`.

See [ADR: one Support inbox and a case page per request](../decisions/2026-09-27-support-inbox-and-case-pages.md) for the reasoning.

## What this PR deliberately did not do

The concurrency, deadlock, inconsistent-state, irreversible-transition and illegal-state-transition audit that #1527 originally scoped alongside the IA work was explicitly deferred to issue #1846, to keep this PR's size (626 files at open, 29 commits after) from growing further. Suspended-member access and org allocate-on-behalf were partially built (a SUSPENDED member keeps read-only access and can still join a session they already booked, but cannot make new org-funded bookings or cancel/reschedule one) and the rest is tracked in #1845 and #1843 respectively. Visual-regression screenshots were not captured; the four QA waves verified behaviour against the live preview instead.
