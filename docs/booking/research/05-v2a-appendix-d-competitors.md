# D — Competitor patterns for multi-week, recurring and package scheduling (subscriptions and classes)

> **Status:** Research snapshot of dev @ 39f98669f on 2026-11-03; findings fixed in the booking v2 correctness PR are marked in the [decision record](../../decisions/2026-11-03-booking-v2-direction.md). Appendix D of the [V2A review](./01-v2a-booking-product-and-system-review.md).

Repo: `familiarise_web` @ `dev` 39f98669f. Read-only. Repo claims carry `path:line` and are marked **VERIFIED** (I read the code) or **SUSPECTED**.
Competitor claims come from `search_web` runs on 2026-11-03, which drew on the vendors' own help centres. The tool returns Google grounding redirect links, so each claim below names the vendor help centre it came from instead. Treat these claims as **medium confidence**: help-centre wording changes often, and Preply's rollover rules came back inconsistent (noted where it matters).

## 0. TL;DR

- Familiarise's subscription is **host-placed and runs one cycle at a time**. Only the consultant (or an org operator, or staff) places sessions. A cycle is one rolling week, and the next week's batch opens only after **all of this week's sessions have been delivered**. No other product I surveyed works like this. Every tutoring marketplace (Preply, italki, Cambly, Superprof) and every package tool (Calendly, Acuity, Topmate, Kajabi) lets the **client** self-schedule inside the host's availability. Preply and Cambly also let the client keep a **standing weekly time** that is reserved weeks ahead.
- The current design is strong on **money safety**: a 48-hour expiry with a full refund if nothing is placed, a pro-rata refund at plan end for undelivered sessions, sessions returned to the allowance on a void, and a plan end that moves when you return a session. In some ways it is more generous than Preply, where unscheduled lessons expire, or Cambly, where minutes don't roll over.
- It is weak on **predictability and effort**. Nobody knows next week's time until this week ends. The consultant must re-place every week. The "cycle complete" message tells the consultee the consultant will act, but the consultant gets no message (**SUSPECTED**). There is no pause, no catch-up, no class waitlist, and no standing time.
- Recommendation: **Option B, a standing weekly time ("Your regular time")**. It is placed rolling 2–4 weeks ahead, each occurrence can be moved on its own, a week that clashes becomes a single "needs a time" item, and unplaced sessions fall back to a self-serve credit pool. Schema changes are additive (§4).

## 2. Competitor research

Legend. **Placer**: who puts future sessions on the calendar. **Horizon**: all at once, rolling, or ad hoc. **Conflict**: what happens when a slot in the series is busy. **Cancel/refund**: what happens to sessions already placed. **Quota**: allowance and rollover rules. **Nudge**: reminders to book.

### 2.1 Preply (1:1 tutoring marketplace with a subscription)

Source: Preply help centre (https://help.preply.com), via search.

- **Plan maths**: a 28-day billing cycle per tutor. Minimum 1 lesson/week = **4 lessons per 28 days**. Plans scale to 2, 3 or more per week. Lessons are 25 or 50 min. A separate subscription is needed for each tutor.
- **Placer**: student or tutor. Either can set up **weekly recurring lessons** (web only, limited on mobile). Individual lessons are booked by the student from the tutor's calendar.
- **Horizon**: rolling. Weekly lessons inside the current 28-day cycle are **"Scheduled"** and deducted from the balance. Weekly slots after the next renewal show as **"Reserved" 🔒**: the time is held but no lesson is deducted. They turn into scheduled lessons automatically at renewal.
- **Moving one occurrence**: rescheduling or cancelling one lesson >12h ahead turns it into a standalone lesson, and the weekly series stays. Changing the weekly time for all future lessons is a separate "change weekly schedule" action. "Cancel weekly lessons" removes reserved slots, but already-scheduled lessons remain as standalones.
- **Cancel/reschedule rule**: student, **>12h** = free (credit back, tutor unpaid); **<12h** = lesson counts as taken and tutor paid. Tutors can do a late reschedule in emergencies.
- **Tutor cancels**: the lesson goes back to the student's balance. The tutor's reliability metrics use a rolling 90 days (cancellation rate must stay <20%, short-notice reschedule rate <40%). Breaching hides the tutor from new-student search, and the profile reappears automatically when metrics recover.
- **Quota/rollover**: officially, unscheduled subscription lessons **expire at renewal**, but a lesson that is _scheduled_ before renewal is kept. "Extra lessons" (top-ups) roll over for up to 2 cycles. _Sources conflicted: some help text suggested a limited rollover on auto-renew. Treat it as "scheduled = safe, unscheduled = at risk"._
- **Pause**: once per cycle, up to **20 days**. The renewal date moves and the balance is kept.
- **Plan change**: upgrades and downgrades take effect at next renewal. Instant extra lessons can be bought now.
- **Transfer**: the balance can be moved to a new tutor ("Switch to a new tutor"). Lessons inside 12h can't be moved.
- **Subscription cancel**: billing stops. Booked lessons in the paid cycle are still delivered. Unscheduled ones expire at cycle end. Reserved weekly slots are released.

### 2.2 italki (1:1 marketplace, packages)

Source: italki support centre (https://support.italki.com), via search.

- **Packages**: e.g. 5, 10 or 20 lessons at a discount. Valid **6 months**. One extension of **1 month**, which both parties must agree to.
- **Placer**: student. Each lesson is booked individually from the package. The teacher must **accept within 48h** (or before the start, whichever is first). Otherwise the request expires and the student isn't charged.
- **Horizon**: ad hoc, week by week or many ahead.
- **Cancel/reschedule**: free **>24h**, and the lesson goes back to the package. A reschedule needs the other party to agree. **<24h** locked; the teacher is entitled to payment.
- **Expiry**: unused lessons are refunded to the wallet, but **used lessons are re-priced at the single-lesson rate**. That is a clawback of the package discount.
- **Termination**: "Request to terminate package" settles the unused lessons.
- **Teacher reliability**: ignored or expired requests and teacher cancellations count against the teacher. Penalties include reduced visibility.

### 2.3 Cambly (subscription of minutes)

Source: Cambly help centre (https://www.cambly.com/help), via search.

- **Quota**: weekly minutes, typically 30-min lessons × 1/2/3/5 per week. Minutes refresh **every Monday 03:00 local time**. Use is capped at **60 min/day**. **No rollover.**
- **Placer**: student. Lessons can be reserved, and a **recurring weekly schedule** can last as long as the subscription.
- **Cancel**: >12h credits the minutes back. Late cancels forfeit them.
- **Pause**: 1–4 weeks, which pushes the end/billing date. Extra minutes can be bought mid-week.

### 2.4 Superprof (marketplace, lesson packs)

Source: Superprof help centre (https://www.superprof.com/faq), via search.

- **Packs**: 5 or 10 hours, **proposed by the tutor in chat**, accepted by the student.
- **Placer**: either side, with "propose a lesson" per session.
- **Payout incentive**: the tutor gets 50% after the first lesson and the remaining 50% once **all remaining hours are scheduled** on the platform. Payment is used to push the host to place every session.
- **Cancel**: >24h gives a full refund for that session. <24h follows the tutor-chosen late policy (0/50/100%).

### 2.5 Wyzant (marketplace)

Source: Wyzant support (https://support.wyzant.com), via search.

- Lessons are managed one by one in the calendar. A repeating routine exists but each lesson is an instance.
- **Tutors set their own cancellation policy**, capped at **36h notice** and at most the full session cost. It must be shown in "Rate details" and communicated in Wyzant Messages. A fee can be submitted from 30 min before the end until 14 days after.

### 2.6 Calendly (scheduling tool)

Source: Calendly help centre (https://help.calendly.com), via search.

- **Recurring**: **not supported for invitees**. An invitee books one slot. Hosts work around it with calendar-side recurrence or "Schedule invitee again".
- **Meeting packages** (Stripe, "Sell" tab): the host bundles N sessions of one 1:1 event type. The client pays once and then **self-books each session** from a package link. Cancelling **returns the credit**. Rescheduling keeps it. Packages can **expire** (e.g. 3 or 6 months), and unused credits then lapse.
- **Cancel rules**: there is no hard cutoff on invitee cancel or reschedule links. Minimum notice applies to new bookings, not to reschedules. Hosts write the policy as text or remove the links.

### 2.7 Cal.com (scheduling, open source)

Source: Cal.com docs (https://cal.com/docs), via search.

- **Recurring events**: the booker picks **one start slot** and a single confirmation books the whole series. The host sets frequency and **max occurrences (≤32)**. The booker can choose a count within a min/max, or the count is fixed.
- **Incompatibilities**: recurring is **not compatible with Seats, Payments or per-booker limits**.
- **Requires confirmation**: the booking is pending until the host approves. A recurring request is approved as a series.
- **Cancel**: cancelling from the booker side cancels **all remaining occurrences**. Single-occurrence reschedules are handled per booking and are a known rough edge.

### 2.8 Acuity Scheduling / Squarespace Scheduling

Source: Acuity help centre (https://help.acuityscheduling.com), via search.

- **Recurring**: the client can "Make recurring" (e.g. weekly ×4). Acuity checks **every** slot, and if **any** one clashes the series can't be booked as a set. The admin can override availability when booking on the client's behalf.
- **Packages**: a code with N appointments. **Not** auto-scheduled; the client books one by one. Optional **expiry**, after which unused credits are void.
- **Cancel**: credit is returned unless the host marks a **no-show**. A reschedule keeps the credit. A **minimum notice for self-cancel/reschedule** blocks late changes and tells the client to contact the business.

### 2.9 SimplyBook.me

Source: SimplyBook.me help centre (https://help.simplybook.me), via search.

- **Recurring service ("pack")**: weekly/daily frequency × **repeat count**, priced as a **total for the batch**.
- **Three conflict modes**, which are the best-articulated I found:
  1. **Not allow**: any busy date rejects the whole series.
  2. **Book only available**: busy dates are skipped and the client gets fewer sessions.
  3. **Add appointments**: busy dates are skipped and **extra sessions are added at the end** to reach the count.
- Clients **can't reschedule a whole batch** themselves; that is admin-only.

### 2.10 Mindbody (studios: classes, courses, appointments)

Source: Mindbody support (https://support.mindbodyonline.com), via search.

- **Recurring class reservations**: the client books instances. Full dates need a waitlist per date.
- **Waitlist**: outside the late-cancel window, the first in line is **auto-enrolled**. Inside it, auto-enrol pauses by default. Optional "late-cancel window automation" either continues auto-add or starts **"first to claim"** by SMS. A **waitlist lock** cutoff (e.g. 30 min before) stops all automation.
- **Courses (multi-day series)**: the waitlist is **not** auto-enrolled. Staff apply payment and enrol by hand. Single-day courses do auto-enrol.
- **Late cancel**: a fee or a lost credit, per studio policy.

### 2.11 Glofox (studios)

Source: Glofox help centre (https://support.glofox.com), via search.

- **Recurring bookings**: the **admin** "Book multiple" from the dashboard. Members don't self-create recurring series in the app.
- **Restricted memberships**: credits per term (week or month). Members can book into the **next term**, and next-term credits appear early to allow this. Unlimited memberships can book the current and next term.
- **Waitlist**: either "first to claim" (everyone notified) or **auto-promote** (needs "pay later"). The auto-enrol cutoff is configurable from 30 min to 10h before.
- **Out of credits**: book at a member rate instead of the drop-in price.

### 2.12 Teachable and Kajabi coaching

Source: Teachable support (https://support.teachable.com) and Kajabi help (https://help.kajabi.com), via search.

- **Teachable**: coaching runs on **milestones**. The first booking comes from a template. For multi-session packages the **coach adds a "book next session" milestone per client**, a manual nudge. There is no hard credit counter on a generic Calendly link. Hosts pair it with Calendly packages for enforcement.
- **Kajabi**: a coaching product defines the **session count**. The native Kajabi Scheduler enforces counts and reminders. With Calendly (1:1 events only, webhooks required), counts are **not** enforced unless Calendly packages are aligned.

### 2.13 Topmate (creator marketplace, India)

Source: Topmate help (https://topmate.io and its Freshdesk help centre), via search.

- **Packages** bundle calls and other services, with a **validity period** (e.g. 30 or 90 days) and optional instalments.
- **Placer**: the buyer. They book the first call right after purchase, then book later calls from the dashboard against package inventory, inside validity. Bulk booking is allowed if the expert permits it.

### 2.14 Practo (healthcare, India)

Source: Practo help (https://www.practo.com), via search.

- In-clinic: patient cancels **>2h** ahead for a full refund. **<2h** costs a small fee (~₹50). Doctor or clinic cancel gives a full refund. Patients reschedule from the app inside the doctor's slots.
- Online consult: the doctor may cancel if a physical exam is needed (full refund). **7-day free follow-up** (≤30 messages) with the same doctor. This is a useful "included follow-up" pattern, not a recurrence.

### 3.1 Design options for "who places future subscription sessions"

**Option A: status quo plus nudges.** The host places one rolling week at a time. Add D-2 nudges, D-5 copy and D-10 sweeps.

- Pros: no schema change, keeps the invariants (C1).
- Cons: predictability problem D-1 stays, consultant toil every week, no parity with Preply/Cambly.

**Option B: standing weekly time (Preply "weekly lessons"). Recommended.**

- At checkout (or on first allocation) the consultee picks, or the consultant proposes, `spw` weekly slots from published availability, e.g. "Tue 19:00, Thu 19:00 IST". The validator checks the next 4 occurrences of each slot. Clashes are shown per date, not as an all-or-nothing block (avoiding the Acuity trap).
- A sweep places occurrences on a **rolling horizon of 2–4 weeks**, using the existing allocator in `manual` mode as a system actor under the same lock order. Each placed row is a normal occurrence. Rows past the horizon show only in the UI as **"Reserved"** (ghost cells), like Preply 🔒, and aren't in the DB until placed. That keeps the exclusion constraint honest.
- **Conflict per week** follows SimplyBook's modes, defaulting to **"add at end if slack, else needs a time"**. That week becomes one actionable item: consultee sees **"Week of 10 Mar needs a time: your regular Tue slot clashes with {consultant}'s holiday. Pick another time"**; consultant sees **"Pick a replacement for Tue 11 Mar"**.
- Moving one occurrence detaches it ("standalone"), and the standing time stays. "Change regular time" applies from next week.
- Cancel or refund of the plan releases all future placed and reserved rows in one transaction, using the existing pro-rata front door (C16).
- Pros: matches the mental model of the target segment (tuition, coaching), removes D-1, D-11 and most of D-2. Consultant toil drops to exceptions.
- Cons: a new table and sweep. The quota invariant must count rolling-horizon rows against the weekly cap. Holiday handling becomes visible.

**Option C: credit pool with self-booking (Calendly/Acuity packages).** The consultee books any slot before plan end, with a weekly cap of `spw` (or the catch-up cap, D-4).

- Pros: the most flexible, the simplest mental model ("12 sessions, use by 31 May"), and good for irregular schedules.
- Cons: no rhythm, front-loading or back-loading, the consultant's calendar fragments, and it needs D-3.

**Recommendation:** ship **B**, with **C as the fallback** for any week the standing time can't fill, plus D-2 and D-10 nudges. Classes stay all-at-once (K1, like Cal.com fixed occurrences) and gain a waitlist (D-9).

### 3.2 Policy benchmarks worth copying

1. **Standing time + reserved ghost slots** (Preply). Keep money and calendar honest by holding real rows only inside a short horizon.
2. **Per-occurrence detach** (Preply). Moving one session never breaks the series, and "change regular time" is a separate, explicit action.
3. **Conflict mode per series** (SimplyBook): not allow / skip / add at end. Default to add-at-end within plan slack.
4. **Rate-based host reliability** over hard caps (Preply 90-day rates, italki TMS). Familiarise already has strikes (C15), so extend them to reschedule rate instead of a 2-move hard cap (D-8).
5. **Pause that moves the end date** (Preply ≤20d, Cambly 1–4 weeks). Familiarise already moves `planEndsAt` on returns, so reuse that.
6. **Host incentive to place everything** (Superprof releases the final 50% when all hours are scheduled). Familiarise already matures tranches on delivery (`lib/booking/entitlement.ts:185-225`). Surface it to the consultant: **"Schedule all remaining sessions to unlock your full earnings forecast."**
7. **Host must respond in 48h or auto-refund** (italki). Already present (C13, D-14). Extend it to self-scheduled requests (D-3).
8. **Waitlist auto-promote with a cutoff and claim window** (Mindbody, Glofox). Use for classes (D-9).
9. **Book into next term early** (Glofox restricted memberships). Equivalent to letting the consultee place next week before this week is delivered (D-1 short-term fix).
10. **Avoid**: italki's re-pricing of used lessons at the single-lesson rate on expiry (it feels punitive), and Calendly's lack of a hard cancel cutoff (Familiarise's 24h rule is better). Avoid Cal.com's "cancel = cancel all remaining" without showing what is lost. Familiarise's cancel quote binding (`money-boundary.md:411-422`) is already better; keep showing the per-session breakdown.
